# FEPC residual fiscal affairs after 1946: source challenge (2026-11-13, dispatch EO-SUP-04)

**What this is:** a different-family, unblinded source challenge by Claude, using the session subscription with no paid
API or subagents. **It is not** whole-lifecycle certification, a canonical claim, or an adoption request. Source pins
are in `SOURCES.json` and page crops in `crops/` (hashes in `crops/SHA256SUMS`). Every figure below was read from the
page image, not the text layer, and checked by cross-footing.

## 1. What each source says

**A. Treasury annual report, FY1946, printed p. 118 (PDF 132)** [AR1946]
- **The FEPC row:** the page continues the "Liquidation of war agencies" passage begun on p. 117: "During the year
  similar arrangements were effected by the President for other war agencies as follows". Its table lists "Committee on
  Fair Employment Practice | President's letter of May 18, 1946 | May 18, 1946", under the column "Liquidation
  commenced".
- **The list of liquidation work is on p. 117 (PDF 131), not p. 118, and is stated for a different agency:** the
  Division of Central Administrative Services of the Office for Emergency Management. The Bureau of Accounts took over
  that division's residual affairs on Dec. 1, 1944, under Executive Order 9471. The work listed:
  - certifying outstanding obligations;
  - recommendations on claims under expired appropriations;
  - answering General Accounting Office exceptions;
  - collections;
  - closing accounts;
  - records for the National Archives;
  - surplus property;
  - personnel;
  - general administrative matters.
- **For FEPC the report says only "similar arrangements".**

**B. Federal Register, Sept. 11, 1946, 31 CFR 270.1(h)(1) (PDF 91; Treasury organization statement, Fiscal Service,
Bureau of Accounts)** [FR1946-09-11]
- "(h) *The Liquidation Division.* (1) This Division has administrative and technical responsibility for the
  liquidation of the residual fiscal affairs of certain discontinued war agencies of the Government. This liquidation of
  the following agencies has been assumed pursuant to Presidential authorization: … and the Committee on Fair Employment
  Practice (letter of the President dated May 18, 1946)."
- "The winding up of the administrative affairs of the foregoing activities, involves generally the termination of
  contracts for rental or services; the liquidation of obligations incurred including payment of vouchers and
  certification of claims; the collection of moneys owed to the Government; the clearance of post-audit exceptions taken
  by the General Accounting Office; the disposition of equipment, supplies, and other property; the separation of
  remaining war agency personnel; and the submission of final reports on liquidation."

**Corrections and additions:**
- **a. Where the function list comes from:**
  - For FEPC specifically, the list rests on FR 270.1(h)(1). It is stated "generally" for every listed agency, not for
    FEPC in particular.
  - The FR's list does not include "records". Records for the National Archives appear only in the AR p. 117 list,
    which describes the OEM division and reaches FEPC only through "similar arrangements".
  - The FR adds termination of rental and service contracts, and final reports on liquidation.
- **b. AR p. 118 gives only the agency, the authority and the date.** Citing p. 118 for the function list is a page
  error.
- **c. Omitted by the producer: the 1945 account was closed in FY1947.** After $959.97 in checks, $5,536.56 was carried
  to the surplus fund under the 1870/1874 Acts, and both balances went to zero. Before that, $3,894.91 had been repaid to
  the appropriation from disbursing-officer funds.
- **d. 1946 account, FY1947 movements:**
  - New accountable warrants of $20,850.00 to disbursing officers. These are advances: transfers of funds, not
    expenditures.
  - $6,084.79 repaid to the appropriation; net warrants $14,776.71.
  - $15,308.25 in checks.
  - The Treasury-books balance fell from $15,719.92 to $943.21.
- **e. One printed defect, which does not change the producer's figures:**
  - **(i) The 1945-account net warrant is printed "3,876.77*".** It should be 3,878.77, because three independent
    totals require it:
    - 16.14 − 3,894.91 = −3,878.77;
    - Treasury 1,657.79 + 3,878.77 = 5,536.56, the amount carried to surplus;
    - disbursing 4,838.74 − 3,878.77 − 959.97 = 0.
    The printed column totals (10,897.94) agree with 3,878.77. **So this is a $2.00 printing error in that one cell;**
    the page was checked at 400 dpi.
  - **Withdrawn (EO-SUP-04 recheck):** this review earlier said the FY1947 opening-balance header reads "June 30, 1945".
    It does not. Enlarged 4x, the committed crop `crops/cs1947_pdf192_p174.png` (same GovInfo bytes, SHA-256
    62bfbd0d…) reads **"June 30, 1946"**, which agrees with the figures and with the supervisor's separate rendering.
    The PDF text layer reads "1948", an OCR error. The "1945" was my own low-zoom misreading, not a different scan and
    not OCR.

## 3. Do the FY1947 payments conflict with a June 30, 1946 fund cutoff?

- **What the tables show:** checks issued in FY1947 against fiscal-year-dated appropriation accounts (the 1946 and 1945
  vintages), with no FY1947 appropriation. Payments drawn on the 1946 account after June 30, 1946 are the pattern
  expected when obligations incurred while the account was available are paid later. That is also the work the FR
  assigns to the Liquidation Division ("liquidation of obligations incurred including payment of vouchers").
- **What they do not show:**
  - when the obligations were incurred;
  - who the payees were;
  - what each payment was for;
  - which disbursing officer paid;
  - that any payment was valid;
  - that FEPC itself operated after June 30, 1946.
- **Conclusion:** on these pages the observations are **consistent with liquidation under a separate fiscal path**
  (Treasury's Liquidation Division under the May 18, 1946 letter), and **do not conflict** with a June 30, 1946 cutoff.
  **They do not prove** that every payment was for obligations incurred by that date. The later checks are not evidence
  that operations revived.
- **The 1945 account lapsed to the surplus fund in FY1947.** This fits the 1870/1874 surplus-fund rule printed in the
  column heading. Its exact availability period is a legal predicate (section 4).

## 4. Legal predicates left unresolved

1. **The May 18, 1946 letter** is unlocated: its text, statutory basis and exact scope. Did it transfer only fiscal
   liquidation, and did it end any FEPC function?
2. **What "liquidation commenced May 18, 1946" means for the committee:**
   - whether the committee was discontinued on that date, or kept operating until its funds ended while Treasury handled
     fiscal affairs;
   - the FR calls FEPC a "discontinued war agency" only as of Sept. 1946.
3. **The governing appropriation act's terms** for the FY1946 FEPC appropriation: any liquidation proviso, the end of
   availability, and whether it set a June 30, 1946 termination. Not examined here.
4. **Obligation timing:** whether the FY1947 checks ($15,308.25 and $959.97) paid obligations incurred within each
   account's period of availability. That needs vouchers, General Accounting Office settlements, or the Liquidation
   Division's final report, which the FR says was to be submitted.
5. **Final disposition of the 1946 account:** $943.21 on Treasury books and $3,339.31 with disbursing officers at June 30,
   1947. Its lapse to the surplus fund is expected under the 1870/1874 Acts but unobserved; the FY1948 Combined
   Statement is needed.
6. **Disbursing officers and advances:** who held the accountable warrants ($239,350 in FY1946; $20,850 in FY1947), and
   whether the repayments were refunds of unused advances or other collections.
