# Owner ruling: work out the legal distinctions from the evidence; do not ask for convenient answers (2026-10-28)

Given by the owner, Danny, on 2026-10-28, in answer to a proposed rule for one-person civil-service appointment
waivers. His words, lightly condensed:

> If the evidence establishes that a one-person appointment waiver served its purpose, record that conclusion,
> distinguish the surviving appointment from the exhausted permission, and give the best supported date or
> interval. An unavailable exact appointment date should not automatically hold the entire history hostage.
> Explaining what happened and what remains uncertain is useful research.
>
> But "we know the appointment occurred, somewhere within this interval" differs from "we haven't established
> whether it occurred." Likewise, a general civil-service rewrite supplies an endpoint only if there's a reason it
> extinguished that permission. Disclosure makes uncertainty visible; it doesn't supply missing evidence.
>
> The AI should work out those distinctions, apply them to the supported cases, and present the remaining
> exceptions, not ask [the owner] to choose a convenient historical answer to unlock 1,500 rows.

Standing application, for every agent and every order type:

1. **Evidence decides the claim.** Where evidence establishes that a permission, waiver or other one-time command
   was used, record that it was exhausted by performance, separate the exhausted command from any surviving
   consequence (an appointment, a transfer, a right), and give the best supported date or interval.
2. **Known-within-an-interval is not unknown-whether.** A bounded interval may be claimed only when the evidence
   shows the event happened within it. If whether it happened is not established, say so; that order stays open
   for that question.
3. **An endpoint needs a reason.** A later general rule, rewrite or revocation ends a command only where something
   shows it extinguished that command. Its date is not a default end.
4. **Disclosure is not evidence.** Disclosing a gap keeps it visible; it never fills it.
5. **Do not route method questions to the owner.** The goal is already set: the legally defensible lifecycle of
   each order. Agents work out such distinctions themselves, apply them to the supported cases, and report the
   remaining exceptions with their reasons.
