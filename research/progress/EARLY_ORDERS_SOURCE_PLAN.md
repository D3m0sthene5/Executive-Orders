# Early presidential directives: source recovery started

Updated 2026-10-17 after a Stage 18 primary-scan pilot. The project aims to recover 1789–1906 directives that satisfy its documented inclusion rule, including unnumbered instruments. The five 1789–1790 entries already transcribed from the scanned Lord index remain **catalogue candidates**, outside the 14,972 denominator. All five now have independently located primary historical representations; the Knox-signed candidate remains held on presidential attribution. A separately identified 1862 Lincoln candidate also remains uncounted. See the [source manifest](../stage18/SOURCE_MANIFEST.json) and [candidate crosswalk](../stage18/data/EARLY_SOURCE_CANDIDATES.csv).

The [Library of Congress Washington collection](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/3115) includes online letterbooks and a transcription dataset. The [Library of Congress guide to presidential papers](https://guides.loc.gov/finding-government-documents/presidential-papers) identifies a free Richardson compilation and other discovery routes. NARA's [Record Group 11 guide](https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/011.html) covers executive-order originals from 1862 onward; the [Office of the Federal Register tables](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/executive-orders/disposition) are useful for 1937 onward, not a complete pre-1937 census. These collections are complementary and require identity and document-level crosswalks.

**Nineteenth-century pilot:** Both images of the [November 13, 1862 Lincoln directive](https://www.loc.gov/item/mal4245100/) have been preserved as a **secretarial copy**, with instructions for confiscation proceedings and military aid; it is not a verified autograph. It appears absent from the Stage 17 included list and remains a candidate. For the already included October 20, 1862 **EO 1**, the [Supreme Court's official opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-86/pdf/USREPORTS-86-519.pdf) reproduces the order and locates the provisional court's actual dissolution on July 28, 1866, through the [congressional transfer statute, 14 Stat. 344](https://www.loc.gov/resource/llsalvol.llsal_014/?sp=376&st=image). This is a source-backed causal history of the court's operation, with earlier partial jurisdictional narrowing still unresolved; see the [Stage 18 record](../stage18/data/EO1_CAUSAL_HISTORY.json). The [NARA finding aid](https://www.archives.gov/fort-worth/finding-aids/rg021-louisiana) is a lead for the court records and 1863 narrowing, not proof of the exact earlier legal mechanism.

## Repeatable recovery pipeline

1. Use the 1,023 Lord OCR date markers only as search leads. Visually verify each index page and transcribe the cited source and exact descriptive text.
2. Search LOC presidential letterbooks/manuscripts, Richardson, Annals, American State Papers, NARA catalog/Record Group 11, presidential libraries, and relevant agency records. Save the stable catalog URL, archival identifier, scan or PDF bytes when permitted, retrieval date, SHA-256, and exact page/image.
3. Match signer, capacity, date, recipients, text, and legal form. Deduplicate copies, amendments, correspondence, proclamations, and presidentially approved departmental acts under explicit rules; retain rejected candidates with reasons.
4. Segment original provisions and link dated amendments, statutes, judicial holdings, and end conditions. Record a bounded unknown where the earliest causal event cannot be proved.
5. Sample the accepted and rejected matches for independent human review. Report missed-source and false-match rates by era and collection before scaling an OCR/AI pipeline.

AI can OCR scans, extract candidate dates and names, search collections, align variants, suggest event edges, and draft evidence packets. Human archival and legal review remains necessary for ambiguous presidential attribution, whole-scope legal effects, and certification. AI speed does not repair missing originals or establish a complete national denominator.

## What may require material beyond the public internet

There is **no basis for claiming that a particular remaining executive order is absolutely impossible to find online** without item-level archival investigation. There is also no basis for assuming all required evidence is online. NARA says most of its holdings are available only at archival facilities in its [digitization strategy](https://www.archives.gov/digitization/strategy.html), and its [2026 holdings figures](https://www.archives.gov/about/info/national-archives-by-the-numbers) show digitized pages are only a fraction of paper holdings. For a specific case, original signed pages, correspondence, provenance, or evidence of performance may require an archive visit, scan request, agency records request, or may no longer survive. Independent human legal certification is a new research action, not a document that can be downloaded.
