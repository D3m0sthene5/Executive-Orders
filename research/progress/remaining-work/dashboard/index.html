Where the open work is

Each rectangle's area is the number of open steps: one step for one order. Color is the kind of work. Hover or tab to a rectangle for its count.

How to read this

  • The goal. A complete account of every order APP counts: is it still in effect, and why or why not, with sources, finished by machine and ready for a person to check. An order is ready when every step below is done or recorded as searched to the end.
  • The steps come from the project's readiness tracker: first yes/no, an official copy of the text, a list of every operative clause, proof of how it ended or what keeps it in force, parts that outlive it, a search of later law for contrary sources, and (for some orders) cited sources and evidence images.
  • Orders not yet in the project. For each president, where APP counts more orders than the project holds, each missing order adds one step to find and admit it, plus the six core steps. Where the project holds more than APP (Coolidge, and a few undated orders), nothing is subtracted.
  • Steps are not equal effort. The AI effort tab weighs each step by how long it would take an AI agent with the project's current tools.
  • Human review comes after. No order has been independently certified by a person yet, and nothing here changes that count.

Where the AI effort goes

The same open steps as the Steps left tab, every one of them counted, but each rectangle's area is the estimated AI agent-hours for its steps. A step the tools have mostly done is small; a step the tools cannot help with is large. Hover or tab to a rectangle for its hours and step count.

How the effort was estimated

  • Unit. An agent-hour is one AI agent working for an hour with the project's tools, including the independent AI reviewer that checks it. Agents running in parallel finish sooner on the calendar but use the same agent-hours. Human review is not included.
  • Each step gets a tier from what the GitHub jobs already hold for that order: machine clause drafts (clause-drafts), histories of later changes (status-history, later-events, family-rules), official originals found (fr-originals, numbered-early-orders), claim drafts that leave only judgment (claim-drafts), and the source fetcher (source-ladder). The table below lists every tier with its minutes per step.
  • The anchor. In Codex's batch pilot, two independent runs each took six prepared modern orders through a producer, an independent reviewer and corrections, with all times and tokens recorded. Each run upheld one of its six, which puts a finished dossier on a well-tooled modern order at about half an agent-hour. The calibrated tiers split that time. The assumed tiers scale it up by how much less the tools do.
  • Claim drafts. For orders that already have a claim draft, the draft's own size estimate makes the four legal steps smaller or larger.
  • Limits. Most weights are judgments, not measurements; the project has not yet run its planned instrumented pilot. Read the hours as within a factor of two. The comparison between kinds of work and between presidents is sturdier than the total.

How to read this

  • Automated and done is not shown here; it is in the done counts on the other tabs (texts fetched, machine searches recorded, clauses drafted).
  • Automated, just run it (machine queued): a wired job already covers it. The source ladder and the Opus re-fetch list only need runner time.
  • Ruled or drafted, not wired yet: the owner ruled on Oct 21 that a labeled transcription may satisfy the text step for now, so the transcription-only orders moved here from "owner ruling", and the 2,750 orders with the owner's ProQuest replica scans joined them on Oct 22. The tracker on main applies the ruling only through a filed claim that confirms the order's working text case by case, which is how the transcription-dependent ready orders got there; every other transcription-only or scan-only order keeps its text step open and sits in this lane, with a page check still owed for the scans. Lambertini's drafted counter-evidence records sit here too.
  • Agent claim: a machine draft or lead exists and a proven agent line (Lambertini, Rastell, X1 rules, Bowditch Y1, Pullman's packets now that report deadlines are ruled) can finish it with independent AI review. This is where every ready order has come from. First Pullman run, Oct 22: 16 orders attempted, 11 claims upheld, 3 rejected, 2 of 2 planted defects caught; a thin-packet variant measured no token saving.
  • Owner ruling: no order-level step in this count waits on an owner ruling; the eight rulings of Oct 21 cleared the lane (see research/readiness/owner_rulings/). Project-level questions still waiting for the owner, including whether sampled review may adopt statuses for whole classes of orders, are listed in HANDOFF/KEAN.md. The Justinian generator's 30 class-level questions remain on the unmerged branch claude/justinian-1.
  • Owner doing it by hand: nearly empty since Oct 22. The owner's 2,750 ProQuest replica scans were ingested (Pinakes) and now sit in "ruled or drafted, not wired" with a page check owed; what remains by hand is a handful of ProQuest links still awaiting acquisition. Lewis's 2,663 phrase searches stay routed to the owner, but those orders already hold a transcription, so they appear in the transcription lane.
  • Earlier step first is the largest lane: an order with no first yes/no cannot have its clauses, ending or savings worked. No route is now small: Clark's number-only orders with no lead anywhere.
  • Human review comes after all of this and is not automated: 0 certified.

How this is measured

  • The goal is every order the American Presidency Project counts, plus the few the project holds that it does not, each with all readiness steps done. That is the moment of the first step. Human certification comes after it and is not counted here.
  • Work done counts every done and open step the readiness tracker records, plus the steps owed by orders the project has not found yet. Each step is weighted by the effort model's average agent-minutes for its kind, frozen on Oct 28 so later model changes do not move past days.
  • The clock is work done times Apollo 11's 109 hours 24 minutes. Milestones are Apollo 11's: leaving Earth orbit at T+2h 44m, lunar orbit at T+75h 50m, landing at T+102h 46m, first step at T+109h 24m.
  • The record starts Oct 17, when the readiness tracker first ran. Work done before that day appears as the starting position, not as a launch. The jump on Oct 18 is the first night the tracker counted the Federal Register originals and the machines' later-law searches.
  • Built by research/progress/remaining-work/build_mission_log.py (Capcom) from the tracker's history on main.

How an order travels, and who moves it

Each box is a named job, a past research campaign, or a human decision point, placed by its purpose. Orders loop: most stop at a gate, go back for a missing source or a correction, and come round again. Ready for human review is not certification; a person certifies. Hover or focus a box for a summary; click or press Enter to pin the full record below.

How to read this

  • Placement is by purpose, not by run order. A job can serve several stages; its box sits where most of its work lands, and its record lists what it feeds.
  • Current means code that runs now. Historical covers past research campaigns and stopped lines, whatever their old label said. Proposed was never built.
  • Catalog stats come from the live tools dataset (TOOLS.csv). Each tool's figures keep their own date and qualifiers, and they overlap: never add tools' ready contributions together. The ready count shown is the tracker's own.
  • Namesakes are what the project recorded, with the place it was recorded. Where the project never recorded one, the record says so or labels a reasoned guess as inferred.
  • Built from research/progress/remaining-work/workflow/WORKFLOW_NODES.json by build_workflow_nodes.py, which checks every catalog name against TOOLS.csv.
