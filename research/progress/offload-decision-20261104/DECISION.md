# What to take off models next

**Recommendation: wire and test the maintained mechanical verifier before commissioning another tool.** For assignments that recreate source hash, span and quotation scripts, replace that mechanical step with the maintained route. Give the worker a pinned mechanical receipt plus the same full source access, and route missing bytes, ambiguous identity, stale inputs and substantive questions to the existing exception/review route. PR135 already supplies the primitive; this assignment finds task-level evidence and tests existing machinery rather than rebuilding it.

This is a bounded recommendation, not a demonstrated project-wide token saving. The evidence does not support a large new optimizer yet. The cheapest decisive next test is an authorized, source-ready, unreserved packet using the maintained verifier, compared with the competent existing workflow, with the same independent source examination and final obligations. Measure whether script-writing/verification turns disappear or merely move to coordination and review. Keep the paused wave out of the test.

## Experiments and what they establish

The **real-source mechanical replay** in `population/` compares repeated reference checks with shared exact-source/span work while retaining every original reference disposition. It checks exact source bytes and negative mutations. The occurrence-wise loop is deliberately naive; the replay also compares a competent per-claim baseline. PR135 already automates per-reference verification, but its `_source` helper has no cross-reference cache. Grouping in this replay is a proposed within-invocation computation, not a measured ordinary invocation improvement or a saving across independent reviewers. This tests processing on real held inputs, not provider-token usage, original authentication or legal conclusions. See its final results for attributable work counts, retained exceptions and overhead.

The **existing-codec replay** in `experiment/` uses the unchanged pure encoder/decoder functions extracted from the pinned native source, with a competent compact canonical JSON baseline and exact reconstruction. It includes pooled and cold per-order accounting, dictionary lookup overhead and refusal probes. Native full-module preparation/load/freshness is not executed; source hash/span verification is not performed by the codec. Compact pooled bytes fell from 355,545 to 162,556; summed individual encodings were 292,244 versus 355,559 literal bytes. These are serialized bytes, not estimated or billed tokens, eliminated model calls or research savings. This result supports **not building another codec**.

The Valla handoff inspection is a **frozen historical projection/static path test**, not a live applicability revalidation or proof of a cheaper producer. No new legal research, provider-call export or human certification was generated.

## Benefit, costs and the decision-changing observation

Observed benefits are reproducible mechanical task consolidation, exact representation parity and discovery of a concrete handoff gap. No reduction in complete model calls, fresh/cache/output tokens, cash or marginal readiness is observed. The small warm-source span replay saves fractions of a millisecond against its competent per-claim baseline, not a demonstrated production bottleneck. Independent review found boolean cache-key, empty-span and changed-byte handling defects in the experimental grouping code; final corrected probes retain those failures. An additional live-file mutation counterexample makes grouped checking differ from fresh per-reference reads: its equivalence requires one immutable held snapshot. A production integration would need the native before/after freshness and custody contract; no persistent or cross-review cache is approved. These findings argue against a new production cache for this tiny timing difference. No raw provider export is available for this session; the corrected Hamilton→NEYMAN route remains ready for a legitimately supported future export, not demonstrated real-work capture here.

One-time costs include this investigation, prototype, integration and review; complete tokens and active labor are unknown. Recurring costs include hashing source bytes, compiling/checking receipts, source access, invalidation, fallback lookups and independent examination. A saved hash/span result must bind exact source bytes, offsets/quote and operation/version; a changed source or obligation invalidates reuse. Share computation only within supported custody/blinding boundaries, never across an unexamined control mapping.

Break-even is conditional: avoided model work must exceed receipt-reading, extra lookup/review/rework, coordination and amortized setup/maintenance **in the same declared metric**. Do not add cached/fresh/output categories as dollars or count the same shared check once for every downstream order. A paired ordinary packet measuring removed verification/script-writing turns and displaced reviewer work is the observation most likely to change the leading recommendation. Cash and full project queues would not change this first test, so we stopped trying to reconstruct them.

The candidates combine sequentially: existing admission avoids known infeasible launches; maintained checks handle admitted mechanical tasks; targeted current context may prevent repeated investigation. These savings overlap. Measure one complete run's unique calls/costs and output obligations rather than summing attractive per-feature percentages.
