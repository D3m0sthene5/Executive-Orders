## Bounded invariants over the executed readiness transition

Let `R` mean an emitted ready row, `S` its adopted provisional status, `E` the steps actually completed using exhaustion, and `G` its claim's gap disclosure. The repair enforces:

1. `R ⇒ S ∈ {yes,no}` for the non-planning path, assuming truthful current status input. Exhaustion cannot supply S.
2. `E ≠ ∅ ⇒` an actual claim exists and G is a nonempty list of nonblank strings. Explicitly WEAK/no-route/retry-later evidence and non-route values supply no E credit.
3. `R ∧ E ≠ ∅ ⇒ ready_tier = machine_exhausted`. This conveys an exception, not supported legal cessation.
4. Active exact source-audit hold ⇒ no readiness; exhaustion cannot override that hold. Changed-version warning behavior is intentionally preserved.
5. Claim-derived step credit ⇒ checker receipt canonical claim SHA equals the selected current claim SHA, the structural step passes, and working-text/factual policy checks pass. Source availability/machine-search routes are separate authorized witnesses, not claim-derived credit.

These statements are tied to actual branches and tested below. They are not a Lean theorem or a proof of historical truth. Nine regressions include all 256 subsets of the eight baseline step names against both an unadopted-status claim and an absent claim (512 bounded cases). Seven retained-source variants execute the real checker and both baseline/repaired tracker implementations. No assertion treats a route string, a metadata label or a declared model family as proof of substantive examination.
