## EO11087: what is supported and what remains conditional

The corrected inventory covers all eight sections and retains the eleven functional subjects in §2 and all §5 subparts. The original assigns standing preparation, foreign-policy guidance, interagency work, research and resource evaluation. Section 5(c) expressly integrates emergency planning into continuing activities and requires preparedness to implement plans. Completing one plan or report therefore does not, without additional evidence, exhaust those continuing assignments. The source contains no fixed expiration date or finite delivery event that establishes an earlier whole-order end.

The original separately limits emergency implementation authority (§6), permits redelegation (§7), and establishes a priority rule against inconsistent prior orders (§8). The inventory preserves them, which is good. However, a shared generic “standing preparedness regime” rationale does not itself decide how the controlling codebook treats a scope limitation, an authorization to redelegate, or an already-effective conflict rule. Their classification and exact first-end treatment must be checked against that codebook before accepting every section's shared date.

EO11490 §3015(14) expressly names EO11087 without a partial qualifier. Section 3014's retained-authority rule expressly yields to §3015. The October 28, 1969 signature and immediate revocation wording support the proposed effective date; I found no delayed commencement in the examined actor material. EO11490 §201 assigns State functions anew. That supports an end to EO11087 as the immediate source, while similar governmental functions continue under the successor and other law. It does not imply destruction of completed plans or prior acts.

## EO11089: what is supported and what remains conditional

The corrected inventory covers all seven sections, all nine §2 subparts, and all three §4 subparts. Sections 1 and 4(c) expressly concern continuing activity and readiness. Section 2 is framed as developing plans for emergency functions. No finite one-time delivery or fixed sunset was identified. In particular, the exception for nuclear weapons control already transferred to Defense is preserved in the inventory; the proposal must not be interpreted as an unconditional assertion that AEC held every weapons-control function.

Section 5's emergency-authority limit, §6's redelegation authorization and §7's priority rule require the same codebook check described for EO11087. Section-level textual completeness is supported; whether each section is an appropriate single legal lifecycle unit is still conditional on that method.

EO11490 §3015(16) expressly revokes EO11089, subject to the same §3014 exception and signature-date reasoning. Part 14 reassigns atomic-energy preparedness functions. Separate authority and the persistence of plans do not by themselves prove continued legal force of the revoked original. AEC's later abolition is not an earlier end: the NRC's official regulation states the Energy Reorganization Act became effective January 19, 1975. [NRC, 10 CFR §1.1](https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/cfr/cfr-title-10.pdf).

## Earlier-end and continuing-authority challenge

The bounded official disposition search corroborates the named 1969 revocation for both orders. It is an editorial finding aid and does not prove absence of every intervening legal event. [National Archives, 1963 Kennedy disposition table](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/executive-orders/1963-kennedy.html), [1969 Nixon disposition table](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/executive-orders/1969-nixon.html).

I separately challenged the change from Office of Emergency Planning in the originals to Office of Emergency Preparedness in EO11490. The official U.S. Code note identifies the 1968 change as a renaming with continuing-reference treatment. It does not support extinguishing the cooperative duties merely because the office name changed. This is corroborating evidence, not a substitute for the original statute if a disputed clause requires adjudication. [6 USC 765, transfer-of-functions note](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=2023&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-2023-title6-section765).

The machine-search coverage supplied with the proposals is bounded and explicitly dates the U.S. Code snapshot through 2026-10-09; I do not upgrade it to an exhaustive search or a YES predicate through the October 14 cutoff. Neither an OFR edge nor a later statutory historical note alone establishes every clause's first cessation. No contrary earlier whole-order end was located in the reviewed sources. Conversely, I did not independently trace every departmental redelegation or historical interagency agreement. The codebook must establish what level of that investigation is necessary for the source-versus-continuing-function distinction asserted here.

## Three blocks are substantively warranted

- **EO11088:** The held original §2(12) includes narcotics-law administration and §2(16) includes maritime security, rescue and navigation functions. The producer appropriately does not treat their later organizational transfer as either an automatically preserved assignment or an automatic first cessation. The exact transfer instrument, effective date and savings provisions must resolve that issue. No claimed common 1969 first end is approved.
- **EO11090:** The held original §3(d) expressly includes civil-aircraft accident investigation. The possible CAB-to-NTSB/DOT transfer is material to a per-clause lifecycle. Retain the block until the relevant instrument and savings are read; transfer of institutional responsibility and cessation of the EO must not be conflated.
- **EO11092:** Held §9 expressly preserves telecommunications orders issued under EO10705, EO10995, EO11051 and their predecessors, subject to inconsistency and later action. An undifferentiated named revocation does not resolve whether subordinate instruments remain independently supported. Retain the block and reconcile the saved instruments and authority chain.
