# 07 Legal AI products and AI deep-research tools (research date 2026-11-06)

Method: WebSearch and WebFetch only; no sign-ups, no credentials, nothing run against any product. The web-search budget ran out before every follow-up was done (gaps listed at the end). Pages marked "vendor-competitor" are written by firms that sell a rival product (mostly Vaquill AI) and were used only for facts that point back to the vendor's own pages.

## Bottom line

1. No tool in this scope has a published measurement on "is Executive Order N still in effect, and what ended it." The Stanford study had no executive-order or statutory-history category, the Vals report did not test Westlaw or Lexis, and I found no documented use of any of these products for executive-order status, Federal Register history or statutory history. Treat any claim of accuracy on this task as unmeasured.
2. The most relevant published numbers are old and not about this task: Lexis+ AI hallucinated on about 17% and Westlaw AI-Assisted Research on about 33% of 202 hard queries tested in March to May 2024. Both products have since been replaced (Protégé, CoCounsel Legal with Deep Research), and no independent retest exists.
3. The vendors' current terms bar the thing the project would want to do at scale. Lexis general terms (effective 2026-06-05) ban programmatic access and any autonomous AI agent querying the service. Thomson Reuters general terms (v6.0, 2026-09-01) ban automated, bulk or programmatic access "including APIs, MCPs, AI agents, scraping" and ban using the service for benchmarking. A student ID is for academic use only. So Westlaw and Lexis are usable as a manual, human-in-the-loop spot-check oracle, not as a pipeline stage.
4. Cheap, legitimate machine access exists only on the open side (CourtListener MCP and API, Federal Register API) or from general deep-research APIs that browse the open web. Those return leads, not source-backed claims, so they feed the existing claim-kit pipeline as hypotheses only.

## Published accuracy and hallucination evidence

- **Stanford RegLab/HAI, "Hallucination-Free?" (Magesh, Surani, Dahl, Suzgun, Manning, Ho)**. Paper at [Stanford NLP copy](https://nlp.stanford.edu/~manning/papers/Magesh-Hallucination%E2%80%90Free-2025.pdf); peer-reviewed version in [Journal of Empirical Legal Studies 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jels.12413). I read the paper text (first 100,000 of about 110,000 characters).
  - Results per query: Lexis+ AI about 17% hallucinated, 65% correct, 18% incomplete. Westlaw AI-Assisted Research about 33% hallucinated, about 41% correct, 25% incomplete. Ask Practical Law AI about 17% hallucinated, 19% correct, 62% incomplete. A secondary summary ([LLRX, 2026-02](https://www.llrx.com/2026/02/what-the-science-says-about-hallucinations-in-legal-research/)) gives GPT-4 at 43%; I did not locate that figure in the part of the paper I read.
  - 202 preregistered queries: general legal research 80, jurisdiction- or time-specific 70, factual recall 30, false-premise 22. No executive-order, Federal Register or statutory-history category. The time-specific and false-premise categories are the nearest proxies for "is this order still in effect" and are the ones tools handle worst.
  - Testing windows: Lexis+ AI and Practical Law 2024-03-22 to 2024-04-22; Westlaw 2024-05-23 to 05-27. The authors say Lexis responses changed during the study, queries were designed to be hard (so rates are not population estimates), and grading was manual (kappa 0.77).
  - Coverage-window caveat: the products tested no longer exist in that form. Lexis Protégé launched January 2025 and CoCounsel Legal (with Deep Research) in August 2025 ([LLRX 2026-04](https://www.llrx.com/2026/04/hallucinations-by-west-lexis-ai/)). Thomson Reuters and LexisNexis disputed the findings and cited unpublished internal data.
- **Stanford, "Large Legal Fictions" (Dahl et al. 2024)**: general-purpose models of 2023 hallucinated 58% (GPT-4), 69% (GPT-3.5) and 88% (Llama 2) on over 800,000 legal questions, per the [LLRX summary](https://www.llrx.com/2026/02/what-the-science-says-about-hallucinations-in-legal-research/). Models of that vintage; not a current measure.
- **Vals Legal AI Report, legal research (October 2025, tested July 2025)**: [LawSites summary](https://www.lawnext.com/2025/10/vals-ais-latest-benchmark-finds-legal-and-general-ai-now-outperform-lawyers-in-legal-research-accuracy.html). 210 questions in nine research types. Scores: Counsel Stack 81%, ChatGPT 80%, Alexi 80%, Midpage 79%, lawyer baseline 71%. Thomson Reuters and LexisNexis did not take part; vLex joined and then withdrew. No per-category figures for statutes or regulations were in the article. Multi-jurisdiction questions cost about 11 points. The earlier [February 2025 Vals report](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/archive/2025/12/VALs-AI-Legal-AI-report-February-2025-.pdf) covered other tasks (not opened in detail).
- **Schwarcz et al. 2025 randomized trial** ([summary](https://www.governance.ai/research-paper/ai-powered-lawyering-ai-reasoning-models-retrieval-augmented-generation-and-the-future-of-legal-practice)): law students using Vincent AI hallucinated about as often as students with no AI; o1-preview gave better analysis but more hallucinations.
- **LePhantomCite (Princeton, [arXiv 2606.21155](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21155))**: tests whether agents can detect fabricated citations using CourtListener and web search. Claude Code with Opus 4.8 had the best precision (76.1%) and F1 (68.8%); GPT-5 in an agentic harness had the best recall (84.4%). Pincite and misquote errors were the hardest, partly because page numbers sit behind Westlaw and Lexis. Relevant as a measure of what open-data agents can and cannot verify, which matches the project's byte-span standard. The benchmark is semi-synthetic.
- **Real-world failure**: a Sixth Circuit decision dated 2026-04-03 (*U.S. v. Farris*) found errors in a brief prepared with CoCounsel, per [LLRX](https://www.llrx.com/2026/04/hallucinations-by-west-lexis-ai/). I did not open the opinion.
- Unverified: user-forum evidence is thin. A [Discury analysis](https://discury.io/report/best-ai-legal-research-tools-reddit-analysis) covered 10 threads and 159 comments (AI-classified, self-described as unrepresentative): Harvey draws hallucinated-citation complaints, Westlaw/Lexis draw cost complaints. A [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50273623) about a self-hosted legal RAG said little about accuracy. I found no r/LawSchool thread with substance.

## Law-student access and terms

- **What students may do**: [LexisNexis educational terms](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-us/terms/educational.page): enrolled JD, LLM or paralegal students only; IDs not shareable; "academic purposes" only; research for a paying firm or company is not academic (Summer Access excepted). The page says nothing about automation, scraping, bulk download or AI. A [university policy page](https://libguides.uakron.edu/aboutthelibrary/LexisandWestlawPolicy) states misuse can be billed "at commercial rates." The Thomson Reuters law-school click-through terms (a 2019 [TWEN/Westlaw document](https://lawschool.thomsonreuters.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/TWEN-Clickthrough-12.9.2019.pdf)) bar "any technology to automatically download, mine, scrape or index our data," bar permanently storing entire documents, and restrict use to coursework.
- **What the general vendor terms add** (likely to bind student accounts too, but I could not confirm which agreement a student ID is under; the safe reading is that they do):
  - [LexisNexis General Terms, effective 2026-06-05](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-us/terms/general.page): automated access "via mechanical, programmatic, robotic, scripted or any other automated means is strictly prohibited"; no use of "any autonomous AI agent, agentic AI system" to "access, log in to, navigate, query, or otherwise interact with the Online Services"; LN IDs may not be used to enable agentic AI; no training of large language models on the Materials; no uploading Materials to third-party AI tools except narrowly defined compliant tools for incidental use; no storing Materials in a database or for more than 90 days; downloads only through platform features. The older [website terms](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-us/terms/default.page) say "You may not use any robot, spider, other automatic software or device … to monitor or copy our Web Site or the Content" without written permission.
  - [Thomson Reuters General Terms and Conditions US v6.0, modified 2026-09-01](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/content/dam/ewp-m/documents/thomsonreuters/en/pdf/other/tr-general-terms-and-conditions-2026-v6-us.pdf): section 4.4(f) bars "automated, bulk, systematic, or programmatic methods (including APIs, MCPs, AI agents, scraping"; 4.4(c) bars training on or using Content as a retrieval source (including RAG); 4.4(a) bars uploading Content to a "Professional AI Tool"; 4.4(i) bars use of the Services for "evaluation, scoring, comparison, benchmarking, or other competitive analysis"; 4.3 limits downloads to "infrequent, irregular, and ad hoc" extracts; section 17 allows suspension without notice.
  - [CoCounsel Legal connector for Claude](https://claude.com/marketplace/connectors/cocounsel-legal) (listed May 2026, pilot, revocable): Thomson Reuters content stays Thomson Reuters content, may not be passed to another professional AI tool or used as a retrieval source. It requires a paid CoCounsel Legal licence.
  - Thomson Reuters's [Westlaw Deep Research and Citation Check API terms](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/WLAapi-pst) bar mining, scraping, indexing or "otherwise automatically access[ing]" the Property, and bar using it to train AI. That API exists but is contract-only (see below).
- **Enforcement history**: *Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence*: the suit alleged a third party used a bot to download Westlaw's Key Number System at scale ([Law.com 2020](https://www.law.com/legaltechnews/2020/05/06/thomson-reuters-claims-competitors-ai-powered-bot-hijacked-westlaw-data-397-33836)); in February 2025 Judge Bibas rejected Ross's fair-use defence for its non-generative model ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/thomson-reuters-wins-copyright-case-against-former-ai-competitor)). Appeal status not confirmed. Thomson Reuters actively polices bulk Westlaw access.
- **Practical reading for Danny**: typing individual questions into school Westlaw, Lexis or CoCounsel for his own coursework-adjacent research is within academic terms on their face; whether a faculty-supervised research project counts as "academic" is a question for the NYU law librarians, who can get a written answer from the vendors. Scripting, agents (including Claude Code driving a browser session) and storing pulled text in the repo are not allowed under the current terms. Raw Westlaw or Lexis text should not be committed to the public repo.

## Product blocks

- **Thomson Reuters CoCounsel Legal with Westlaw Deep Research** ([TR press release, 2026-01-22](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en/press-releases/2026/january/thomson-reuters-brings-agentic-ai-to-over-200-law-schools); [launch post, 2025-08-05](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/thomson-reuters-is-changing-legal-research-forever-meet-deep-research/)): an agent that plans a research task and runs Westlaw tools (Key Numbers, KeyCite, statute annotations) in parallel, grounded in Westlaw and Practical Law.
  - Cost and maturity: released August 2025; the January 2026 release says more than 120,000 students at over 200 law schools get CoCounsel Legal and Deep Research, with no price or terms stated (a [BC Law newsletter](https://sites.bc.edu/bclawedtech/2025/12/01/ai-news-insights-december-2025-edition/) says student launch on 2026-01-05). Treat as free through the school licence; confirm with NYU. API: Westlaw Deep Research and Citation Check API exist but only under a signed order form ([terms](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/WLAapi-pst)); no student or self-serve route found. CoCounsel connector for Claude needs a paid licence.
  - Evidence: Thomson Reuters publishes no accuracy figures for Deep Research. Source description names Westlaw and Practical Law, and does not mention the Federal Register. The only measurement of its predecessor is the 2024 Stanford result (33% hallucination). The Sixth Circuit *Farris* decision is a single anecdote.
  - Fit: human spot-check of orders where a statute's Westlaw annotation or a later order is the likely terminator. Does not address OCR, custody, bulk status, or cost. Automation is barred.
  - Verdict: TRY (manual, 20 to 30 orders, as an independent human cross-check; log the answers by hand, do not paste Westlaw text into the repo).

The 2026 general terms bar autonomous agents from querying the service.
  - Evidence: 2024 Stanford result of about 17% hallucination (best of the three tested). No retest of Protégé found.
  - Fit: manual cross-check only; same limits as above.
  - Verdict: TRY (manual, alongside Westlaw, so the two are an independent pair of human spot-checks).

- **Descrybe** ([LawSites 2026-07](https://www.lawnext.com/2026/07/descrybe-brings-verified-legal-research-to-chatgpt.html)): free case-law site that moved to paid tiers: $25 per month standalone (works as a ChatGPT app and other connectors), $50 per month full platform; claims 300 million primary-law records including statutes and regulations; Federal Register not mentioned; terms bar bulk extraction, automated high-volume use and model training. ## General deep-research tools

- **OpenAI Deep Research and ChatGPT agent** ([model page](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/o3-deep-research); [web-search guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/tools-web-search)): the o3-deep-research API model, priced at $10 per million input and $40 per million output tokens, was shut down on 2026-07-23. OpenAI now points to long-running web research with a reasoning model (`gpt-6-astra` at high or xhigh reasoning), background mode, up to 100 allowed or blocked domains, and a tool-call fee per search. In ChatGPT, free users get limited deep research on a rolling 30-day counter, Edu gets five queries per 24 hours (a [Fast.io page](https://fast.io/resources/chatgpt-deep-research-limits/), vendor-competitor, low reliability). Student pricing: the 2025 Plus pilot ended ([Krater, April 2026](https://krater.ai/blog/ai-student-discounts-2026), vendor page). Project already runs Astra. Evidence: community reports of long runs that burned tokens without output ([OpenAI forum](https://community.openai.com/t/o3-deep-research-1-million-tokens-spent-no-output/1301382)). Fit: domain-filtered web research (govinfo.gov, federalregister.gov, archives) to propose terminators; duplicates what the existing Astra workers do. Verdict: SKIP as a separate product; use the Astra web-search tool inside existing batches if a lead-generation step is wanted.
- **Gemini Deep Research** ([cost analysis, April 2026](https://tokencost.app/blog/gemini-deep-research-agent-cost)): launched 2026-04-21 as a public preview in the Interactions API (`deep-research-preview-04-2026` and a Max variant). Estimated $1 to $3 per standard task and $3 to $7 per Max task (Google's estimates, per that page; no independent measurements). Paid API tiers only; asynchronous, usually under 20 minutes, 60-minute cap; search at $14 per 1,000 queries after a free monthly quota. Students: Google AI Pro was free for up to 12 months for US college students per the April 2026 Krater page (unverified for 2026-27); consumer limits are about 20 reports per day on paid plans per a vendor-competitor page. I could not fetch Google's own docs (blocked). Fit: the cheapest per-task agentic research with citations among the options; it browses the open web and cannot read the repo's hash-verified spans. Adoption: a day to write a wrapper and compare 30 orders to known answers. Verdict: TRY (small pilot as a lead generator for orders where the terminator is unknown; accept only leads that the existing pipeline then confirms against held sources).
- **Claude Research** ([pricing, opened 2026-11-06](https://claude.com/pricing)): the Free plan includes web search; Pro $17 per month billed annually ($20 monthly); API web search $10 per 1,000 searches; Sonnet 5.5 $2 / $10 and Opus 5.5 $4 / $20 per million tokens. Claude for Education is sold to universities, not individuals. The pricing page did not list a "Research" product by name, so I cannot state its limits. The project already uses Claude Code with web search and the CourtListener connector. Verdict: SKIP as a separate item (already in use).
- **Perplexity** ([Sonar Deep Research price listing](https://pricepertoken.com/pricing-page/model/perplexity-sonar-deep-research), third-party): $2 per million input and $8 per million output tokens, citation and search fees not listed; Education plan at $10 per month per the April 2026 Krater page. - **Elicit, Consensus, Undermind** (comparison pages only, e.g. [Casrai](https://casrai.org/compare/elicit-vs-consensus)): academic-paper search tools. They index scientific papers, not the Federal Register or statutes. - **FutureHouse and Edison Scientific** ([docs](https://docs.edisonscientific.com/)): agents for scientific literature, data analysis and molecules (Kosmos, Literature, Precedent); free tier for academics; no API mentioned in the docs I opened. Verdict: SKIP (science domain).
- **Kimi (Moonshot)**: only third-party pricing and review pages turned up, none opened in detail; no legal evidence. Verdict: SKIP (unverified, no fit).

## Could any of them answer "is EO N still in effect and what ended it"?

Reasoning from established facts, not measurement:

- Status needs the later document that names the earlier one. The Federal Register shows "Revokes" and "Revoked by" notes, and a [vendor-competitor article](https://www.vaquill.ai/blog/executive-orders-api) notes the revocation is recorded in the later order, so a tool must find later orders that cite the number. Library guides ([TAMU](https://law.tamu.libguides.com/blog/Tracking-Executive-Orders-Resource-Guide), [GWU](https://law.gwu.libguides.com/CWA/EOs)) say only that Westlaw and Lexis contain executive orders and point to Bloomberg's tracker, the Federal Register, the American Presidency Project and Cornell LII; none mention AI.
- Legal AI products are built to answer case-law and statute questions from curated Westlaw, Lexis or vLex content. For pre-1993 orders, the decisive text is often an unindexed Federal Register notice, a Statutes at Large provision or an expiry clause, and my sources do not show those tools covering them. Westlaw's source description names Westlaw and Practical Law only. I could not confirm Federal Register coverage for any product.
- The 2024 Stanford figures suggest a one-in-six to one-in-three chance of a hallucinated or wrong answer on hard legal-research questions, with time-sensitive and false-premise questions the weak spot. Expect an AI answer to an order-status question to be a lead to verify, with unknown accuracy on this task.
- General models also fail on currency: o3 applied *Chevron* after it was overruled because of its cutoff ([LLRX summary](https://www.llrx.com/2026/02/what-the-science-says-about-hallucinations-in-legal-research/)); an order revoked in 2025 or 2026 is exposed to the same problem unless live search is used.

## Considered and rejected

- Vaquill AI primary-law API (vendor-competitor to Midpage and others): its executive-order corpus holds only 3,674 Federal Register presidential documents since 2015, marks every item `in_force` and `goodLawStatus` unknown by its own description. SKIP.
- Westlaw and Lexis developer APIs: contract-only, no student route, barred from AI and scraping use by their terms. SKIP.
- CaseCite and other self-hosted legal RAG repos seen on Hacker News: no evidence of quality or federal-document coverage. SKIP.
