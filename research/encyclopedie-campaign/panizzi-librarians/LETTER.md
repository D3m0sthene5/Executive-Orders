# Questions for NYU Law's librarians (draft for Danny to send)

Named for Antonio Panizzi, the British Museum librarian whose cataloguing rules settled what readers could ask of a
collection. Draft prepared 2026-11-06 by the Encyclopédie campaign. Nothing has been sent. **Owner, 2026-11-06: last resort only.**
The campaign first looks for free, lawful routes to the same material (the Naudé survey,
`research/naude-free-sources/` on branch `enc/naude`); this letter is sent only for whatever that survey cannot find. Edit freely, and choose one
of the two bracketed sentences in the second paragraph.

**To:** NYU Law Library reference and research services (the electronic-resources or licensing librarian, if there
is one)
**Subject:** Licence terms for scripted retrieval from HeinOnline, ProQuest Congressional, Westlaw and Lexis in a
student research project

Dear Librarians,

I am a 1L at NYU Law working on an empirical legal-history project, "The Afterlife of Executive Orders." It asks, for
every U.S. executive order, whether it still has legal effect, and when and how it ceased. The output will be a
reproducible public dataset and a law-review article. [The project is supervised by Professor ______.] [The project is
not yet supervised by a faculty member; I would welcome advice on whether that matters for the questions below.]

The project already relies on public-domain government sources (govinfo, the Federal Register, the U.S. Code and
HathiTrust full-view volumes). Some historical material exists in usable form only in NYU's licensed databases. Before
I use those databases for this project, I want to make sure I stay within NYU's licences. My questions:

1. **HeinOnline** (Federal Register Library, CFR, U.S. Statutes at Large, U.S. Code archive, the List of CFR Sections
   Affected). May I download page images or PDFs of specific pages for research and keep them in a private project
   archive? May I use a script to retrieve a defined list of pages (a few hundred to a few thousand), slowly and with
   pauses? Does NYU's licence allow text and data mining of these collections, and if so, through what route (for
   example a TDM agreement or a data file from Hein)?
2. **ProQuest Congressional** (Serial Set, Congressional Record, executive-branch documents). The same questions:
   downloading specific documents, scripted retrieval of a defined list, and text and data mining.
3. **Westlaw and Lexis.** I understand that the current Thomson Reuters terms bar automated or programmatic access,
   including AI agents and scraping, and that LexisNexis's terms bar autonomous AI agents from querying the service. I
   will not automate either. Is it acceptable for me to run a short list of manual lookups myself (for example, KeyCite
   and Shepard's history for about 20 executive orders) and record my own notes of the results (dates, case names,
   citations) in a public dataset, without copying the services' text?
4. **Publication.** For each database, may short quotations of government text (which is itself public domain), page
   citations and my own coded data derived from these sources appear in a public GitHub repository and a public
   dataset release (for example on Zenodo)? Are there restrictions on publishing derived data from licensed sources?
5. **Data services.** Does the library have a text-and-data-mining service, data licences or a contact at Hein or
   ProQuest who could provide bulk data for an academic project, and would a faculty sponsor change what is possible?

If written confirmation is possible for any use that is allowed, that would help me document compliance in the
project's methods. Thank you for your help.

Best regards,
Danny Kind
NYU School of Law, Class of 2029

## Notes for Danny (not part of the email)

- Item 3 matches the campaign's standing rule: no logged-in automation of Westlaw, Lexis, HeinOnline or ProQuest,
  whatever the answer, until a librarian confirms in writing that a specific use is allowed.
- The faculty-supervision question matters because some vendor TDM terms are offered only for research under faculty
  direction, and some libraries route TDM requests through a sponsoring faculty member.
- A KeyCite/Shepard's worksheet is deferred: the free citator route (CourtListener plus Harvard's Caselaw Access
  Project, with eyecite) is being tried first.
