# Stage 12 — First independent targeted legal-chain probes (limited results)

**Status of this document:** Original and successor texts were independently looked up from public sources; this is an *adversarial issue-spotting comparison*, not an independently completed whole-order 2026 adjudication. None of the following changes Stage 11's YES/NO labels. Sources are explicitly linked so future reviewers can reproduce and challenge each inference.

## 1. EO 2596, April 14, 1917

Original Wilson order: <https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-2596-allowing-treasury-department-employees-be-appointed-defense>. This waives the 1873 EO 9 restriction specifically to permit Treasury employees to join state/local committees of defense with secretary approval. It is therefore a *waiver/amendment of a host restriction*, not solely a one-use naming of an individual appointee. EO3461 of May 16 1921 expressly included it in wartime exception revocations, while EO11408 subsequently revoked the underlying EO9/amending line to its stated scope. Sources: <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Executive_Order_3461>, <https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/codification/executive-orders-01.html>. The Stage 11 substantive NO may be well-supported **as present-status conclusion**, but 1921 is only an explicit revocation event; *first* cessation of individual wartime permissions may be earlier under original circumstances or the March 1921 joint resolution. No first-cause promotion.

## 2. EO 2602, April 18, 1917

Original Wilson order: <https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-2602-cooperation-among-civil-service-commissions>. This gave the Civil Service Commission discretion to adopt state/county/municipal eligible registers during a wartime exigency. **It also expressly conferred federal civil-service rights and privileges on the people thus appointed.** EO3461 both revoked wartime permissions and preserved the position/excepted status of employees appointed under the earlier orders. Source: <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Executive_Order_3461>. Thus a 2026 NO might be defensible under the source-attribution convention and exhaustion, but treating the original as a trivial spent recruitment permission without explaining vesting and the successor savings would be a systematic false-NO mechanism. Need trace whether the original's effect persisted until the successor and whether retained appointee rights rested exclusively on successor authority. No legal-status reversal proven.

## 3. EO 3016C, EO 3016K, and EO 3016N (1919) — who 'issued' an executive order?

Woodrow Wilson's December 3, 1918 EO3016 delegated statutory power under §12 of the Trading with the Enemy Act to Frank L. Polk; <https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Executive_Order_3016>. The Supreme Court in *United States v. Chemical Foundation*, 272 U.S. 1 (1926), independently discussed Polk's orders dated **Feb. 26 and Apr. 5, 1919**, validated action through delegated presidential power and sustained presidential ratification of the patent sales: <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-272/pdf/USREPORTS-272-1.pdf>. EO3016C and 3016K correspond to those dates and Chemical Foundation sale descriptions in the project's numbered index. EO3016N is a separate April 18 sale authority not independently settled by that Court decision. The classification must distinguish **valid legally delegated authority**, **presidential signature**, **numbered index membership**, and **lasting transferred property rights**. This is direct evidence that removing these from a presidential-order bibliography solely because Polk signed them could be legally misleading. It does not mean the original authority continues to operate in October 2026.

## 4. EO 13174, October 27, 2000 — conflicting governmental metadata

The signed presidential text reproduced in 3 CFR reads October 27, 2000: <https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2001-title3-vol1/pdf/CFR-2001-title3-vol1-eo13173.pdf> (following EO13173); APP likewise dates EO13174 October 27. The NARA 2000 disposition page records it signed **October 31, 2000**: <https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/executive-orders/2000.html>. The overlay prefers the contemporaneous text while explicitly flagging the disagreement. EO13174's commission was ordered to terminate 30 days after its report, but actual report submission was not established here; a later repeal in EO13218 may provide an outer termination bound without the *first* cause. No legal-status promotion.

## 5. EO 7933A vs EO 7933a — an actual identity duplication

The original 1938 order is titled *Inspection of Income, Excess-Profits, and Capital Stock Tax Returns by the Special Committee on Un-American Activities, House of Representatives*, dated July 14, 1938 and published at **3 FR 1943**. NARA: <https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/executive-orders/1938.html>; presidential transcription <https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/executive-order-7933-inspection-income-excess-profits-and-capital-stock-tax-returns-the>. Stage 11's uppercase early-index and lowercase modern IDs record the same object; no distinct instrument was recovered for the uppercased variant. The Stage 12 projection groups both, retaining the accepted modern NO and preserving the early-source alias as historical metadata. This is a documentary census correction, **not** a new legal-status classification.

## 6. Codebook TC07/TC09 attribution problem — unresolved, not masked

Treating an executed repeal as exhausted while continuing to attribute amended host text to its amending EO can assign different present statuses to structurally equivalent forms of a lasting legal change. The prospective codebook supplement supplies competing estimands and a fixed adversarial case panel. Pending a rule decision, it would be methodologically improper to silently relabel thousands of previously accepted cases or to present current YES/NO numbers as ontology-free measurements.
