# PD-WAR-EMERGENCY: war and emergency orders

Family doctrine for 263 orders typed by X1 as `alien_property_sale` (158), `wwi_emergency` (97), `labor_hours_suspension` (7) and `defensive_sea_area` (1). Machine form: `research/pothier/doctrines/PD-WAR-EMERGENCY.json`. Status leads are proposals for the owner, not statuses. Nothing here changes a status, the codebook, the census or the certified count (0).

## Conclusions

The doctrine proposes a `no` lead (ended before the cutoff of 2026-10-14) for **55** members, a `yes` lead for **none**, and leaves **208** `open`. Every `no` lead rests either on the order's own words (`TERM`, 20 members) or on a later order or statute whose terms reach it (`ACT`, 35 members). No member gets a `yes` lead, because no current statute, regulation or official record shows any of these orders in force today.

The 55 decided members end in one of six ways:

1. **Spent on issue (14 members, B03).** The order only amends, revokes, rescinds, restores or dissolves something made by another order, or (EO 3176) itself takes title to all seized enemy vessels. Its whole command was carried out when it was signed. Whatever it changed (the amended text, a revived power, title to the vessels) belongs to the lifecycle of the order it acted on.
2. **Own stated end (4 members).** EO 3233 ran until midnight on April 30, 1920. EO 6023 closed the air over Washington from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on March 4, 1933, inauguration day; despite its X1 type it is not a defensive sea area. EO 2929 ran during the war and for six months after a peace treaty was proclaimed, so it expired no later than June 20, 1922. EO 2652 ran "for the duration of the war", so it expired no later than December 20, 1921.
3. **Abolished by a later order in the family (20 members).** EO 3320 (August 21, 1920) abolished the Food Administration and the office of Wheat Director (9 members, B07-B08). EO 3154 (August 21, 1919) ended the Committee on Public Information (4 members, B09). EO 2952 revoked EO 2913 (B10). EO 3180 rescinded EO 2837 (B10A). The transfer of the War Trade Board to the State Department, completed by June 30, 1919, used up the two transfer orders and ended two appointments to the Board (B11-B12). EO 3019-A dissolved the War Industries Board created by EO 2868 (B16).
4. **Lever Act powers ceased (7 members, B13).** These orders gave officers powers under the Lever Food and Fuel Control Act or directed their use. The Act's provisions ceased to be in effect when the war ended, and the joint resolution of March 3, 1921 fixes that date as March 3, 1921.
5. **Overman Act ended (5 members, B15).** The Overman Act let the President reassign the work of executive agencies during the war. When it terminated, every agency went back to its statutory functions "any authorization of the President under this Act to the contrary notwithstanding".
6. **Emergency shipping fund authority ceased (5 members, B14).** All authority under the emergency shipping fund section, including authority "by him delegated", ceased six months after the peace treaty with Germany was proclaimed.

The 208 open members fall into five groups:

- **Alien Property Custodian sale orders (153 members, B18).** An order authorizing the Custodian to sell, reject bids for, resell or release named enemy property is used up once that act is done. The orders set no deadline, and no held record shows whether or when each act was done. No class-wide event ends an unexercised authorization. The Trading with the Enemy Act was excepted from the 1921 resolution and is still law. The later transfers of the Custodian's functions (1934, 1942, 1946, 1966) moved the authority without extinguishing it.
- **Other Trading with the Enemy Act delegations (8 members, B19).**
- **Eight-hour suspensions (7 members, B20).** The completion of the work they covered is not recorded, and the 1921 resolution does not reach the statute they rest on.
- **Single acts and transfers whose performance is not recorded (19 members, B21).**
- **Other members (21).** Fifteen are war-era bodies and controls without a held ending (B99). Five members' held texts are unrelated orders that the X1 typing misclassified (B01). One member's held text is another order's (B02).

The Custodian's reports would decide most of the 153 sale orders, so acquiring them is the most valuable next step.

## (a) Mechanisms

**M1, spent on issue (own terms).** A command that only revokes, rescinds, amends or restores another order, or dissolves a body that another order created, does everything it commands at signature. The lead date is the order's date. The project's earlier rule for revoking and amending orders applies.

**M2, a stated end (own terms).** The end is the stated day, read inclusively: the order runs through the end of that day, under the owner's ruling of 2026-10-21, and the exclusive reading is noted. Two orders key their end to the war.

- EO 2652 runs "for the duration of the war". The state of war with Germany was declared at an end on July 2, 1921 (Knox-Porter Resolution) and closed by the treaty proclaimed November 14, 1921. The last 1921 treaty, with Hungary, was proclaimed December 20, 1921, so the stated period was over by then on any reading.
- EO 2929 runs for the war "and for six months after the termination thereof by the proclamation of a treaty of peace", which ends no later than June 20, 1922.

The joint resolution of March 3, 1921 does not supply these dates. It governs "Acts of Congress, joint resolutions, or proclamations", not the words of an executive order.

**M3, abolition by EO 3320 (later instrument).** On August 21, 1920, EO 3320 did three things:

- It declared the Food Administration "and all remaining divisions and branches thereof" terminated and abolished.
- It terminated and abolished "the executive administration and office created by said Executive Order of May 14, 1919", which is EO 3087, the Wheat Director.
- It ordered the Grain Corporation to continue "as a governmental agency for this purpose only", namely winding up and liquidation.

Orders that create or staff those offices end on that date. Orders that direct those offices, or the Corporation as a wheat buyer, end no later than that date. EO 3320 saved sugar licenses and sugar regulations, so EO 2765, which governs licensee profits including sugar, is decided under M5 instead.

**M4, end of the Committee on Public Information (later instrument).** EO 3154 directed the Committee to transfer its papers and property to the Council of National Defense. It then provided that "upon such relinquishment and transfer the Committee on Public Information shall cease and be at an end". The National Archives records the abolition as of August 21, 1919. An order creating the Committee or one of its divisions has nothing left to govern once the Committee is gone.

**M5, cessation of the Lever Act (statute).** Section 24 of the Act of August 10, 1917 provides that "the provisions of this Act shall cease to be in effect when the existing state of war between the United States and Germany shall have terminated". It saves only acts already done, rights and obligations already accrued, pending suits and prosecutions. The joint resolution of March 3, 1921 covers any Act whose provisions depend on the date the war ended. For such an Act it treats the resolution's own effective date as that date "notwithstanding any provision ... providing any other mode of determining the date". The Lever Act is not among the resolution's exceptions; only Title 2 of the 1919 Food Control and D.C. Rents Act is. The Lever Act's powers therefore ceased on March 3, 1921.

The orders in B13 do nothing except confer those powers or direct their use. Once the powers ceased, nothing in the orders continued to operate. Each is claimed to have ended no later than March 3, 1921.

This is the one branch where the statute does not name delegations expressly. Section 24 reaches "the provisions of this Act". The doctrine reads that as reaching a delegation whose only content is those provisions. The owner's rule that repealing an authority does not end existing orders is aimed at orders with an effect of their own, such as a land withdrawal. A delegation has no effect apart from the power it passes on.

**M6, termination of the Overman Act (statute).** The Act of May 20, 1918 was to "remain in force during the continuance of the present war and for six months after the termination of the war by the proclamation of the treaty of peace, or at such earlier time as the President may designate" (40 Stat. 556). Upon its termination every agency and officer "shall exercise the same functions, duties, and powers as heretofore or as hereafter by law may be provided, any authorization of the President under this Act to the contrary notwithstanding" (40 Stat. 557). The ending therefore reaches the orders made under the Act.

The end date depends on which reading applies. Under the 1921 resolution it is September 3, 1921. Counted from the German treaty, proclaimed November 14, 1921, it is May 14, 1922. Counted from the Hungarian treaty, proclaimed December 20, 1921, it is June 20, 1922. The lead claims an interval from the order's date to June 20, 1922.

**M7, cessation of the emergency shipping fund (statute).** The Act of June 15, 1917 provides: "All authority granted to the President herein, or by him delegated, shall cease six months after a final treaty of peace is proclaimed between this Government and the German Empire" (40 Stat. 182, 183). That date is May 14, 1922, or September 3, 1921 if the 1921 resolution governs the clause. The lead claims an interval ending May 14, 1922.

**M8, dissolution of the War Industries Board (later instrument).** EO 3019-A, issued under the Overman Act, ordered the Board to "terminate and dissolve on January 1, 1919". It made exceptions: functions transferred to other bodies until they were completed, and a continuation "for the sole and only purpose of winding up". Those exceptions rest on Overman Act authority, so they ended no later than June 20, 1922 (M6).

**M9, revocation by a later member (later instrument).** EO 2952 (September 6, 1918) revokes the order of July 15, 1918 on Squares 229 and 230, which is EO 2913. EO 3180 (November 25, 1919) rescinds EO 2837 "as of the 14th day of July, 1919". EO 2837 was not merely spent: it forbade certain licenses "until further order".

**M10, the War Trade Board transfer (later instrument and performance).** EOs 3059 and 3086-A told the Board's chairman to transfer its work to the State Department when he judged it expedient. The National Archives records the Board as abolished effective June 30, 1919, with a War Trade Board Section of the State Department as its successor. That shows the transfer was done by then. It used up the transfer orders and ended the seats on the Board (EO 3024-A, 3057).

**M11, the joint resolution of March 3, 1921 (class event, lead only).** The resolution ends no order directly. It reaches an order only through the statute the order rests on, and only if that statute's force depends on the war. It does not govern an executive order's own words. It excepts the Trading with the Enemy Act and its amendments, the Liberty Bond Acts, parts of the War Finance Corporation Act and the D.C. rents title.

**M12, continuity of the Alien Property Custodian's functions (continuity only).** The Custodian's office and functions moved several times:

- EO 6694 (1934) moved them into the Department of Justice.
- EO 9095 (1942) set up a new Custodian.
- EO 9788 (1946) terminated that office and vested "all authority, rights, privileges, powers, duties, and functions" and all property still held in the Attorney General.
- EO 11281 (1966) recites that the Office of Alien Property would be abolished by June 30, 1966. It moves only the blocking controls under EO 8389.

None of these extinguishes an authorization already given. Section 5 of the Knox-Porter Resolution retained enemy property; it did not cancel sale orders. The Trading with the Enemy Act, including section 12 (now 50 U.S.C. 4312), is still law.

**M13, sale authorizations used up by performance (own terms, lead only).** Each order determines, for named property, that the Custodian may sell other than by public sale after advertisement. Some orders instead reject bids or order a resale. The order is used up when the act is done, and that date comes from the Custodian's reports. No order sets a deadline; a search of all 158 texts found none.

**M14, eight-hour suspensions (own terms, lead only).** A suspension for named work ends when the work is completed, on its own term, or by a further order. The power to suspend in case of national emergency (39 Stat. 1192) is a standing power. It is not tied to the 1917 war, so the 1921 resolution does not reach it.

## (d) Members the procedure cannot decide

The 208 open members fall into these groups:

- **153 Custodian orders (B18).** Whether each sale, rejection, resale or release took place, and when, is recorded in the Custodian's reports (S17), which are not held. EO 2832 in this branch is a general rule rather than a single authorization. Where property was returned under the 1928 Settlement of War Claims Act, the authorization became moot, which needs its own evidence.
- **8 Trading with the Enemy Act delegations (B19).** The Act is excepted from the 1921 resolution and still in force. A revocation or supersession of each delegation, for example by EO 6694, whose text is not held, must be found and read. EO 2729-A has several parts: two of them ended (its Censorship Board under EO 3132, its War Trade Board with the Board), but its other parts remain.
- **7 eight-hour suspensions (B20).** Completion records for the work are missing. So is any instrument fixing the end of the "present national emergency" that EO 2864 and EO 2928 speak of, and any order revoking EO 3015, which runs "until further order".
- **19 single acts and transfers (B21).** No record shows the acts performed: the seizure of named vessels, transfers of piers, ships, records and funds, the appraisal board, and the Committee on Public Information's transfer under EO 3154. EO 2859-A has no held text.
- **15 catch-all members (B99).** These are:
  - The Grain Corporation and the U.S. Housing Corporations (EO 2681, 3320, 2985-A, 3408), whose dissolution records are not held.
  - Radio and cable control (EO 2585, 2605-A, 2604), whose ending is not held.
  - EO 2826, whose "during the continuance of the war and until further orders" can be read to continue after the war.
  - Orders under statutes whose own termination clauses have not been read: the naval emergency fund (EO 2687), the Act of July 18, 1918 (EO 3017) and the Act of April 22, 1918 (EO 2888, which would fall under B14 if that Act amended the emergency shipping fund section).
  - Other continuing war orders (EO 2616, 2645, 2874, 3053-A).
- **6 members whose text does not belong to this family or is not their own (B01, B02).**
