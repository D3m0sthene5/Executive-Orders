# PD-WAR-EMERGENCY: war and emergency orders

This family doctrine covers 263 orders. X1 typed them as `alien_property_sale` (158), `wwi_emergency` (97), `labor_hours_suspension` (7) and `defensive_sea_area` (1). The machine form is `research/pothier/doctrines/PD-WAR-EMERGENCY.json`. Both forms apply the coordinator's rulings P1-P6 (`research/pothier/COORDINATOR_RULINGS.md`). Status leads are proposals for the owner, not statuses. Nothing here changes a status, the codebook, the census or the certified count (0).

## Conclusions

The doctrine proposes these leads:

- **`no` for 43 members**: the order ended before the cutoff of 2026-10-14. Eighteen rest on the order's own terms (`TERM`). Twenty-five rest on a later order or statute whose words reach the member (`ACT`).
- **`yes` for no member**: no current statute, regulation or official record shows any of these orders in force today.
- **`open` for 220 members.**

The 43 decided members end in one of four ways.

1. **Spent on issue, or merged into the order they amend (14 members).**
   - Four orders only rescind another order, dissolve a body another order created, or restore powers (B03, ruling P1).
   - Nine orders only amend another order or rule (B03B, ruling P2). Each one's text continues as part of the parent order until the parent ends; the parent's lifecycle carries it.
   - EO 3176 itself takes title to all seized enemy vessels, a single act done on issue (B03C).
2. **The order's own stated end (4 members).**
   - EO 3233 ran until midnight on April 30, 1920.
   - EO 6023 closed the air over Washington from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on March 4, 1933 (inauguration day). It is not a defensive sea area.
   - EO 2929 ran for the war and six months after a peace treaty was proclaimed, so it ended no later than June 20, 1922.
   - EO 2652 ran "for the duration of the war", so it ended no later than December 20, 1921.
3. **A later order whose words reach the member (8 members).**
   - EO 3320 (August 21, 1920) abolished the Food Administration (EO 2679-A) and the office of Wheat Director (EO 3087). It also ended four orders that commanded only through those offices (EO 2736, 2884, 3100, 3143).
   - EO 2952 revoked EO 2913.
   - EO 3180 rescinded EO 2837.
   - EO 3019-A, which names EO 2868, dissolved the War Industries Board.
4. **A delegation of statutory powers that the statute itself ended (16 members).** Each of these orders does nothing but hand on powers that one statute granted. That statute's own words then ended those powers:
   - the Lever Act, 6 members: "the provisions of this Act shall cease to be in effect";
   - the emergency shipping fund, 3 members: "All authority granted to the President herein, or by him delegated, shall cease";
   - the Overman Act, 7 members: "any authorization of the President under this Act to the contrary notwithstanding".

## (a) Mechanisms

**M1. Spent on issue, or merged into the parent (own terms).**

- Under P1, an order whose only operative content rescinds another order, dissolves a body another order created, or restores powers is spent on its date. What it changed belongs to the lifecycle of the order it acted on.
- Under P2, an order whose only operative content amends another order merges into that parent on issue. Each such lead carries the disclosure "its text continues as part of the parent until the parent ends; the parent's lifecycle carries it". If the owner prefers to treat an amending order as living as long as the amended text lives, these leads become `open` pending the parent.
- EO 3176 says "the possession and title of the United States in all such vessels is taken over". That single act is done by the order itself. The title is a surviving consequence.

**M2. A stated end (own terms).** A stated day is read inclusively: the order runs through the end of that day, under the owner's ruling of 2026-10-21. The exclusive reading is noted. Two orders key their end to the war:

- EO 2652 runs "for the duration of the war". Three later instruments bear on the war's end:
  - the Knox-Porter Resolution (42 Stat. 105) declared the state of war with Germany at an end on July 2, 1921;
  - the treaty with Germany, proclaimed November 14, 1921 (42 Stat. 1939), closed the war;
  - the last 1921 treaty, with Hungary, was proclaimed December 20, 1921 (42 Stat. 1951).

  On any reading, the stated period was over by December 20, 1921.
- EO 2929 runs for the war "and for six months after the termination thereof by the proclamation of a treaty of peace". That ends no later than June 20, 1922.

The joint resolution of March 3, 1921 does not supply these dates. It governs "Acts of Congress, joint resolutions, or proclamations", not the words of an executive order.

**M3. Abolition by EO 3320 (later instrument).** EO 3320 of August 21, 1920 did four things:

- It declared terminated and abolished "said United States Food Administration", which it describes as "a Government agency organized and conducted pursuant to Executive Orders and Proclamations of the President". EO 2679-A is the order that organized it. The abolition extends to "all remaining divisions and branches thereof".
- It terminated and abolished "the executive administration and office created by said Executive Order of May 14, 1919". That is EO 3087, which created the Wheat Director.
- It kept the Grain Corporation alive only "as a governmental agency for this purpose only", meaning liquidation.
- It saved sugar licenses and sugar regulations.

The four members that command only through these offices end no later than August 21, 1920.

**M4. The Committee on Public Information (lead only).** EO 3154 ordered the Committee's papers and property transferred to the Council of National Defense. It provided that "upon such relinquishment and transfer the Committee on Public Information shall cease and be at an end". So the end is conditional on a transfer, and no held record shows that transfer.

The National Archives dates the abolition to August 21, 1919, but that finding aid is not held. Once it is acquired, the four orders creating the Committee and its divisions become `no`, ended by EO 3154.

**M5. The Lever Act's provisions ceased (statute).** Section 24 of the Act of August 10, 1917 (40 Stat. 276, 283) provides that "the provisions of this Act shall cease to be in effect when the existing state of war between the United States and Germany shall have terminated". It saves only acts already done, rights and obligations already accrued, pending suits and prosecutions.

The joint resolution of March 3, 1921 (41 Stat. 1359) fixes the date. For any Act whose provisions depend on when the war ended, it treats the resolution's effective date as that date, "notwithstanding any provision ... providing any other mode of determining the date". It does not except the Lever Act, so the date is March 3, 1921.

Some orders' only operative content is a delegation of the Act's powers, or a direction on how the delegate is to use them. Those orders end with the powers. The ceasing words reach the very provisions delegated, so the delegate has nothing left to exercise.

A repeal of the authority to make new orders works differently; the standard example is FLPMA section 704(a). Existing orders keep operative effect of their own after such a repeal.

**M6. The Overman Act terminated (statute).** The Act of May 20, 1918 was to remain in force "during the continuance of the present war and for six months after the termination of the war by the proclamation of the treaty of peace, or at such earlier time as the President may designate" (40 Stat. 556). It then provides: "Upon the termination of this Act all executive or administrative agencies ... shall exercise the same functions, duties, and powers as heretofore or as hereafter by law may be provided, any authorization of the President under this Act to the contrary notwithstanding" (40 Stat. 557).

An order whose only operative content is a reassignment of functions under the Act ends with the Act. The Act's end date has three readings:

| Reading | End date |
|---|---|
| Under the 1921 resolution | September 3, 1921 |
| Six months after the German treaty | May 14, 1922 |
| Six months after the Hungarian treaty | June 20, 1922 |

The lead claims an interval from the order's date to June 20, 1922.

**M7. The emergency shipping fund ceased (statute).** The Act of June 15, 1917 (40 Stat. 182, 183) provides: "All authority granted to the President herein, or by him delegated, shall cease six months after a final treaty of peace is proclaimed between this Government and the German Empire". That date is May 14, 1922. It would be September 3, 1921 if the 1921 resolution governs the clause. An order whose only operative content is a delegation of this authority ends with it.

**M8. The War Industries Board was dissolved (later instrument).** EO 3019-A recites that EO 2868 established the Board. It orders the Board and its subordinate bodies to "terminate and dissolve on January 1, 1919". Two kinds of exception survive that date:

- functions transferred until completed;
- a continuation "for the sole and only purpose of winding up".

Both exceptions are Overman Act authorizations, so they ended with that Act (M6).

**M9. Revocation by a later member (later instrument).**

- EO 2952 (September 6, 1918) revokes "the Executive Order issued July 15, 1918" on Squares 229 and 230. That order is EO 2913.
- EO 3180 (November 25, 1919) rescinds "the Executive Order of April 11, 1918", which is EO 2837, "as of the 14th day of July, 1919". EO 2837 had forbidden certain licenses "until further order".

**M10. The War Trade Board (lead only).** The held EOs 3059 and 3086-A only authorize the transfer of the Board's work to the State Department "whenever ... expedient". The National Archives records the Board as abolished effective June 30, 1919, but that finding aid is not held. So June 30, 1919 is disclosed as an earlier candidate end, and no lead rests on it. EOs 3059 and 3086-A are decided under M6 as Overman Act reassignments.

**M11. The joint resolution of March 3, 1921 (class event, lead only).** The resolution ends no order directly. It reaches an order only through a statute the order rests on, and only if that statute's force depends on the war. It does not govern executive-order wording. It excepts:

- the Trading with the Enemy Act and its amendments;
- the Liberty Bond Acts;
- titles 1 and 3 of the War Finance Corporation Act;
- Pub. Res. 55;
- Title 2 of the 1919 Food Control and District of Columbia Rents Act.

**M12. The Custodian's functions continued (continuity only).** Four instruments moved the Custodian's functions:

- EO 6694 (1934) moved them into the Department of Justice.
- EO 9095 (1942) created a new Custodian.
- EO 9788 (1946) terminated that office. It vested "all authority, rights, privileges, powers, duties, and functions", and all property still held, in the Attorney General.
- EO 11281 (1966) recites that the Office of Alien Property would be abolished by June 30, 1966. It moves only the blocking controls of EO 8389.

None of these extinguishes an authorization already given. Section 5 of the Knox-Porter Resolution retained enemy property. The Trading with the Enemy Act, including section 12 (now 50 U.S.C. 4312), is still law.

**M13. Sale authorizations used up by performance (lead only).** Each sale, rejection, resale or release order is used up when its act is done. The date of that act comes from the Custodian's reports. No order sets a deadline; a search of all 158 texts found none.

**M14. Eight-hour suspensions (lead only).** A suspension for named work ends on completion of the work, on its own term, or by a further order. Its authority (39 Stat. 1192) is a standing power "in case of national emergency", not one tied to the 1917 war, so the 1921 resolution does not reach it.

**M15. Named persons (ruling P4).** An order whose operative content is one appointment of, or designation to act by, one named person follows PD-CS-ONE-PERSON.

## (d) Members the procedure cannot decide
