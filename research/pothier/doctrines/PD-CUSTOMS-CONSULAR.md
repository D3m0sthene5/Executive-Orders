# PD-CUSTOMS-CONSULAR: customs, tariff and consular/foreign service orders, 1873-1936

Family doctrine for the 263 members of X1 types `tariff_customs` (173) and `consular_foreign_service` (90). Machine
form: `research/pothier/doctrines/PD-CUSTOMS-CONSULAR.json`. Leads are proposals for the owner, not statuses.

## Conclusions

The doctrine decides 151 of the 263 members and leaves 112 open.

- **32 `yes` leads (branch B01).** The current customs regulation, 19 CFR 101.3(b)(1), lists every port of entry and
  cites beside a port's name the presidential or Treasury orders "which affected these ports, or which created (or
  subsequently affected) additional ports". Thirty-two members are cited there by number as the order that created a
  port that exists today or fixed its limits. The regulation therefore relies on their operative effect, which is a
  current-law predicate. The held text is the April 1, 2025 CFR edition; the eCFR amendment history, read on the web
  but not held, shows no change to the section after 2023-11-16.
- **16 `no` leads resting on a later instrument (ACT, branches B02-B12).** In each case a held later executive order
  revokes, supersedes or cancels the member by number or date, or abolishes the one port the member created: EO 5011
  (revoked by EO 5577), EO 2878, 5078, 5453, 4265, 4899 and 5284 (their ports abolished by EO 4572, 6282, 6635, 7236
  and 7463), EO 4337 (revoked by EO 4709), the four unhealthful-post orders of 1925-1930 (canceled by EO 5644 from
  July 1, 1931), EO 4210 (superseded by EO 5435), EO 5126 and 5127 (their leave-of-absence text canceled by EO 5642
  from July 1, 1931) and EO 6066 (its retention ended by the retirement ordered in EO 7061).
- **103 `no` leads resting on the order's own terms (TERM, branches B13-B20).** These comprise 4 orders with a stated
  maximum period that ran out, 7 one-person retirements or reinstatements, 38 orders whose only content abolishes ports
  of entry, 16 orders that only amend, suspend or revoke another executive order, and 38 orders that only amend the
  Consular Regulations, the Instructions to Diplomatic Officers or the Tariff of Consular Fees.
- **112 `open`.** These are port orders the current list does not cite (35), internal revenue district orders (28),
  customs district orders (13), Tariff Board orders (9), complete fee tariffs and standing fee directives (6), standing
  Foreign Service regulations (11), members outside the family's subject (6) and members whose held text cannot be used
  (4).

Two of these results depend on owner decisions. Branch B20 (38 members) applies the rule that an amending order is
spent on issue to amendments of presidential regulations that are not themselves numbered executive orders. Branch O5
keeps the complete fee tariffs open pending a ruling on whether a complete replacement tariff ends an earlier tariff
that it does not name.

Terms used here. A **port of entry** is a place where customs officers may accept entries of imported goods and
collect duties. A **customs collection district** was a larger administrative area headed by a collector of customs,
containing one or more ports. A **Treasury Decision (T.D.)** is a numbered Treasury or Customs ruling published in the
Federal Register and in the Customs Bulletin. The **Tariff of Consular Fees** is the presidential schedule of fees that
consuls charge for official services. The **Consular Regulations of 1896** and the **Instructions to Diplomatic
Officers** (1897, revised 1927) were presidentially prescribed rulebooks for the consular and diplomatic services. An
order is **spent on issue** (a project term, not a statutory one) when its command was fully performed on issue or on
its stated effective date, so nothing in it continues to command.

## (a) Mechanisms by which members end or persist

**M1: Citation in the current list of ports (persists).** 19 CFR 101.3(b) states that many ports were created by the
President's message of March 3, 1913 under the Act of August 24, 1912, and that later orders creating or affecting
ports "are cited following the name of the ports". A member cited there is an order on which the regulation relies for
a port that exists today. The lead is that the order is in force at least in the provision the regulation cites.
Members that also abolished other ports (for example EO 4156, 4572 and 4780) contain abolition clauses that were
single acts already performed. Later Treasury Decisions cited beside the same port may have redrawn part of the
limits. The predicate is dated by the CFR edition (April 1, 2025), and the eCFR versioner shows no amendment after
2023-11-16.

**M2: A later executive order that names or reaches the member (ends).** The date of the end is the later order's
stated effective date. Some later orders take effect immediately (EO 5577, EO 5435). Some fix a date (EO 5644 and EO
5642, both July 1, 1931; EO 7061, December 31, 1935). Most port orders take effect thirty days after their date, and
for those the lead records the interval from the order date to the thirtieth day. A port-creation member is treated
as ended by a later abolition only when two conditions hold: the member's sole operative act was to create that port,
and the later order abolishes the port by name. A port later re-created under the same name rests on the new order,
not on the member. Bar Harbor shows the pattern: EO 4572 abolished it and created a new, differently bounded port, and
19 CFR 101.3 today cites EO 4572, not EO 2878.

**M3: Single act of abolition (ends on the order's own terms).** An order whose only operative words abolish or
discontinue named ports performed that act on its effective date. The continuing absence of the port is a
consequence, not a continuing command. A later re-designation of the same place is a new act. For example, EO 7495
re-designated Orange, Texas after EO 7067 had abolished it. The date recorded is the order date. The held texts state
effective dates up to about two months later, most often thirty days later. EO 5291 is excluded because EO 5317
suspended it indefinitely before it took effect.

**M4: Amending order spent on issue (ends on its own terms).** The owner's rule, restated in the author brief, holds
that an order which only revokes, restores or amends another order is spent on issue as a command. Under the
strike-and-insert ruling of 2026-10-21, the amended text continues inside the parent until the parent ends. Branch B19
applies the rule where the parent is an executive order. The parents in B19 are the consular appointment regulations
of June 27, 1906 (EO 469), the diplomatic appointment regulations of November 26, 1909, the 1906 tariff (EO 496), EO
5644, EO 3104, EO 4284, EO 5291 and EO 5011. Branch B20 applies the same rule where the parent is the Consular
Regulations of 1896, the Instructions to Diplomatic Officers or the Tariff of Consular Fees. The date is the order
date.

**M5: One-person Foreign Service orders (end on their own terms).** A retirement or reinstatement of named officers is
a single act performed on the date the order states. This follows the project convention for one-person orders in
TR-CANAL-ZONE. Annuities that followed are consequences of the act.

**M6: Stated maximum period (ends on its own terms).** EO 4043 retains an officer "for a period of one year from July
1, 1924". EO 5247 and EO 5519 retain officers "not in excess of five years" from a stated day. EO 3535 authorizes
extending passports issued before June 4, 1920 so that they are valid "not to exceed two years from the date of
issuance", so no extension under it could run past June 3, 1922. The lead records the interval from the order date to
the last day the period allows.

**M7: Class events examined and not applied.** Four candidate class events were tested and rejected:
- T.D. 95-77 (60 FR 50008, effective September 30, 1995) eliminated customs districts and regions only "for the most
  part". It kept districts "as geographical descriptions for limited purposes", so it does not show that the district
  orders were extinguished.
- EO 7968 (1938) prescribed a complete new Tariff of United States Foreign Service Fees and provides that no fee is
  collected for a service it does not cover. Its express supersession list names 22 orders, and none of them is a
  member.
- EO 10718 (1957) delegated fee-setting to the Secretary of State and provides that it "shall not operate to amend,
  supersede, or terminate any rates or tariffs of fees" then in force.
- Reorganization Plan No. 1 of 1952 abolished the offices of collector of internal revenue. It has not been read, and
  nothing held shows that it extinguished the district orders.

A repeal or delegation of the authority under which an order was made is not an end of the order.

## (d) Members the procedure cannot decide (112)

- **Port orders not cited in the current list (O1, 35).** Many of these ports still exist (Sitka, Tulsa, Oklahoma
  City, Winston-Salem, Morehead City), but 19 CFR 101.3 cites a later Treasury Decision for their limits. Others were
  abolished or merged by orders outside this family. Deciding them needs the T.D. or order that last fixed or ended
  each port. Two members in O1 have a specific complication. EO 5291 was suspended indefinitely by EO 5317 before it
  took effect, and EO 6283 then abolished the same ports afresh. EO 4284 was suspended indefinitely by EO 4299, and no
  later act is held.
- **Internal revenue collection districts (O2, 28).** These orders were made under R.S. 3141-3142, and several
  designate a named collector. Reorganization Plan No. 1 of 1952 and the later Treasury district orders have not been
  read.
- **Customs collection districts (O3, 13).** T.D. 95-77 kept the districts as geographical descriptions.
- **Tariff Board (O4, 9).** The Board lapsed for want of appropriations, which is not shown to end the orders.
- **Complete fee tariffs and fee directives (O5, 6).** EO 100 and EO 496 are each a complete tariff, and the later
  complete tariffs (EO 496 for EO 100; EO 7968 for the 1906 tariff) do not name them. EO 2730 runs "during the
  continuance of the war and until further orders".
- **Standing Foreign Service regulations (O6, 11).** These include EO 469, 368, 829, 240, 2357A, 4696, 5400, 5644,
  2139, 2341 and 5435.
- **Outside the family (O7, 6).** EO 6778 (Federal Alcohol Control Administration), EO 5966 (border patrols to the
  Coast Guard, subject to congressional disapproval under section 407 of the act of June 30, 1932), EO 1960 and EO
  2017 (1914 arms-export and neutrality enforcement), EO 2620 (Virgin Islands customs) and EO 4353 (Dominican customs
  receivership).
- **Unusable text (O8, 4).** The scans of EO 10, 12 and 14 are illegible, and the held text of EO 99 is a covering
  letter about the fee tariff rather than the order.

## Data issues found

Three held later-event edges in this family are false matches. They decide nothing here, but they should be
corrected at their source.
- **EO 10.** The supersession edge from EO 10082 quotes "Executive Order No. 10,0041", which refers to EO 10004.
- **EO 12.** The revocation edge from EO 12900 quotes "Executive Order No. 12729 is revoked".
- **EO 2462.** The revocation edge from EO 9804 concerns an "Executive Order No. 2462 of September 29, 1916, placing the
  administration of the Employees' Compensation Act with respect to employees of the Alaskan ..." (quote truncated in
  the edge). The member is the September 28, 1916 amendment of Consular Regulations
  paragraph 692, so the number may be duplicated in the census.
