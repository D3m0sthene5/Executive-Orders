# PD-CS-RULES: amendments of the Civil Service Rules and related class orders (1872-1936)

Doctrine version 1, 2026-11-05. Members: the 540 orders typed `cs_rules_amendment`
(`research/pothier/work/PD-CS-RULES/MEMBERS.csv`). Machine form: `research/pothier/doctrines/PD-CS-RULES.json`.

## Conclusions

The doctrine proposes a `no` lead for 418 of the 540 members and leaves 122 `open`. It proposes no `yes` lead.
No member's own command is in force on 2026-10-14 under any branch that the held sources support. The present
Civil Service Rules (5 CFR parts 1-10) and the excepted-position schedules (5 CFR part 213) descend from later
instruments: Executive Order 10577 (1954) as amended, and regulations of the Office of Personnel Management (OPM).

The 418 `no` leads rest on four grounds.

1. **Spent amendments, 398 members (branches B12, B13, B14, B15).** These orders do one thing: they change the
   text of the Civil Service Rules, of Schedule A or B, of another executive order, or of a set of regulations the
   President prescribed. The owner's rule in the author brief says that an order that only revokes, restores or
   amends another order is spent on issue as a command, so each lead is `no` with the order's own date. The text
   the order inserted then lived on only as part of the amended instrument. For amendments of the Civil Service
   Rules, the held sources also show when that inserted text ended at the latest. EO 89 revoked all earlier rules on
   May 6, 1896. EO 209 replaced the 1896 rules "and the amendments thereof" on April 15, 1903. EO 7915 and EO 8043
   restated the whole text of the rules and of Schedules A and B with effect from February 1, 1939.
2. **General revisions, 2 members.** EO 89 (1896) ended on April 15, 1903, when EO 209 took effect. EO 209 (1903)
   ended on February 1, 1939, when EO 7915 and EO 8043 took effect (B01, B02).
3. **Rule sets revoked by EO 89, 10 members (B09, B12b).** These are rule sets prescribed, extended or amended
   before 1896: the Grant-era rules of 1872-1874 and the Internal Revenue and Government Printing Office rules of
   1895. Each ended no later than May 6, 1896, when EO 89 "revokes all others". The end date is given as an
   interval that closes on that day.
4. **Named revocations and own terms, 8 members.** Three orders only revoke another order and are spent on issue
   (B10). EO 179 was revoked by EO 250, EO 2985 was rescinded by EO 3014, and EO 3807 was superseded by EO 5459
   (B03-B05). EO 5514 and EO 5977 closed government offices on days they name (B19, B20).

The 122 `open` members are kept open for the following reasons:

- Special rules of 1897-1902 (3 members). There is evidence that special rules outlived the 1903 revision.
- Classification and covering-in orders (41 members). These orders put groups of positions into the classified
  service, or took them out. Their effect is a continuing state, and later rules saved the status such orders had
  granted.
- Waivers (9 members).
- Separate regulation sets (10 members).
- Amendments that the held text does not confirm (8 members).
- Undated scans (2 members).
- Orders of other types that were typed into this family (49 members).

The coordinator must decide two points: whether amending orders should carry their own date (the owner rule
applied here) or the date their inserted text ended, and whether a provision repeated word for word in a full
restatement counts as ended (see "Points for the coordinator").

## Terms used

- **Civil Service Rules.** The rules the President makes under the Civil Service Act of January 16, 1883
  (22 Stat. 403) and Revised Statutes section 1753 (now 5 U.S.C. 3301-3302). They govern examination, appointment,
  transfer and removal in the classified (competitive) service. The census numbers several general revisions as
  executive orders: EO 89 of May 6, 1896, EO 209 of March 20, 1903, and EO 7915 of June 24, 1938.
- **Schedule A / Schedule B.** Lists that formed part of the rules from 1903. Positions in Schedule A were excepted
  from examination. Positions in Schedule B could be filled on a noncompetitive examination. These are standard
  terms.
- **Special rule.** A freestanding rule promulgated in addition to the numbered rules, for example a rule letting
  one office fill a single named vacancy without examination. This is a term the period itself used.
- **Class order.** A project label for an order that puts a group of positions (often with their present holders)
  into the classified service, takes them out, or excepts them, without changing the text of the rules.
- **Spent on issue.** The order's command was fully performed when it was issued. Under the owner rule, an order
  that only amends or revokes another order is spent in this sense, and what it did becomes part of the history of
  the order it acted on.
- **Restatement "to read as follows".** An amending clause that replaces the whole text of an instrument with new
  text.

## (a) Mechanisms

**M1. Amendment spent on issue (own terms).** An order whose only operative content changes the text of the rules,
a schedule, another order or a presidential regulation performs its act on the day it is issued. Lead: `no`. Date:
the order's date. What the amendment did is evidence for the lifecycle of the amended instrument. For amendments
of the rules, mechanisms M3-M5 give the latest date on which the inserted text ended.

**M2. Revocation spent on issue (own terms).** The same rule applies to an order that only revokes or rescinds
another order. Date: the order's date.

**M3. EO 89 revokes all earlier rules (later instrument).** EO 89 was approved May 6, 1896. It "makes and promulgates
the following rules and revokes all others". It cites the same powers as the earlier rules: the Constitution,
R.S. sec. 1753 and the 1883 Act. Its terms therefore reach every civil service rule standing on that day,
including the Grant-era rules of 1871-1874 made under the 1871 act that became R.S. 1753, the 1888 departmental,
customs, postal and other rules, and their amendments. Date: no later than 1896-05-06. Some members may have ended
earlier. For example, the Grant-era rules lapsed in practice after 1875, but the doctrine does not rely on that
lapse.

**M4. EO 209 replaces the 1896 rules (later instrument).** EO 209 was signed March 20, 1903. The President
"promulgates the following rules in lieu of those promulgated May 6, 1896, and the amendments thereof", and the
order is "Approved, to take effect on and after April 15, 1903." Date: 1903-04-15.

**M5. EO 7915 and EO 8043 restate the rules and schedules (later instrument).** EO 7915 (June 24, 1938) provides:
"the Civil Service Rules are hereby amended, effective February 1, 1939, to read as follows". Its Rule II sec. 3(a)
continued Schedules A and B for the time being. EO 8043 (January 31, 1939) then provided: "Schedules A and B of the
Civil Service Rules are hereby amended to read as follows", and "This order shall be effective February 1, 1939."
From that day, no text of EO 209 and none of the text that the 1903-1938 amendments had inserted operated as
such. Date: 1939-02-01.

EO 7916 (June 24, 1938) sec. 9 had announced that the 1903 schedules "will be superseded by schedules designating
policy-determining positions..." to be set out in later orders. It ends nothing by itself; EO 8043 carried out the
change.

**M6. Named revocation (later instrument).** EO 250 (1904) revoked EO 179, EO 3014 (1918) rescinded EO 2985, and
EO 5459 (1930) superseded EO 3807. Date: the date of the revoking order.

**M7. Continuity under later instruments (does not end or save any member).** The following later instruments
carry the rules and schedules forward:

- EO 9830 restated the rules effective May 1, 1947. Its sec. 6.4 set out Schedules A and B in full, and its Part
  III revoked EO 7915 by name.
- EO 10577 restated the rules again with effect from 1955-01-23 ("the first Sunday after the sixtieth day after"
  November 22, 1954). It made Schedules A, B and C lists kept by the Civil Service Commission. Its sec. 301 carried
  the 1947 entries forward only "unless and until they are removed therefrom by the Commission".
- Present Schedule A, entire executive civil service (5 CFR 213.3102), rests on OPM's republication at 47 FR 28902
  (July 2, 1982), under 5 U.S.C. 3301-3302 and EO 12364.

Some present provisions match old ones in substance. Examples are chaplains (moved within Schedule A by EO 4005 in
1924) and positions to which the President appoints without Senate confirmation (EO 1784 of 1913 struck "and
confirmed by the Senate" from a related paragraph). Where such a match exists, the provision rests on these later
instruments, not on the 1872-1936 member.

No held source supports any of the following:

- a statute that ended the members as a class;
- a review program for them;
- the life of a named person as an ending mechanism (apart from the waivers, which this doctrine routes elsewhere).

The Ramspeck Act (1940) was not examined. Its role, if any, would concern coverage, not the amending orders.

## (c) Decision procedure

Branches are tried in order, and the first branch whose conditions all hold decides. The counts are from
`python3 -I jobs/pothier/pothier.py try research/pothier/doctrines/PD-CS-RULES.json --hints` (540 members,
418 decided, 1 contradiction flag).

Most branches read the WPA 1944 summary (the "summary") to learn what an order does. Amendment branches also
require the held order text to contain amending language ("amend", "strike out", "insert", "to read as follows",
"hereby revoked" and similar). Three regular expressions recur in the conditions:

- **CSR_START** matches a summary that begins with "Civil Service Rule(s)".
- **AMEND_SUM** matches an amending verb in the summary.
- **NOT_PURE_SUM** excludes summaries that show the order did more than amend: "waived", "special rule",
  "promulgat-", "prescribed", "construed", "revised and codified", "new series" or "extended to".

## (d) Members not decided (122)

- **B06, 2 (EO 3050, 3051).** No date or summary, and the scans are unread. Retype from the ProQuest page.
- **B07, 3 (EO 97, 98, 191).** These are freestanding special rules. EO 209 replaced the 1896 rules "and the
  amendments thereof", but EO 250 found it necessary in 1904 to revoke a special rule of 1902. That shows special
  rules were treated as outliving the 1903 revision. EO 97 and EO 98 except only "the successor to the present
  incumbent" and were probably exhausted by one appointment, but no evidence of the appointment is held. EO 191
  lasts "for the duration of the work" on the Government Printing Office building, and the completion date is not
  held.
- **B08 and B16, 41.** These are class orders, such as EO 290 (laborers classified), EO 1659 (Navy artisans), EO 5042
  (press helpers covered in), EO 6772 and EO 6784 (positions placed outside the classified service), and EO 205
  (one position excepted). Their effect is a continuing state of coverage. The 1939 rules saved status granted "by
  executive orders covering groups of employees with their positions into the competitive classified service"
  (EO 7915 Rule II sec. 1), which is a savings clause and not an end. Deciding these members needs either an owner
  doctrine for covering-in orders or position-by-position evidence of later exception or abolition. The branch
  also catches a few loosely matched orders of other kinds, such as EO 633, EO 91 and EO 984, which are open either
  way.
- **B11, 9.** Waivers and suspensions for persons or occasions, such as EO 1275, 3517, 3688, 3762 and 4259. Route
  them to PD-CS-ONE-PERSON or a class-waiver rule.
- **B17, 10.** Separate presidential regulation sets: labor regulations (EO 275, 285, 1108, 210), postmaster
  regulations (EO 2569A, 4538, 5104, 7421), the furlough regulations (EO 5887) and similar. The later
  postmaster regulations probably replaced the earlier ones, but their revocation clauses have not been read.
- **B18, 8.** The summary says the order is an amendment, but the held text is empty, wrong or not confirming:
  - EO 3797's held text is a different order, a one-person reinstatement of Thomas S. Shelley.
  - EO 3455's APP text is a restatement without amending words.
  - EO 2689's text grants noncompetitive examination without saying "amend".
  - EO 1180 and EO 986 add further provisions.
  - EO 173 and EO 213 construe the rules.
  - EO 687 is an erroneous amendment, revoked by EO 689.

  Once a correct text confirms a pure amendment, the member falls into B13-B15.
- **B99, 49.** Orders of other types: directives (EO 163, 1142, 362), transfers of functions or records (EO 2894,
  3439, 4239), one-person orders (EO 188, 4238), the Boxer indemnity order (EO 999), the FHA seal (EO 7058),
  Saturday half-holiday orders (EO 470, 4644), EO 4240 (reduction-in-force regulations, rescinded only in part by

## Points for the coordinator

1. **Which date for an amending order.** The brief's owner rule makes an order that only amends another order spent
   on issue, so B12-B15 use the order's own date. The coordinator's starting lead instead treated an amendment as
   merging into the rule and persisting until the rule was replaced. Both views give `no`. If the owner prefers the
   merger date, replace the date rule in B12 and B13 with an interval from the order date to 1896-05-06 and to
   1903-04-15 respectively, and in B14 with an interval to 1939-02-01. These are intervals because a later
   amendment may have replaced the paragraph earlier. For example, EO 2112 (December 28, 1914) added Subdivision IV,
   paragraph 1, to Schedule B, and EO 4065 (August 29, 1924; held CSC slice
   `jobs/csc-annual-reports/output/texts/EO_4065.txt`) struck "paragraph 1 of Subdivision IV, War Department".
2. **Word-for-word re-enactment.** Under *Bear Lake v. Garland*, 164 U.S. 1, 12, a replacing act that repeats old
   provisions may be read as continuing them. This bears on EO 209 (B02). It does not bear on the spent amending
   orders. Any EO 209 text that EO 7915 or EO 8043 repeated was continued by those orders, and EO 9830 later revoked
   EO 7915 by name. The doctrine treats the 1939 restatement as ending EO 209 and puts any surviving substance under
   M7. The owner should confirm this reading for the census.
3. **Candidate instruments.** EO 7916, EO 8743, EO 9830 and EO 10577, which the starting leads listed, end none of
   these members. The earlier amendments ended in 1896, 1903 and 1939 (EO 89, EO 209, EO 7915 and EO 8043). EO 9830
   and EO 10577 matter only as later restatements (M7). EO 9830's Part III revocation list names no member of this family.
   Its numbered orders up to No. 5924 of 1932 were checked against MEMBERS.csv and none is a member. The two
   unnumbered orders it lists (June 2, 1920 and February 19, 1934) were not matched to census ids. The rest of the
   list postdates 1936.
