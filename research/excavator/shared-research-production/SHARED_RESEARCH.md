# Shared NRA research: conditional propositions for individual applications

This brief is produced by the shared-research coordinator (OpenAI) from the independently examined historical Anthropic analysis and the separate auxiliary investigation. It establishes reusable source propositions and exclusions; it supplies no individual first-ending date or approval. Legal cutoff: October 14, 2026. Source baseline: f9c6d806ad059dcad7875ab3708715f20ea386df. Exact sources and spans are in `review-existing/EVIDENCE.json` and `auxiliary/SOURCE_PROPOSITIONS.json`; their qualifications remain part of this brief.

1. **Statutory event.** S.J. Res. 113 section 2 (49 Stat. 375) expressly repealed the Title I provisions delegating code approval/prescription and providing enforcement on June 14, 1935. Section 1 replaced the scheduled two-year Title I sunset with April 1, 1936. These are different acts. The latter remains subject to section 2(c)'s earlier-emergency-termination alternative. Reuse the exact repeal event and its functional scope, not a universal first cessation. Evidence: review-existing R1, N2C, N3ABCD, N3F4A.
2. **Earlier invalidity and operation.** Schechter (295 U.S. 495, 541–542, 551) held the common section 3 code-making delegation unconstitutional and disposed of the Live Poultry prosecution. A lack of code-specific cancellation is not affirmative proof that other codes remained legally operative until June 14. Do not automatically substitute May 27 or issuance either. A claim requiring first cessation must reconcile constitutional effect, its own terms and member-specific acts. Historical assertions that general savings law preserved all liabilities were not supported by a held savings-law source. Evidence: review-existing S_DELEGATION and S_JUDGMENT and its exact corrected P7/P8. The 10/21 unnamed-instruments ruling and 10/28 evidence ruling are retained as constraints; this brief does not silently replace either with a new ending policy.
3. **Code condition versus directed act.** Section3(a) authorizes conditions including reports/accounts, so a report is not automatically independent merely because it is a report. Sections 2 and 10 supply distinct administrative functions. Determine the actor, required conduct, trigger, authority and relationship to an operating code from the actual clause. Pure prospective code compliance may lose its predicate with the code; a study of past code operation may still have a function. Evidence: auxiliary A01–A03.
4. **Continuation counterevidence.** EO 7075 establishes study/review of past code experience and transfers eligible powers. EO 7076 continues prior Title I orders/agencies only so far as consistent with amended Title I, expressly withholding code approval/prescription/enforcement authority. EO 7252 transfers specified functions. This defeats a blanket inference that code-related administrative work ended with the repeal; it does not establish that a particular report or committee survived, was transferred or first ended April 1. EO 7075's held APP representation is abridged and cannot certify its complete inventory. Evidence: auxiliary 7075TRANSFER, 7075STUDY, 7076CONTINUE, 7252TRANSFER.
5. **Performance and separate acts.** An affirmative contemporary recital can establish that a named report/appointment occurred, with the date or interval the source actually supports. EO 6871 recites the EO 6861 committee's appointment, hearings and October 10 report and adopts its recommendations October 12; it also continues the committee. Performance of a mission is distinct from dissolution. A deadline alone proves neither completion nor noncompletion. Separate preparation, appointment, submission, receipt and continued activity. A self-executing unconditional denial or cancellation may exhaust its decisional act when effective, but only after full operative text establishes that no further act or condition is required; signature alone is insufficient. Evidence: auxiliary A09–A12; review-existing corrected P11. New orders still need individual examination.
6. **Conditional stays and amendments.** A stay pending a determination/further order requires investigation of that event; a later outer bound does not prove its first end. A relative fixed term requires the actual starting event. EO 6604B's optional hearings within 90 days and report thereafter must not become a fictional report deadline. Apply the 10/21 strike-and-insert ruling: the parent clause continues as amended; record before/after text and the amendment as a later event, with a substantive-change note when applicable. Explicit cancellation/termination is examined separately. Evidence: auxiliary A13–A14 and the owner ruling.

The filed EO 6686/6729/6870A directions and blocked EO 6601/6271/6292/6871 directions require comparable individual evidence. Their differing historical outcomes are not justified merely by calling one report or appointment code-dependent. EO 6861 has affirmative occurrence evidence and therefore presents a materially different question. Exact old claims remain unchanged and owned by their producers; corrections must go through owner review.

Exclude automatic answers for section 4 agreements/licenses, section 7 labor arrangements/limited codes, section 9 petroleum, agricultural authority, Title II, independent delegations, mixed committees/appointments/reports, or accrued defenses/liabilities. Section 5's exemption and the resolution proviso require their actual scope and wording; no conversion of every code into a voluntary agreement is established.

Every case must still establish complete working-text identity/custody and inventory, effective conditions, earlier acts and competing endpoints, independent commands, exact scope, savings/residual consequences, bounded search coverage and all material gaps. Preserve the original-replacement and quotation-recheck note. Each exact resulting claim needs fresh independent source review and ordinary controls; shared review never supplies whole-case approval. Unresolved members stay in production results.
