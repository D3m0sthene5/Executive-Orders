## Why the first-ending inference fails

The resolution supplies the missing causal connection for a statutory-repeal event: NIRA section 3 made approved codes legal standards and provided compulsory enforcement; section 2 removes that authority and enforcement. June 16 is not the repeal date, because section 1 changes the ordinary sunset and section 2 contains its own present repeal. The relevant point is the abolition of statutory support, not the mere temporal proximity of another instrument.

That causal connection does not establish an affirmative history of operation until June 14. Schechter at 541–542 condemns the unfettered delegation for trades and industries generally, not merely poultry's local commerce. At 551 the Court reverses the actual conviction. The limited parties/disposition do not make its constitutional rationale legally irrelevant to every other section 3 code. A code-specific government cancellation is one possible earlier event; the memo has not established it as the exclusive route by which legal operation could already have failed.

The memo simultaneously says the approval was void from issuance, cites a later operative-fact doctrine without holding its source, and preserves June 14 as the first end of operative effect. Those are different propositions. Prior factual existence, performed acts, final judgments, enforceability, and formal statutory authority cannot be collapsed into one endpoint. The listed sources do not supply the general temporal doctrine needed to reconcile all of them. Calling Schechter a validity characterization accurately identifies its character, but does not itself prove continued operative legal force.

**Answer A:** For an order whose sole command genuinely approves a section 3 code, record: (i) section 3's delegation was held unconstitutional May 27, 1935; (ii) its code-making/enforcement statutory basis was expressly repealed June 14, 1935; (iii) any earlier expiry, cancellation, modification or completed act found in that order's evidence. June 14 is an established later statutory backstop. The held shared sources do not prove it is the first ending for an arbitrary member. This review also does not automatically substitute May 27 or issuance as every order's first-end date. The claimed universal first-end conclusion remains unapproved.

## Savings and auxiliary-command counterexamples

The general saving statute is expressly absent from the source list. Its text, temporal application and interaction with constitutional invalidity cannot be supplied by the memo's assertion that all accrued liabilities were preserved. No prosecution found in this small source set is not evidence that no prosecution continued. A continuing injunction, final conviction, antitrust defense, contract, or accrued obligation must be treated as a separate legal effect where implicated; whether it survives is not answered by labeling it completed. Preservation of an accrued consequence also need not mean a general future code command remains operative.

The section 5 proviso's agreements category is materially different from a code approved under section 3. NIRA section 4(a) expressly provides voluntary agreement authority, section 7(b) gives certain approved labor agreements code-like effect, and section 7(c) creates limited codes. That overlap warrants separate legal analysis, rather than automatic inclusion or automatic survival. A future independent voluntary agreement is not evidence that an old compulsory code continued unchanged.

Report and committee directions supply a practical counterexample to a blanket repeal date. Section 3(a) authorizes approval conditions that include reports/accounts; sections 2 and 10 provide administrative powers; EO7075 continues review, records and voluntary cooperation and modifies earlier administrative orders only as necessary. A report about past code operation could remain a live administrative task, while a code-only prospective compliance duty loses its predicate. Conversely, a condition or stay may already have expired or been performed. Classify by each actual clause, actor and authority, not by the EO's title or by proximity to an approval. These are source-grounded counterexample patterns, not completed findings about unidentified orders.

Apply the October 21 strike-and-insert ruling: a clause whose words are replaced, or a section restated to read as follows, continues amended until its parent order ends. Record the amendment as a later_event with before-and-after text, and note a whole-section substantive rewrite for the human reviewer. Do not turn changed wording into a first-ending event. Only a separately established actual repeal, cancellation or revocation, distinguished from such a rewrite, can support ending the command it reaches. Any purported denial-exhaustion rule likewise needs an actual self-executing denial; no denial order is among the five shared sources reviewed here.

**Answer B:** Exclude automatic treatment of sections 4(a), 4(b), 7(b), 7(c), 3(e), 8(b), 9, Title II, administrative sections 2/6/10, independent labor-board authority, agricultural-adjustment authority, the adjudicated Live Poultry provisions, post-repeal orders, mixed approvals with committees/appointments/delegations/reports, accrued liabilities or defenses, and any earlier own-terms or actor ending. Section 7(c) is plausibly reached by the functional repeal description, but its separate legal basis and invalidity analysis are not adjudicated by this section 3 review. A later independent industry statute does not, without a specific bridge, continue the old order.
