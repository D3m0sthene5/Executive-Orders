## Main finding

The held sources support a distinction between the **operation of a code or exception to it** and **a separately directed act such as appointment, study, submission, or transmission**. They do not support an automatic conclusion from the labels “condition,” “committee,” or “report.” NIRA §3(a) expressly authorizes approval conditions including reports/accounts, which is strong evidence against declaring all reports independent by type. But NIRA §2(a)-(b) separately provides administrative/research powers, and post-repeal EO7075 and EO7076 expressly contemplate research and continued consistent activities. Thus code-related subject matter alone does not establish that every study/report/appointment obligation was extinguished by June 14, 1935.

S.J. Res. 113 §2 selectively repeals code-approval/prescription/enforcement provisions. It supports an outer statutory boundary for those powers; it does not establish the first ending of every instrument issued under them. Schechter, own terms, prior performance, an Administrator determination, cancellation, or replacement can present earlier-end questions. This worker has not established a general first-ending answer and does not adopt the original shared analysis's requirement of a code-specific cancellation record before May 27 can matter.

## A concrete counterexample to categorical repeal reasoning

EO7075 paragraph 3 establishes a Division of Review that shall assemble, analyze, and report on the experience of trades and industries “heretofore subject to codes,” and study code effects. Paragraph 2 preserves eligible formerly delegated Board powers, subject to the amended Act. EO7076 continues existing Title I orders and agencies until April 1, 1936 **so far as consistent** with the amended Act, while expressly excluding authority to approve/prescribe/enforce codes. EO7252 then transfers specified divisions and functions to Commerce, with directed termination no later than April 1, rather than simply obliterating all related work with the NRA.

These are affirmative evidence that the legal fate of research/administrative functions differs from that of compulsory code operation. They do **not** prove that any particular earlier report survived, that a particular committee was transferred, or that April 1 is its first end. Applicability to each old command still requires identity, authority, prior performance, and intervening-instrument evidence (A03; `7075TRANSFER`, `7075STUDY`, `7076CONTINUE`, `7252TRANSFER`).

## Actual performance evidence and its limits

**EO6861 → EO6871 is a genuine positive control drawn from the sources, not a planted item.** EO6871 affirmatively recites that the Board appointed Hotchkiss, Lauck and Nelson, that they heard protests and investigated facts, and that they reported on October 10, 1934. Its operative language adopts the report/recommendations. This supports occurrence of the earlier mission, unlike inference from a due date. Appointment is proven no later than the report date, not necessarily on that date. Presidential adoption establishes possession/consideration no later than October 12; inferring transmission by then is reasonable but is not a literal recital of the transmission act or its exact date.

The same EO6871 expressly continues that committee for further investigation and reporting. Completed work is therefore not committee dissolution. It expressly revokes only paragraph 1 of EO6861; it does not expressly revoke the whole order. The project ruling concerning emergency-board report orders is class-specific and cannot be silently generalized into either “all reports end on submission” or “all auxiliary NRA duties remain until express revocation.” Functional exhaustion, formal revocation and continuing organization need distinct assertions (A09–A10).

**New useful lead for EO6292:** EO6320 says the Oil Burner Code Authority was “duly constituted under article VII” and made an amendment application dated September 29, 1933. This is affirmative evidence of a constituted body and activity. It rebuts any claim that the held collection contains no such evidence. However, EO6292's specified planning agency must be matched to article VII and the composition requirement. If established, the constitution act can be bounded by approval and the application date; continuing functions do not thereby end (A11).

**Weaker lead for EO6271:** EO6543O identifies amendment applications by the Wall Paper Executive Committee dated September 27 and October 7, 1933. “Executive Committee” has not been proven identical to the eight-member body in EO6271. The approved code article XIV, applications and membership record are the concrete next evidence, not an assumed constitution finding (A12).

## Conditional stays

EO6513B grants an infancy-to-age-fifteen knitted-outerwear stay until the Administrator determines inclusion. EO6653 stays the price-list waiting period pending further Administrator order. Those operative stays have no function apart from their codes; for prospective code enforcement they cannot remain independently operative once the code no longer has that effect. Nevertheless, an unknown Administrator determination/order may have ended or altered them earlier. A later statutory outside bound is not their proven first end. The current held search found no matching terminating determination; that is a coverage statement, not evidence none occurred (A13).

EO6604B separates the problem especially well. Its paragraph 4 suspends a provision pending investigation/report/further order; paragraph 5 says the Administrator **may** hold hearings within ninety days and **thereafter shall** report. It is inaccurate to reduce the latter to a report required within ninety days. Whether hearings occurred, the report obligation's trigger, and subsequent administrative action remain to be established (A14).

## Evidence needed before an individual conclusion

1. Read the exact command and linked code provision, distinguishing present textual amendment, actual appointment, ongoing authority, report preparation, submission and receipt.
2. Establish identity of the body and the source of the specific function. A code name or general Title I citation is not a substitute.
3. For performance, obtain the actual act or affirmative contemporary recital and use only its supported date/interval. A due date does not supply performance; silence does not supply nonperformance.
4. For stays, obtain the triggering determination/further order and any effective-date clause. For relative sunsets, obtain the code's effective date and check alternative earlier triggers.
5. For repeal/termination, articulate how the later source extinguishes this command, accounting for continued compatible administrative/research powers and transfer language. Do not make EO7075, EO7076, EO7252, or April 1 universal endpoints.
6. Preserve the difference between an act exhausted by actual performance and a continuing body or obligation; no whole-order outcome may silently absorb unresolved clauses.
