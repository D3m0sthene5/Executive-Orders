Thus a literal whole-order sunset sentence is unnecessary to the preferred first-ending conclusion. Every remaining **prospective** operative command is bounded by Council existence. Treating the completed §4(c) revocation as a continuing command would confuse the lasting consequence with the act that produced it, contrary to the owner's evidence-decides distinction. That consequence does not keep membership ceilings, administrative support, delegated Council functions or the Council itself alive until September.

This does not assert that a named member was appointed, every service was provided, a debt existed, or all reports were submitted. It also does not conclude that termination erases the records, reports, historical appointments, accrued legal rights or the prior commission's non-revival. Those matters are residuals rather than prospective authority for a Council after its existence has expired.

## Owner B and the competing reading

The exact owner rule says:

> When a later order strikes words from a clause and inserts replacement text, or restates a section "to read as follows", the clause continues, amended, until the parent order ends. The amendment is a `later_event`, and that event records the clause text before and after.

The rule's file/hash/exact offsets remain pinned in `SOURCE_FINDINGS.json`. The preferred application gives it full effect: none of these amendments ends on its own issuance; EO 12980 does not end merely when 29 becomes 35; and a date substitution is not an ending of the amended duration clause. The amended provision continues through the **legal ending of the parent's prospective commands**. The rule does not say that only an express later revocation can supply that ending. The evidence-decides ruling separately requires distinguishing an exhausted command from surviving consequences and testing earlier endings.

The strongest competing reading preserves formal instrument force until September 30 because §4(b) expressly limits the Council rather than the entire order, and §4(c)'s legal result continues. On that reading, September 30 is the formal document endpoint and the June date is only a functional sunset. V1 adopted this reading. V2 rejects it as the first **operative-command** ending because it identifies no remaining prospective independent command after June 30. The distinction remains explicit for independent legal judgment; v2 does not conceal it by relabeling the September source as a June source.
