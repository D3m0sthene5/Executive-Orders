## Delegated authority and the competing legal reading

EO13038 §3(e) delegates the President's functions **under FACA**, except congressional reporting, to the Assistant Secretary of Commerce for Communications and Information or his designee, subject to GSA procedures. I do not assume this delegation has no legal content: it can support FACA administration and, within its scope, appropriate continuation/renewal action. Nor do I assume the charter signer's different office proves he lacked a designation. The charter's “notwithstanding any other Executive Order” recital concerning FACA functions cannot itself confer authority broader than the President actually delegated.

The best reading of the complete instruments is that this FACA delegation did not give the delegate a freestanding power to contradict the specific establishment and sunset terms of the same presidential order. Section4's reservation concerns extension after the report-relative sunset; it is not, by itself, a categorical prohibition on every possible earlier termination. The relevant point is narrower: the examined charter does not expressly revoke or abolish the **presidential** establishment authority, and no separate competent early-termination action is evidenced. It repeats the old paired reporting/termination schedule. The President subsequently commands reporting beyond October31 while preserving §4. The charter-conformity rule supports reading the obsolete October schedule subject to that later presidential action, rather than allowing it to extinguish the continuing presidential command.

The serious contrary theory is that §3(e)'s broad delegation, FACA §9(c)(I), and the signed charter's mandatory duration language together make October31 a duly authorized earlier legal termination. On that reading the charter clause itself could constitute the relevant termination action without a separately titled revocation, and .1029(b)'s renewal/new-charter requirements would matter. EO13102 expressly changes only §2; it is not an explicit charter renewal or an express amendment of October31. These are genuine interpretive objections, not cured by showing later work or by assuming a missing charter existed.

I reject that reading as the better inference on this record because it gives a subordinate, expressly derivative charter priority over the President's later prospective reporting command and preserved specific sunset, without identifying an express delegation to alter those commands. The President acts before the supposed October31 cessation, so the reasoning does not depend on implied revival after termination. The statutory two-year ceiling had not yet arrived on either October31 1998 or January17 1999, counting from March11 1997. A new charter is consequently **not demanded as an unconditional prerequisite** to the proposal: that demand would presuppose the very earlier legal termination in dispute. Any actual authorized termination or extension instrument would be important contrary evidence, but its absence alone is not the legal argument.
