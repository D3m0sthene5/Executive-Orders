## What the primary texts establish

EO 13038 §1 itself establishes the committee. Section 4 terminates it 30 days after submission unless extended by the President. The complete §3(e) delegates presidential FACA functions, except congressional reporting, to the Assistant Secretary or designee. EO 13102 is the President's later instruction, signed September 25, 1998, extending the report deadline beyond October 31 while leaving the §4 termination formula in place. The held final report affirmatively submits to the Vice President December 18; January 17 is the 30-calendar-day result.

The July 1998 annual CFR supplies distinct rules. Section 101-6.1013 makes charter filing a prerequisite to committee action. Section .1027 connects early termination to revocation or abolition of establishment authority. Section .1029 distinguishes statutory rechartering from presidential renewal. Section .1031(a) requires the agency to conform a presidential committee charter to changed executive-order authority. The statutory-committee exception in .1029(a)(1) expressly permits existence without action when a charter is not refiled; its class cannot simply be transferred to a presidential committee.

FACA §9(c)(I), as enacted, requires a charter to state a termination date if shorter than two years; §14 separates continuation/renewal from charter filing. Thus a charter is more than irrelevant paperwork. This original statute is a 1972 source, while the annual CFR is contemporaneous with the dispute; source requests preserve that difference and call for checking the statutory amendment history.

## Legal assessment and remaining adjudication

The strongest January 17 theory is that the operative presidential establishment and §4 termination formula govern the legal existence of this presidential committee. A stale agency charter inconsistent with the later presidential reporting instruction should be conformed under .1031(a); failure to amend or file may affect permission to act without itself revoking the presidential establishment. October 31 should therefore not be called an automatic earlier legal extinction merely because it is printed in the charter. No document examined shows that Commerce or its delegate revoked or abolished EO 13038's establishment authority, or exercised a separate competent power to override the President's still-operative formula. EO 13102 preceded the putative charter end, so its continuing command is not an attempt to revive a committee after October 31.

That is legal interpretation, not a newly discovered renewal fact. The contrary theory is that the charter's express termination clause was itself a legally operative, authorized earlier termination, rather than a stale or administrative estimate. The charter's wording and FACA requirement to state a short termination date warrant examining that theory. EO 13038's broad FACA delegation in §3(e) also must be reconciled with §4's specific presidential extension reservation; one cannot assume that every agency officer lacked all relevant authority. Conversely, the existence/action distinction for statutory committees cannot alone establish that this presidential committee survived.

The specific questions for source-based legal examination are:

1. Did the October 31 charter clause legally revoke or terminate presidential establishment authority, or only record an expected duration/limit on action under the then-current reporting schedule?
2. Did the later EO 13102 and the unchanged §4 formula displace the inconsistent charter date as a matter of governing authority, without requiring a separately proved renewal before a two-year statutory sunset had arisen?
3. If charter action is instead legally constitutive here, what exact delegated authority and duly adopted action establish its earlier effect, and what was the effect of any failure to conform/file after EO 13102?
4. Is there an actual later charter amendment, presidential continuation or termination notice? It would remove the factual uncertainty, but its absence does not by itself decide the hierarchy question.

The v2 formulation requiring a proved charter renewal as an unconditional prerequisite was too categorical. The narrower remaining issue is legal effect and scope of the express charter clause under the presidential order and contemporaneous FACA rules. January 17, 1999 is now the preferred legal candidate if that clause did not independently extinguish the presidential command. December 18 submission is supported; receipt remains unproved and is not the specified trigger. I retain the existing exact-first-ending hold until the competing legal readings are adjudicated from complete sources, rather than changing a frozen producer result by assumption.
