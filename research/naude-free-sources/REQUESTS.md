# Polite bulk-data requests the owner could send (drafted, not sent)

Contact addresses are deliberately not invented here; use each institution's own contact page. Each draft is short,
states the use, and asks only what is needed. Signature and affiliation are the owner's to fill in.

## R1. Law Library of Congress (CFR, Federal Register, U.S. Code digital collections)

Subject: Request for guidance on research use of the CFR, Federal Register and U.S. Code digital collections

I am building a public, citation-checked record of what happened to each U.S. executive order from 1862 to 2026
(its source texts are public-domain government documents). Your collections at loc.gov/collections/code-of-federal-regulations,
/federal-register and /united-states-code are the only free item-level copies of CFR 1938-1995, Federal Register
1936-1993 and U.S. Code 1934-1988 that I found. The item notice says the collections were made possible by an agreement
with William S. Hein & Co. and that Hein has not made them available for bulk download.

(a) May I retrieve about 200 specific items (the Title 3 executive-order chapters of the CFR and the executive-order
tables of the U.S. Code) at one request every five seconds, with a descriptive User-Agent and my contact address?
(b) If larger retrieval is allowed, is there a bulk export, a file list, or a preferred schedule?
(c) May I publish extracted facts (order number, date, status entry, page cite) and short quoted spans with citation?
I will not republish whole scans.

## R2. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)

Subject: Congressional Record bound edition 1873-1998: granule identifiers and bulk access

I am a researcher using govinfo's public-domain collections. For the digitized bound Congressional Record (volumes 1-144)
I can fetch whole-part PDFs by URL (for example GPO-CRECB-1936-pt1-v80, 491 MB) but cannot list section-level granule
ids without the API, and the DEMO_KEY allowance runs out after about ten calls.
(a) Is there a keyless listing (bulk-data file or sitemap) of package and granule ids for CRECB 1873-1998?
(b) Is a higher rate limit available for a research key, and what is the preferred way to register one?
(c) For Federal Register issues 1936-1993, is a separate OCR text or XML derivative available beside the PDF?

## R3. Harvard Library Innovation Lab (Caselaw Access Project)

Subject: Clarifying robots.txt at static.case.law

https://static.case.law/robots.txt reads "User-agent: *, Disallow: /". Your public documentation invites bulk download
of the data. I would like to download the reporter files politely (one connection, descriptive User-Agent, off-peak) to
find later court decisions that cite U.S. executive orders. Is that welcome? If so, could the robots rule be amended
for the data paths, or is there an alternative endpoint or archive you prefer? I currently use the Hugging Face
common-pile mirror and would rather cite the original.

## R4. HathiTrust (research data and datasets)

Subject: Request for page text of a small set of public-domain U.S. government volumes

My job honours robots.txt, which for generic agents disallows /cgi/ on babel.hathitrust.org, so I have not fetched any
page. I need the OCR text of about 60 full-view volumes: the 1938 Code of Federal Regulations and its 1938-1947
supplements (catalog record 006222387, htids such as uc1.b4343106 and uc1.b4343107 onward), and a few Attorney General
opinion and Statutes at Large volumes. Is there a sanctioned route (HTRC data capsule, dataset request, or a
permitted bulk download for public-domain volumes) for a non-member independent researcher? Please say which
agreement, if any, would apply.

## R5. Department of Justice OLC and Government Accountability Office (optional)

Subject: Historical opinions in machine-readable form

Your sites answer scripted requests with HTTP 403, so I have not fetched anything. I need the text of OLC opinions
that discuss executive orders (1934-1976 listings on justice.gov/olc/opinions) and Comptroller General decisions that
interpret them. Would you share a file list or bulk archive, or tell me which crawl rate and User-Agent you would
accept? If neither, I will use the Internet Archive and govinfo copies and cite them.
