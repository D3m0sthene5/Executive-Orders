**Research date:** 2026-10-17. **Legal cutoff:** 2026-10-14, America/New_York. **State:** additive AI research; zero new human certifications and no change to the Stage 17 canonical database or the reconciled 3,359 legacy decisions.

## 1. Fourteen pilot orders now have locally retained official printed pages

The [manifest](SOURCE_MANIFEST.json) pins the complete Federal Register issue URLs, byte counts and SHA-256 values, PDF page indexes, printed pages, and locally extracted page hashes. The [case audit](data/PRIMARY_SOURCE_CASE_AUDIT.json) maps **14 instruments**, **13 Stage 17 scoped provisions**, and **26 Stage 17 transitions** to 13 preserved issue excerpts. Five of the 14 instruments are amenders without their own Stage 17 provision record; their printed pages are linked to the parent provision's events. This closes a concrete source-custody gap for this pilot. The scans are **official contemporary publications, not original signed presidential papers**. The audit does not locate every later amendment, exercise of a temporary power, accrued benefit, or saved legal consequence.

The [original EO 10084](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1949-10-14/pdf/FR-1949-10-14.pdf) set a January 31, 1950 end for its adopted pay regulations. Direct scans of [EO 10098](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1950-01-27/pdf/FR-1950-01-27.pdf), [EO 10118](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1950-03-30/pdf/FR-1950-03-30.pdf), [EO 10136](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1950-07-04/pdf/FR-1950-07-04.pdf), [EO 10158](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1950-09-01/pdf/FR-1950-09-01.pdf), and [EO 10174](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1950-10-26/pdf/FR-1950-10-26.pdf) support the Stage 17 scoped prospective end dates: April 1, September 1, and November 1, 1950, and February 1, 1951, depending on statutory section. These are **prospective clause-level endpoints**, not certified whole-order first-cessation findings. The September issue's page scan identifies **EO 10118** in the predecessor chain; a searchable rendering previously returned **10113**. The direct page controls this crosswalk.

The six annual [acting-judge](SOURCE_MANIFEST.json) authorizations and two ceremonial flag orders likewise have explicit calendar bounds in their printed pages. In the [March 16, 1955 issue, p. 1569](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1955-03-16/pdf/FR-1955-03-16.pdf), **EO 10599 is dated March 14**. The March 15 signature and filing context belong to the neighboring EO 10600 and publication process respectively. Stage 17 preserved an unresolved APP/NARA conflict; the image resolves the signing date for the next migration. No claim is made that the authorized judge actually served or that resulting judicial acts expired with the authorization.

## 2. EO 1: the legal mechanism is independently traceable

The Supreme Court's [official report, *Burke v. Miltenberger*, 86 U.S. 519, 519–25](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-86/pdf/USREPORTS-86-519.pdf), reproduces Lincoln's October 20, 1862 order and its military-occupation/civil-restoration limit. The Court rejected **May 1865 surrender** as an end trigger. It held that the **April 2, 1866 proclamation** authorized dissolution but did not itself dissolve the court. The Court identified **July 28, 1866** as the actual dissolution when Congress enacted [14 Stat. 344, ch. 310](https://www.loc.gov/resource/llsalvol.llsal_014/?sp=376&st=image), transferring pending cases and judgments/decrees to regular federal courts. The statute itself speaks in terms of transfer; the “dissolution” characterization is the Court's. The [structured causal record](data/EO1_CAUSAL_HISTORY.json) identifies actor, mechanism, affected scope, competing dates, and surviving consequences.

The defensible claim is that **the provisional court's prospective operation ended by congressional transfer as construed by the Court**. It does not follow that its judgments ceased to matter. The [NARA finding aid](https://www.archives.gov/fort-worth/finding-aids/rg021-louisiana) suggests a **June 1863 narrowing of federal jurisdiction**, but the operative instrument and exact date have not been recovered; that is a possible earlier scoped change, not an asserted whole-court end. EO 1's Stage 17 status remains indeterminate and its newly proposed normative NO is **outside** the 3,359 reconciled-label numerator pending independent review and canonical integration.

## 3. Early census: source recovery is possible, but admission is a separate test

The earlier Washington link to letterbook 31 for June 1789 was erroneous: that volume begins in 1790. The [June 8, 1789 departmental request](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mgw2.028/?sp=21&st=image) is in **letterbook 28, image 21**, as a copy. The [October 6, 1789 St. Clair instructions](https://www.loc.gov/resource/law.llsp007/?sp=98&st=image) are reproduced in official *American State Papers, Indian Affairs* I, printed pp. **96–97**, now retained as a two-page excerpt. On August 28, 1790, [images 360–362](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mgw2.031/?sp=360&st=image) contain borrowing instructions and [image 363](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mgw2.031/?sp=363&st=image) a **distinct commission**. Thus Lord's two August entries correspond to different documents; neither has yet passed the project's order-inclusion test.

The fifth lead is now source recovered too. The [LOC image of the contemporary October 13, 1789 pension broadside](https://www.loc.gov/resource/rbpe.21201200/?sp=1) includes the regulation and ends with Henry Knox as War Secretary acting **“By command of the President.”** A [NARA archival guide](https://www.archives.gov/files/native-americans/m1062.pdf) calls it an executive order issued over the Secretary's signature. The printed **George Washington** name near the top is the approval line for the preceding **statute**; it cannot be mistaken for his signature on the regulation. The broadside prints that act's approval as **September 25**, whereas the [official 1 Stat. 95 page](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/STATUTE-1/pdf/STATUTE-1-Pg95-3.pdf) dates it **September 29, 1789**. This is a source-level date error in the broadside's statutory preface; the regulation itself remains dated October 13. The by-command inclusion rule, and any surviving underlying presidential instruction, still need adjudication. All **five of five** initial Lord entries now have primary historical representations; none is counted yet.

The two images for the [November 13, 1862 Lincoln confiscation directive](https://www.loc.gov/item/mal4245100/) are preserved. LOC calls it a **secretarial copy**. It is a candidate for the numbered and unnumbered census crosswalk, not an authenticated signed original or counted new identity. The [candidate table](data/EARLY_SOURCE_CANDIDATES.csv) records all six early leads and next tests. **No early candidate has been added to the 14,972 working bibliography**, and the historical denominator is still unknown.

## Scientific interpretation and next tests

The Stage 18 pilot demonstrates a useful division of labor: search and OCR produce leads; page images resolve transcription and identity errors; original statutes and judicial opinions distinguish a planned sunset from an actual cause; independent human review must certify contested legal attribution. The direct-source yield is five of five initial Lord leads, but this tiny convenience sample says nothing about national capture or AI accuracy. The status bar remains **3,359/14,972 = 22.435212%**, 384 valid additional legacy decisions short of 25%. The **3,345** decided cases without Stage 16/17 structured scoped records remain the major causal backlog.

Next, review the EO 1 claim against the full court record and the June 1863 narrowing; adjudicate early inclusion/identity for the first five Lord leads; run a stratified sample of 50 additional dated early markers; and use the 14-case source crosswalk as the pattern for auditing both a positive and a negative sample of the large decided-case backlog. Each gate needs separate source, identity, legal-scope and review-state counts. No finish date is defensible from the present pilot.
