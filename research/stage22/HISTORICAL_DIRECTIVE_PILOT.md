# Stage 22 — original-text early-presidency candidates, targeted legal inclusion review

Research cutoff: October 14, 2026. **All cases in this document are candidates, not admitted new executive orders and not completed lifecycle dossiers.** This file develops document-level leads from previously preserved original Library of Congress item metadata and downloadable LOC XML transcriptions. The historical manuscript/facsimile still controls any legal or authorship judgment.

## Directly useful, newly recovered textual evidence

### Madison to Secretary of War John Armstrong, August 13, 1814

- **Original item catalog:** https://www.loc.gov/item/mjm017456/ ; catalog title: “James Madison to John Armstrong, August 13, 1814.” LOC item says Madison is the creator/recipient as cataloged and offers a multi-page manuscript resource.
- **Preserved LOC transcription:** [mjm017456.txt](../../jobs/early-ranger-transcriptions/output/derived_text/mjm017456.txt) (LOC-provided transcription, unreviewed); original XML SHA-256 `6592b275afad9cf7556462c8712fe7d3ae1c950b29fd7325c617b4f999583e6a`.
- **Possible substantive legal instrument:** Madison gives a standing procedure for War Department decision-making requiring advance presidential communication on ten categories. The numbered list covers (1) general or permanent War Department regulations; (2) general-officer inquiry and court-martial orders; (3) certain appointments; (4) officer dismissals; (5) corps consolidations and officer transfers; (6) acceptance/refusal of higher-officer resignations; (7) militia requisitions and federal service; (8) Indian treaty instructions; (9) military district/operations directions; and (10) military district boundary changes and general-officer command transfers. He also describes urgent-case exceptions during his absence and immediate forwarding of military intelligence.
- **Why worth prioritizing:** Explicit prospective departmental norms in an instruction addressed to the serving War Secretary. This is a materially stronger potential presidential directive than a cataloged private letter. The exact text uses the formulation “The following course will be observed in future,” followed by enumerated command categories.
- **Corroboration candidates:** [August 24, 1814 Madison memorandum](https://www.loc.gov/item/mjm017468/) and [alternative copy](https://www.loc.gov/item/mjm022930/) reference the August 13 paper of instructions. [August 29 Madison memorandum](https://www.loc.gov/item/mjm017471/) also points back to the instruction. These are *leads to historical use and provenance*, not independent verification of the memorandum's later legal effect.
- **Still needed:** Read the LOC manuscript's exact original pages (not solely the 1900–1910 edited published transcription); ascertain whether the instruction was actually transmitted and in which form; distinguish mandatory presidential approval from prior communication, departmental exceptions, and later administrative practices; compare against project inclusion criteria and Lord's index. The precise beginning and first cessation of any operative requirement remain **unknown**. An administration transition alone cannot establish it.

### Andrew Jackson to Secretary of War Lewis Cass, October 29, 1832

- **Original catalog item:** https://www.loc.gov/item/maj012871/ ; [preserved LOC transcription](../../jobs/early-ranger-transcriptions/output/derived_text/maj012871.txt), original XML SHA-256 `74a232156e53906a1b79759dd304aac3615ece66c82fc7fbf68c52adca6a1a40`.
- **Evidence lead:** Explicit confidential presidential direction that the War Secretary issue defensive instructions to officers commanding Charleston harbor forts, prepare against attempted seizure, and resist force. This appears to be a time-sensitive operational directive with downstream subordinate orders to seek.
- **Open issues:** Identify actual dispatch, downstream secret orders, dates, signatory/capacity and scope. Even if issued as a presidential order, the event's practical end cannot be inferred from the end of the Nullification Crisis. Whether classified as an EO under frozen codebook remains unresolved.

### Jefferson to Meriwether Lewis, June 1803

- **Catalog item:** https://www.loc.gov/item/mtjbib012509/ ; [LOC transcription](../../jobs/early-ranger-transcriptions/output/derived_text/mtjbib012509.txt), original XML SHA-256 `ebea45e6d767548c44c2070f75a0b6fc2345e2301aefd1bedc4481cf09a31134`.
- **Potential substantive content:** The multi-page instruction discusses statutory authorization, Lewis's appointment, expedition personnel and authority, geographical survey tasks, diplomatic relations and other operational directions.
- **Date caution:** Transcription itself titles it as drafted *before June 20, 1803* while the LOC catalog gives June 20; exact issuance and signature may differ. Any presidential executive-order inclusion decision must resolve the date and test project criteria for a mission-specific instruction.

## Exclusions and controls for rapid, accurate batch screening

The following titles illustrate why keyword-based identification inflates historical counts:

- [Madison to William Eustis, December 4, 1812](https://www.loc.gov/item/mjm016773/) mainly expresses appreciation and hopes Eustis will continue until replaced. **Likely ordinary correspondence** on present text; do not admit automatically.
- [Madison to Governor Daniel Tompkins, September 28, 1814](https://www.loc.gov/item/mjm017428/) requests permission for a possible nomination. **Likely nomination-related correspondence**, not a mandatory War Department instruction.
- Madison's separate August 24/29 memoranda may relate to the August 13 instruction but are retrospective notes/interpretation sources; do not count them as several executive orders. Compare the two August 24 manuscript witnesses for possible overlap.
- [Jefferson to Henry Dearborn, March 29, 1807](https://www.loc.gov/item/mtjbib017088/) discusses whether removal of an officer is justified and leans against it. Do not infer an actual dismissal order.
