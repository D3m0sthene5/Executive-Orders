## EO 6877 — a purported original expiration is not the first cessation date

The text of EO 6877 (1934-10-16) purported to govern the Wool Textile Work Assignment Board through February 1, 1935. On **February 1, 1935**, EO 6955-A amended EO 6877's board mandate so that the Board administered the relevant code provisions **until 30 days after submission of its report to the President**. Thus any automated method that puts `first_cessation_date=1935-02-01` merely because the original order contained that date is **wrong**. The actual report date remains unproved from the sources reviewed; the fate of Title I of NIRA after *Schechter Poultry* in May 1935 creates a potentially earlier later-imposed boundary. Do not assign an exact first cessation or a natural/intervention causal category without the report and rule chronology. Current Stage 11 substantive NO is independently supported by the obsolete Title I code apparatus, not by the original February 1 date.

## Historical unnumbered President Polk order, February 9, 1849

The National Archives 1989 numeric codification lists an unnumbered EO dated **February 9, 1849**, and EO 9761 (signed President Truman July 23, 1946) explicitly supersedes the **order of President James K. Polk dated February 9, 1849** concerning preservation and display of captured enemy flags. This is independent presidential attribution and dating evidence. But the original Polk-signed page has **not** yet been inspected, and this record must remain a bibliographic reference candidate until its document identity is crosswalked. The succession itself shows that a nineteenth-century presidential legal instruction had a legally recognized later endpoint, contrary to treating pre-1862 orders as an empty sample.

## Same-day February 26, 1852 orders cannot be collapsed from a date alone

The NARA table's unnumbered `Feb. 26, 1852` entry concerns lands later affected by Presidential Proclamations 3273 (1959) and 4319 (1974), namely Point Loma/Fort Rosecrans. This is independently supported by the Navy's Point Loma history, which traces presidential withdrawal to February 1852. However, a 1878 Senate document also prints and discusses a separately described military reservation at Cape Disappointment **approved by President Millard Fillmore on February 26, 1852**. Whether these were distinct signed instruments, separate provisions, or editorially separated entries requires original-document investigation. An unnumbered DATE is **not** a unique document key. This must be explicitly resolved before the source row is counted as precisely one presidential signed act.

## 1914 August 8 archival row collision, high-priority documentary test

The NARA codification lists **two separate unnumbered source-row appearances** of `Aug. 8, 1914` and annotates *both* as amended by EO 8507. EO 8507 (Aug. 8, 1940) refers to **one** order of August 8, 1914 creating Public Water Reserve No. 22 and supersedes it **insofar as the 1940 land withdrawal overlaps**. This strongly suggests duplicate archival source-row representations, but the original August 8, 1914 signed instrument(s) have not been recovered, so **do not merge yet**. The whole-order cessation of the 1914 instrument also cannot be inferred from EO 8507 because its supersession was expressly limited to affected tracts.

## Frank L. Polk 1919 delegated property orders — validity does not prove presidential authorship

In *United States v. Chemical Foundation, Inc.*, 272 U.S. 1 (1926), the Supreme Court affirmed the legal validity of orders Polk made **February 26 and April 5, 1919**, following President Wilson's **December 3, 1918** delegation of power under the Trading with the Enemy Act. Historic order labels associate these with **3016-C and 3016-K**. This is proof that a lawful exercise of presidential delegated authority can be an instrument authored by a non-President. A strict `signed_presidential_orders_only` sample and an `officially_numbered_delegated_instruments` catalogue cannot be silently combined. EO3016-N and others require individual signature evidence.
