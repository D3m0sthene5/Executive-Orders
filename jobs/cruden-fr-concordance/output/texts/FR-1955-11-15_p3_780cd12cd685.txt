(a) In computing gross income, adjusted

shall be taken into account regardless

be separately computed. In computing
the adjusted gross income or the taxable
income of a taxpayer other than a cor­

long-term capital gain exceeds the net
short-term capital loss, 50 percent of the
amount of the excess is allowable as a
deduction from gross income under sec­
tion 1202.
(b)
For the purpose of computing the
deduction allowable under section 1202
in the case of an estate or trust, any
long-term or short-term capital gains
which, under sections 652 and 662, are
includible in the gross income of its in­
come beneficiaries as gains derived from
the sale or exchange of capital assets
must be excluded in determining
whether, for the taxable year of the es­
tate or trust, its net long-term capital
gain exceeds its net short-term capital
loss. To determine the extent to which
such gains are includible in the gross
income of a beneficiary, see the regu­
lations' under sections 652 and 662. For
example, during 1954 a trust realized a

for 10 months. Under the terms of the

be distributed during the taxable year to

that under section 652 or 662 A must in-'
elude all of such gain in his gross income,
the trust is not entitled to any deduction
with respect to such gain under section
1202. Assuming A had no other capital
gains or losses for 1954, he would be en­
titled to a deduction of $500 under sec­
tion 1202. For purposes of this section,
