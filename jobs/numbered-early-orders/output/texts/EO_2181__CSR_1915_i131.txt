April 27, 1915.
Mrs. Lillian B. S. Grant may be reinstated to a position in the classified service in
the Post Office Department without regard to the length of time she has been sep-
arated from the service.
Mrs. Grant, as Mrs. Lillie B. Silcott, was appointed to the position of printer's
assistant in the Bureau of Printing and Engraving on August 2, 1895, and was pro-
moted to operative and through the several grades of that position. On July 1, 1902,
she was transferred to the Post Office Department as clerk at |900, and was promoted
to $1,000 July 1, 1905. She resigned May 9, 1912.
The Postmaster General advises that it is stated that Mrs. Grant's husband is a
permanent invalid, and that she is therefore compelled to provide a livelihood for
both her husband and herself. In view of the circumstances and the fact that the
records of the department show that her services were satisfactory and her duties
faithfully dischajged, the Postmaster General states that no objection would be
offered by the department to the reinstatement of Mrs. Grant by Executive order.

April 27, 1915.
Mrs. Lucy Jones Harris, of Kentucky, may be appointed to a position in the Immi-
gration Service, Department of Labor, without reference to the civil-service rules.
This order is issued in view of the statement of the Secretary of Labor that the
department can use to advantage the services of Mrs. Harris in the Immigration Service,
in which the Commissioner General of Immigration informs him there is urgent need
of a woman of Mrs. Harris's peculiar qualifications in the labor distribution branch
of tiie service at New York City, where it is deemed advisable, in the development
of tiiis new service, to have a woman of her high character and sympathetic initiative,
who is able at once to take care of the reauirements of the woman labor problem ana
greatly assist in carrying out the plan of aistribution inaug[uarted by the department.
