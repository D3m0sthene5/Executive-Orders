Executive Order
Article XXIX of the Consular Regulations of 1896, entitled " Record Books
and Archives " , is hereby canceled and the following substituted :
ARTICLE XXIX
RECORD BOOKS AND ARCHIVES
604. Record books at consulates . The following record books are to be kept at
consular offices :
At inland consulates :
(1) A record of fees and declared exports , in which shall be recorded daily in
the order in which received all fees for services , including gratis services , authorized
by the Tariff of United States Consular Fees , the serial number, date, and amount
of each fee, the nature of the service , and the person for whom performed . When
fees for invoices are recorded , the kind , quantity , and value of the merchandise
covered by the invoice shall also be recorded .
(2) A fee stamp ledger, in which shall be kept a daily record of the kinds and
values of all fee stamps received and canceled , or otherwise expended .
(3) A register of correspondence received and sent, in which shall be recorded
daily every communication of whatever nature received by or sent from the office ,
together with the postage expended on each .
(4) A miscellaneous record book, in which shall be kept such records as the
Department may from time to time direct, as well as records of all official matters
pertaining to the office that require to be recorded and for which no other book is
provided .
(5) A passport book, in which shall be recorded , in the prescribed manner, all
passports issued by the consulate .
(6) A register of landing or debenture certificates , in which shall be recorded
the number and date of each certificate authenticated, the port of shipment, the
name of the vessel from which the merchandise was landed , the name of the master ,
the landing port , the numbers , marks and contents of packages , the date of the
bill of lading , the name of the shipper, the name of the consignee , and the date of
unlading .
At seaport consulates :
In addition to the foregoing , the following record books will be kept at seaport
consulates :
(1 ) A record of arrivals and departures of American vessels , in which shall be
recorded the number, date of arrival , class , name , and tonnage of all American
vessels arriving at and departing from the port, where belonging , from what port ,
where bound , date of clearance , and cargo both inward and outward by kind , quan￾tity, and value .
(2) A seaman's register, in which shall be recorded a detailed list of all seamen
shipped , discharged , deserted , or deceased at the consulate , the name of the vessel
to which belonging, and the arrears of wages and extra wages collected and paid
on account of each .

2
(3) A record of American seamen relieved , in which shall be recorded the num￾ber and names of all seamen relieved , from what vessel discharged , date and cause
of discharge , date of leaving the consulate , and a detailed statement of the amounts
disbursed on account of each .
(4) A ship's daily journal , in which should be recorded the name of every
American vessel arriving at the port, its registry number, tonnage , where belonging ,
date of arrival , from what port , when and where built , owners , cargo both inward
and outward and value thereof, the names of the master and other deck officers ,
any service performed for or any incident occurring to the vessel during its stay in
port, and the date of its departure.
(5) A marine note of protest book, for the entry of marine notes of protest, in
accordance with Form No. 37.
(6) A marine extended protest book, for the entry of extended notes of protest ,
in accordance with Form No. 38 .
605. Record books at consular agencies . The following record books are required
to be kept at consular agencies :
At inland consular agencies :
(1 ) A record of fees and declared exports .
(2) A fee stamp ledger.
(3) A register of correspondence received and sent.
(4) A miscellaneous record book.
(5) A register of landing or debenture certificates .
At seaport consular agencies :
In addition to the foregoing , the following record books will be kept at seaport
consular agencies :
(1) A record of arrivals and departures of American vessels .
(2) A seamen's register .
(3) A record of American seamen relieved .
(4) A ship's daily journal .
(5) A marine note of protest book .
(6) A marine extended protest book.
(7) A record of official services to American vessels and seamen , in which shall
be recorded an itemized statement of the official services actually and necessarily
rendered to American vessels and seamen .
606. Files . In addition to the record books mentioned in paragraphs 604 and
605 , the following files are to be kept at consulates or agencies as may be required :
(1) A file of correspondence received and sent , which shall contain all cor￾respondence received and copies of all correspondence sent , classified and arranged
in accordance with regulations prescribed by the Secretary of State .
(2) A file of the Department's numbered circular instructions .
(3) A file of original invoices certified at the office during the past five years .
(4) A file of powers of attorney to sign invoices .
(5) A file of triplicate bills of health .
(6) A file of duplicate accounts and returns of the office .
(7) A file of approved applications of American citizens for registration , and
card file thereto .
(8) A file of alien visa applications , visa refusals and refusal notification cards .
(9) A confidential file, containing true readings of code telegrams sent and
received , and correspondence of a confidential nature.
(10) A file of the efficiency records of consular officers and employees submitted
by the officer in charge .

3
607. What are to be regarded as official documents . The originals of all
instructions , letters and telegrams addressed to a consular officer and copies of all
despatches , letters , and telegrams written by him in his official capacity including
all official reports , accounts and returns, also all the record books described in this
article, and all books presented to the consulate or sent to it by the Department
of State are to be considered as official documents . They must be deposited
among the consular archives , after being duly entered upon the inventory of United
States government property of the consulate (Form No. 223) and must be trans￾ferred by the officer in charge , together with all other property in his care belonging
to the United States , to his successor in office .
608. Care of archives . All consular officers are instructed to take care that the
archives of their offices are kept in proper order ; and with this in view, as well as
to facilitate reference to previous correspondence , they will keep in their offices
records (Form No. 223) of all the documents , papers , letters and books which have
been received or sent on matters connected with their official duties .
THE WHITE HOUSE ,
June 26, 1926.
[No. 4463 ]
CALVIN COOLIDGE

Executive Order
It is hereby ordered , under authority contained in the Act of June 21 , 1906
(34 Stat. 325-326 ), that the trust period on all allotments of the Papago and other
Indians of the Papago Reservation in Arizona , the title to which has not passed
out of the United States and which would otherwise expire during the calendar
years 1926 , or 1927, be , and the same is hereby extended for a further period of
ten years .
THE WHITE HOUSE ,
June 28, 1926 .

The Consular Regulations are hereby amended as follows :
Paragraph 518 is amended to read as follows :
518. President to prescribe fees for official services .-The President is author￾ized to prescribe from time to time the rates or tariffs of fees to be charged by
consular officers for official services , and such rates or tariffs are required to be
reported annually to Congress .-R. S. 1745; 34 Stat . L. 101 , Secs . 7, 8 , 9.
Paragraph 520 is amended to read as follows :
520. American vessels not required to pay .-By law no fees named in the tariff
of consular fees prescribed by the President can be collected by consular officers
from regularly documented American vessels and seamen for official services to
them. Consular agents , however , who are compensated by fees , must furnish the
masters of every such vessel with an itemized statement of such services performed
on account of said vessel , with the fee so prescribed for each service , and also make
a detailed report of such services and fees , under such regulations as the Secretary
of State may prescribe , and they will be allowed such compensation for said ser￾vices as they would have received prior to the passage of said law. Such services
will not be compensated , unless they have been necessarily rendered .-23 Stat . L.
56; 18 Op . Att. Gen. 111 , 234 .

Paragraph 522 is amended to read as follows :
522. No additional charges allowed .--When a fee is fixed in the tariff of fees for
any particular act or service , no additional fee is to be demanded for signature ,
attestation or affixing the seal of office ; nor may any subordinate in the consulate
charge a fee for any assistance rendered by him in connection with any service
performed by the principal officer and for which the latter has charged a fee .
In regard to the verification of invoices, a penalty is provided for charging for
blank forms, advice, or for clerical services in the preparation of the declaration
or certificate, or for receiving any fee greater than that authorized by law; and if
a consular officer collects or knowingly allows to be collected any other or greater
fees than are allowed by law for any service, he is liable in treble the amount of

Paragraph 524 is amended to read as follow :
524. Tonnage and other fees charged against the Treasury. The tonnage fees
provided for in fees 16 and 17 of the Tariff of the United States Consular Fees are
not charged by consular officers against regularly documented ' American vessels
and are not charged by consular agents against the Treasury for such vessels run￾ning regularly , making weekly or monthly trips , or otherwise , to or between foreign
ports, for more than four trips in a year . These fees are chargeable , however , by
consular agents against the Treasury for each vessel on the first four trips in each
calendar year . They are chargeable at the foreign port where the principal offices
of the steamship company or owners are located, but are not to be charged at
other consular offices on the route, whether within or without the same allegiance .
Consular fees other than tonnage fees are charged by consular agents against
the Treasury for all services performed for regularly documented American vessels ;
and when a vessel makes a special trip to a foreign port other than that to which
it runs on its regular trips , a consular agent at the port thus visited is entitled to
receive tonnage fees for the special trip .-R. S. Sec. 1720; 1 Comp . Dec. 374 .

Executive Order
It is hereby ordered that two small islands known as Johnston island and Sand
island, located in the Pacific Ocean , approximately in latitude 16 ° 44 ′ 45 ″ North
and longitude 169 ° 30 ′ 30 ″ West from Greenwich, as segregated by the broken line
upon the diagram hereto attached and made a part of this order, be and the same
are hereby reserved and set apart for the use of the Department of Agriculture as
a refuge and breeding ground for native birds .
It is unlawful for any person to hunt, trap , capture, wilfully disturb or kill any
bird of any kind whatever , or take the eggs of such bird within the limits of this
reserve , except under such rules and regulations as may be prescribed by the
Secretary of Agriculture .
Warning is expressly given to all persons not to commit any of the acts herein
enumerated, under the penalties prescribed by Section 84 of the U. S. Penal Code,
approved March 4 , 1909 (35 Stat .
, 1088 )
, as amended by the Act approved April
15 , 1924 (43 Stat .
, 98 ) .
This reservation to be known as Johnston Island Reservation .

605. Record books at consular agencies . The following record books are required
to be kept at consular agencies :
At inland consular agencies :
(1) A record of fees and declared exports .
(2) A fee stamp ledger .
(3) A register of correspondence received and sent .
(4) A miscellaneous record book .
(5) A register of landing or debenture certificates .
At seaport consular agencies :
In addition to the foregoing , the following record books will be kept at seaport
consular agencies :
(1) A record of arrivals and departures of American vessels .
(2) A seamen's register.
(3) A record of American seamen relieved .
(4) A ship's daily journal .
(5) A marine note of protest book .
(6) A marine extended protest book.
(7) A record of official services to American vessels and seamen , in which shall
be recorded an itemized statement of the official services actually and necessarily
rendered to American vessels and seamen .
606. Files . In addition to the record books mentioned in paragraphs 604 and
605, the following files are to be kept at consulates or agencies as may be required :
( 1) A file of correspondence received and sent , which shall contain all cor￾respondence received and copies of all correspondence sent , classified and arranged
in accordance with regulations prescribed by the Secretary of State .
(2) A file of the Department's numbered circular instructions .
(3) A file of original invoices certified at the office during the past five years .
(4) A file of powers of attorney to sign invoices .
(5) A file of triplicate bills of health .
(6) A file of duplicate accounts and returns of the office .
(7) A file of approved applications of American citizens for registration , and
card file thereto .
(8) A file of alien visa applications, visa refusals and refusal notification cards .
(9) A confidential file, containing true readings of code telegrams sent and
received , and correspondence of a confidential nature.
(10) A file of the efficiency records of consular officers and employees submitted
