AP1000 Design Certification Renewal

AGENCY: Nuclear Regulatory Commission.

ACTION: Direct final rule and issuance of environmental assessment.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) is amending its 
regulations to renew the AP1000 design certification and incorporate 
Revision 20 of the AP1000 design control document. The purpose of this 
rulemaking action is to update the AP1000 design to be consistent with 
changes made to the design during the licensing, construction, and 
operation of Vogtle Electric Generating Plant, Units 3 and 4. This 
rulemaking incorporates AP1000 design control document Revision 20, 
which includes updates made to the AP1000 design to represent a 
current, construction-validated design. The NRC is also issuing an 
environmental assessment supporting this rule.

significant adverse comments are received by November 13, 2026. If the 
direct final rule is withdrawn as a result of such comments, timely 
notice of the withdrawal will be published in the Federal Register.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION.)

    A significant adverse comment is a comment where the commenter 
explains why the rule would be inappropriate, including challenges to 
the rule's underlying premise or approach, or would be ineffective or 
unacceptable without a change. A comment is adverse and significant if:
    (1) The comment opposes the rule and provides a reason sufficient 
to require a substantive response in a notice-and-comment process. For 
example, a substantive response is required when:
    (a) The comment causes the NRC to reevaluate (or reconsider) its 
position or conduct additional analysis;
    (b) The comment raises an issue serious enough to warrant a 
substantive response to clarify or complete the record; or
    (c) The comment raises a relevant issue that was not previously 
addressed or considered by the NRC.
    (2) The comment proposes a change or an addition to the rule, and 
it is apparent that the rule would be ineffective or unacceptable 
without incorporation of the change or addition.
    (3) The comment causes the NRC to make a change (other than 
editorial) to the rule.

III. Background

    The NRC originally approved the AP1000 design certification in a 
final rule published on January 27, 2006 (71 FR 4464). On December 30, 
2011 (76 FR 82079), the NRC published a final rule amending appendix D, 
``Design Certification Rule for the AP1000 Design,'' to part 52, 
``Licenses, Certifications, and Approvals for Nuclear Power Plants,'' 
of title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations (10 CFR) to incorporate 
AP1000 Design Control Document (DCD), Revision 19, APP-GW-GL-702, dated 
June 13, 2011. On September 22, 2021 (86 FR 52593), the NRC published a 
direct final rule extending the duration of the AP1000 design 
certification by five years, with an effective date of December 6, 
2021.
    On July 2, 2025 (90 FR 28869), the NRC amended its regulations to 
replace the 15-year duration of design certifications with a 40-year 
duration, both for existing design certifications currently in effect 
and generically for

[[Page 65093]]

future design certifications, including renewals. The NRC did not 
change the date of issuance or renewal for existing design 
certifications but changed their expiration dates based on the most 
recent date of issuance or renewal. The current AP1000 design 
certification expires on February 27, 2046.
    On February 10, 2012, the NRC issued combined licenses (COLs) to 
Southern Nuclear Operating Company (SNC) for the Vogtle Electric 
Generating Plant (VEGP) Units 3 and 4 (COLs NPF-91 and NPF-92). During 
the licensing, construction, and operation of VEGP Units 3 and 4, SNC 
submitted over 180 license amendment requests (LARs) to the NRC 
addressing design changes, construction-discovered issues, and 
operational refinements. The NRC reviewed and approved these LARs 
through safety evaluations for each approved amendment. In addition, 
SNC implemented numerous Tier 2 departures under the section VIII.B.5 
of appendix D to 10 CFR part 52 change process that did not require 
prior NRC approval. The VEGP licensing process also resolved the COL 
action items identified in DCD Revision 19 with site-specific design 
details, operational program descriptions, and as-built information. 
The VEGP Unit 3 entered commercial operation on July 31, 2023, and Unit 
4 did so on April 29, 2024.
    In SECY-26-0034, ``NRC Regulatory Pathways and Licensing Readiness 
for Near Term Large Light-Water Reactor Deployment,'' dated March 9, 
2026, the NRC staff informed the Commission, among other things, that 
it anticipated submission of the Westinghouse Electric Company LLC 
(Westinghouse) application to update and renew the AP1000 design 
certification. The NRC staff also informed the Commission of its plan 
to incorporate the as-built changes incorporated into VEGP Units 3 and 
4, previously approved safety findings, and lessons learned into a 
revised AP1000 design certification. The NRC's verification review 
determined that no new changes were incorporated into AP1000 DCD 
Revision 20 that require additional technical review, and that all 
changes incorporated into the AP1000 DCD Revision 20 were either 
previously reviewed and approved by the NRC or were incorporated using 
a process that does not require NRC approval. Therefore, the NRC is 
using the direct final rule process to update appendix D to 10 CFR part 
52 to include AP1000 DCD Revision 20. This renewal would improve 
regulatory clarity, avoid unnecessary re-review, and streamline future 
AP1000 licensing activities by providing applicants with an updated, 
standardized design basis. The NRC is amending appendix D to 10 CFR 
part 52 to include AP1000 DCD Revision 20. The NRC is also retaining 
the incorporation by reference of AP1000 DCD Revision 19 in appendix D 
to 10 CFR part 52 because several combined license holders were 
licensed to AP1000 Revision 19 and the incorporation of the AP1000 DCD 
Revision 19 in appendix D to 10 CFR part 52 remains part of the 
licensing basis for those combined license holders. Applicants could 
reference either revision in future applications.
    While 10 CFR 52.63(a) imposes restrictive limits on the types of 
changes that may be made while a design certification rule is in 
effect, 10 CFR 52.59(c) allows a design certification renewal applicant 
greater flexibility in seeking changes to the design certification. 
Thus, a design certification renewal application that includes 
amendments to a certified design is not required to address the 
criteria in 10 CFR 52.63, ``Finality of standard design 
certifications.'' However, if the amendment request entails such an 
extensive change to the design certification that an essentially new 
standard design is being proposed, 10 CFR 52.59(c) provides that an 
application for a design certification must be filed. The AP1000 
amendment request does not propose an essentially new standard design.
    The NRC is amending the design certification for the AP1000 
standard plant design to renew the AP1000 design certification for 40 
years from the effective date of this direct final rule. The NRC 
determined the submitted application for incorporation by reference of 
DCD Revision 20 into appendix D to 10 CFR part 52 is acceptable. 
Further, the Commission granted an exemption from 10 CFR 52.57(a) to 
allow renewal more than 36 months before expiration of the current 
design certification rule (91 FR 40596; July 2, 2026). The requirements 
of 10 CFR 52.55, ``Duration of certification,'' govern the duration of 
a design certification and the effect of timely renewal. The AP1000 
design certification rule does not expire until 2046.
    The NRC is amending section VIII.B.6 of appendix D to 10 CFR part 
52 to incorporate the alternative change process that was previously 
reviewed and approved for Vogtle Units 3 and 4 into appendix D to 10 
CFR part 52. The license amendments and exemptions included in AP1000 
DCD Revision 20 include a previously reviewed and approved change 
process for Tier 2* information. On December 21, 2017, SNC submitted 
LAR-17-037, and an associated exemption request, seeking changes to the 
Vogtle Units 3 and 4 COLs to add a license condition that would apply 
the change process for Tier 2 information for a proposed departure from 
Tier 2* information provided that specific criteria are not met. If one 
of the criteria were met for the proposed departure, then the proposed 
departure would continue to require prior NRC approval. On September 
20, 2018, the NRC granted the amendments and exemptions to Vogtle Units 
3 and 4 that effectively replaced the Tier 2* change process with an 
alternative process (reflected in a license condition) that applied the 
Tier 2 change process along with nine additional criteria to determine 
whether a license amendment was required. Inclusion of this change 
process in section VIII.B.6 of appendix D to 10 CFR part 52 promotes 
regulatory efficiency by eliminating the need for unnecessary licensing 
actions for applicants referencing either AP1000 DCD Revision 19 or 
AP1000 DCD Revision 20.

IV. Discussion

A. Final Safety Evaluation Report

    The final safety evaluation report for the renewed AP1000 standard 
design consists of (1) the original final safety evaluation report 
NUREG-1793 published in September 2004 and Supplement No. 1 published 
in December 2005, supporting the original certification of the AP1000 
design; (2) NUREG-1793 Supplement 2 published in September 2011, 
supporting an amendment to the AP1000 design certification; and (3) the 
final safety evaluation report published in August 2026 supporting this 
renewal of the AP1000 design certification. These final safety 
evaluation reports and supplements are available as indicated in 
section XVI, ``Availability of Documents,'' of this document.

B. AP1000 Design Certification Renewal Rule

    The discussion in section V, ``Section-by-Section Analysis,'' of 
this document describes the purpose and key aspects of each amended 
section of the AP1000 design certification renewal rule. The AP1000 
design certification renewal rule maintains the structure of the 
existing design certification rule and is consistent with the other 
design certification rules (i.e., appendices A through G to 10 CFR part 
52).

V. Section-by-Section Analysis

    The following paragraphs describe the specific changes in this 
direct final rule.

[[Page 65094]]

Appendix D to Part 52--Design Certification Rule for the AP1000 Design

Section III. Scope and Contents

    This direct final rule revises paragraph A to include DCD 
Revision 20. This revision is appropriate because DCD Revision 20 
includes all of the information necessary to incorporate by 
reference. This direct final rule retains DCD Revision 19 and its 
supplemental information document because several combined license 
holders were licensed to DCD Revision 19, and it is necessary to 
retain these documents in this rule. Future applicants may reference 
either DCD Revision 19 or DCD Revision 20, as specified in paragraph 
B. As used in appendix D, the term generic design control document 
refers to both revisions of the AP1000, unless otherwise specified.

Section IV. Additional Requirements and Restrictions

    This direct final rule makes conforming changes to the 
requirement in paragraph A.3 to provide sensitive unclassified non-
safeguards information and safeguards information in the plant-
specific DCD to reflect the certification of the AP1000 DCD Revision 
20.

Section V. Applicable Regulations

    This direct final rule makes conforming changes in paragraphs 
A.1 and A.2, and adds a new paragraph A.3 to show the regulations 
that are applicable to the generic AP1000 design, as amended, and 
updates language to reflect the certification of DCD Revision 20. 
This section retains the references to safety evaluations supporting 
prior design certifications and adds the safety evaluation 
supporting the renewal because portions of the AP1000 design were 
initially certified or amended under different regulations.

Section VI. Issue Resolution

    This direct final rule revises paragraph B.1 to reference the 
final safety evaluation report supporting this renewal and to 
reference the rulemaking record for this renewal. It also revises 
paragraph B.7 to include the NRC's final environmental assessment of 
the renewal of the AP1000 design certification. These changes are 
necessary to specify those matters resolved as a result of this 
design certification renewal.

Section VII. Duration of This Appendix

    This direct final rule revises the period of reference for this 
appendix D to 40 years after the effective date of this direct final 
rule. This change is necessary to indicate the renewed rule's 
duration. The NRC's regulations in 10 CFR 52.61 require a design 
certification renewal duration to be not less than 10 nor more than 
40 years.

Section VIII. Processes for Changes and Departures

    This direct final rule revises paragraph B.6 to incorporate the 
alternative change process that was previously reviewed and approved 
for Vogtle Units 3 and 4. This change is necessary because the NRC 
approved significant changes to the changes and departures process 
while licensing Vogtle Units 3 and 4. These processes are 
appropriate for future licensees referencing the renewed AP1000 
design certification.

VI. Regulatory Flexibility Certification

    Under the Regulatory Flexibility Act (5 U.S.C. 605(b)), the NRC 
certifies that this direct final rule does not have a significant 
economic impact on a substantial number of small entities. This direct 
final rule affects only the licensing and operation of large nuclear 
power plants. The companies that own these plants or would apply to own 
such a plant do not fall within the scope of the definition of ``small 
entities'' set forth in the Regulatory Flexibility Act or the size 
standards established by the NRC (10 CFR 2.810).

VII. Regulatory Analysis

    The NRC has not prepared a regulatory analysis for this direct 
final rule. The NRC prepares regulatory analyses for rulemakings that 
establish generic regulatory requirements applicable to all licensees. 
Design certifications are not generic rulemakings in the sense that 
design certifications do not establish standards or requirements with 
which all licensees must comply. Rather, design certifications are NRC 
approvals of specific nuclear power plant designs by rulemaking, which 
then may be voluntarily referenced by applicants for combined licenses 
or construction permits. For these reasons, the NRC concludes that 
preparation of a regulatory analysis is neither required nor 
appropriate.

VIII. Backfitting and Issue Finality

    The NRC has determined that this direct final rule does not 
constitute backfitting as defined in 10 CFR 50.109, ``Backfitting,'' 
and does not affect the issue finality of an approval under 10 CFR part 
52. This direct final rule renewing the AP1000 design certification 
does not constitute backfitting because it does not impose new or 
changed requirements on any previously issued licenses that incorporate 
by reference this design certification. Further, as discussed in 
section III of this document, the NRC does not need to address the 
finality criteria in 10 CFR 52.63.

X. National Environmental Policy Act: Environmental Assessment and 
Final Finding of No Significant Impact

A. Introduction

    The NRC is renewing the design certification for the AP1000 
standard plant design for 40 years from the effective date of this 
direct final rule. The NRC also is updating the design certification to 
reflect changes provided by Westinghouse, by letter dated March 27, 
2026. The NRC issues design certification rules as appendices to 10 CFR 
part 52. The purpose of this amendment is to renew the period that the 
AP1000 design certification is valid for referencing by an applicant 
and includes incorporation of VEGP Units 3 and 4 license amendment 
approvals under their respective COLs. Specifically, Revision 20 of the 
AP1000 DCD incorporates the issuance of Amendments 142 and 141 for VEGP 
Units 3 and 4 granting changes to the Tier 2* departure evaluation 
process with a 10 CFR 51.22(c)(9) categorical exclusion determination 
and is incorporated by reference into this NEPA evaluation. This rule 
amends Section VIII.B.6 of appendix D to 10 CFR part 52 to include 
changes to the Tier 2* departure evaluation process for the AP1000 
design. With the renewal approval, the AP1000 design certification rule 
would remain valid for referencing until 40 years after the effective 
date of this direct final rule.
    The NRC developed in section X, ``National Environmental Policy 
Act: Environmental Assessment and Final Finding of No Significant 
Impact,'' of this document containing this evaluation of the 
environmental impacts of the renewal request. The NRC has documented 
its finding of no significant impact in accordance with the 
requirements of 10 CFR 51.21, ``Criteria for and identification of 
licensing and regulatory actions requiring environmental assessments''; 
10 CFR 51.31, ``Determinations based on environmental assessment''; and 
the National Environmental Policy Act of 1969, as amended (NEPA). This 
evaluation also addresses the severe accident mitigation design 
alternatives (SAMDAs) that the NRC has previously evaluated for the 
Westinghouse AP1000 design in the environmental assessment (EA) dated 
September 1, 2021. The evaluation does not address the site-specific 
environmental impacts of constructing and operating any facility that 
references the AP1000 design certification at a particular site. The

[[Page 65095]]

SAMDA analysis completed as part of this direct final rule can be 
incorporated by reference into the environmental determination related 
to an application for siting, construction, or operation of a nuclear 
plant that references the AP1000 standard design. The NRC would 
evaluate those impacts as part of any application(s) for the siting, 
construction, or operation of such a facility.
    As discussed later in this section of this direct final rule, the 
NRC has determined that issuing the subject renewal does not constitute 
a major Federal action significantly affecting the quality of the human 
environment. This determination is based on the generic finding made in 
10 CFR 51.32(b)(2) that there is no significant environmental impact 
associated with this renewal of the AP1000 design certification. This 
renewal does not authorize the siting, construction, or operation of a 
facility referencing the AP1000 design; it only codifies an extension 
of the period an applicant can reference the AP1000 design in a COL 
application. Furthermore, because the certification and update of the 
design constitute a rule change rather than a physical action, it does 
not involve the commitment of any resources that have alternative uses. 
The 10 CFR 51.32(b)(2) generic finding of no significant impact is, 
essentially, the legal equivalent of a categorical exclusion (72 FR 
49427; August 28, 2007). Therefore, the NRC has not prepared an 
environmental impact statement (EIS) for this direct final rule.
    Under 10 CFR 51.30(d), an EA for an amendment, of which a renewal 
is, to a design certification is limited to the consideration of 
whether the design change that is the subject of the proposed amendment 
renders a SAMDA previously rejected in the earlier EA to become cost 
beneficial, or results in the identification of new SAMDAs, in which 
case, the costs and benefits of new SAMDAs and the bases for not 
incorporating new SAMDAs in the design certification must be addressed.
    This amendment renewal request brings the AP1000 design 
certification up to date. Revision 20 to the DCD includes construction 
and operating experience from current AP1000 nuclear power plants 
operating in the U.S. today (i.e., Vogtle Units 3 and 4) and will allow 
for consistency and standardization in future AP1000 plant 
applications. Although DCD Revision 20 does not affect the overall risk 
profile for the AP1000 design (i.e., Revision 20 to the AP1000 DCD does 
not change the risk insights and conclusions of the AP1000 design 
certification or COL probabilistic risk assessments), as discussed in 
this section of the direct final rule, the NRC updated its SAMDA 
evaluation and performed sensitivity analyses in section X.B.2 of this 
document to determine whether any SAMDAs previously rejected in the 
earlier AP1000 design certification EAs could become cost beneficial 
due to updated cost parameter information.

B. Severe Accident Mitigation Design Alternative Evaluation

    Consistent with the objectives of standardization and early 
resolution of design issues, the Commission decided to evaluate SAMDAs 
as part of the original design certification for the AP1000 design. In 
its ``Policy Statement on Severe Reactor Accidents Regarding Future 
Designs and Existing Plants'' (50 FR 32138; August 8, 1985), the 
Commission defined the term ``severe accident'' as an event that is 
``beyond the substantial coverage of design-basis events (DBEs),'' 
including events where there is substantial damage to the reactor core 
(whether or not there are serious offsite consequences). These DBEs are 
analyzed in accordance with NUREG-0800, ``Standard Review Plan for the 
Review of Safety Analysis Reports for Nuclear Power Plants: LWR 
Edition,'' and documented in several chapters of the AP1000 DCD, such 
as Chapters 2, 3, 15, and 19.
B.1 Westinghouse's DCD Revision 20 Assessments of SAMDAs
    In the transmittal letter for the AP1000 renewal, Westinghouse 
states that Appendix 1B of the AP1000 DCD Revision 19 fulfills this 
requirement and addresses the costs and benefits of 15 SAMDAs that are 
not included in the AP1000 plant design. Revision 19 Appendix 1B has 
been retained in AP1000 DCD Revision 20. Westinghouse continues to find 
that the previously considered SAMDAs, which were deemed to be non-cost 
beneficial, remain non-cost beneficial and that no new design 
alternatives were identified. Their evaluation concludes that the SAMDA 
review performed in AP1000 DCD Revision 19 with regard to the viability 
of the proposed SAMDAs remains valid and applicable to the AP1000 plant 
design presented in AP1000 DCD Revision 20.
B.2 NRC Evaluation
    The NRC conducted an audit of Appendix 1B as part of its DCD 
Revision 20 application review. During that review, the NRC found that 
Westinghouse did not evaluate the effect of applying current dollar 
values to the cost analysis in Appendix 1B of Revision 20 of the AP1000 
DCD. An environmental evaluation considers the present conditions at 
the time of the application submission and this would include cost 
evaluations related to SAMDAs. Several recent new reactor construction 
permit applications have used appropriate current cost values in their 
initial SAMDA evaluations. For example, these applicants applied the 
updated dollar-per-person-rem value in accordance with NUREG-1530, 
``Reassessment of NRC's Dollar per Person-Rem Conversion Factor 
Policy,'' Revision 1, issued February 2022. These new reactor 
applicants also applied current dollar inflation adjustments to 
undiscounted dollar parameter values in the cost formulas in Section 
5.7 as directed in Section 5.8 of NUREG/BR-0184, ``Regulatory Analysis 
Technical Evaluation Handbook,'' published in January 1997.
    The NRC uses the dollar-per-person-rem value to monetize the impact 
of a potential severe accident, which the agency then compares to the 
costs of a mitigation measure to determine if it is cost beneficial. 
Significantly, the agency published the revised dollar-per-person-rem 
values in 2022, after the agency reviewed Revision 19 of the AP1000 
DCD, but before Westinghouse submitted Revision 20. Thus, consideration 
of this new information could change the agency's determination on 
whether any of the proposed SAMDAs are cost beneficial.
    Similarly, Westinghouse did not provide a discussion on adjusting 
for inflation for undiscounted cost parameters as recommended in 
Section 5.8 of NUREG/BR-0184. Therefore, the NRC conducted a review of 
Appendix 1B to DCD Revision 20 to determine if the Westinghouse SAMDA 
conclusion is still valid when applying the noted updated monetary 
values.
    The NRC reviewed the information in Appendix 1B to Tier 2, Chapter 
1, of the AP1000 DCD, Revision 20, and in the EAs issued for the 
original AP1000 design certification rule in January 2006, for the 
amendment to the AP1000 design certification in January 2011, and for 
the amendment to extend the design certification in September 2021. Due 
to the intervening time between when Westinghouse originally developed 
Appendix 1B and the submission of Revision 20, the NRC examined, with 
respect to the guidance in Section 5.8 of NUREG/BR-0184 and Revision 1 
of NUREG-1530, whether the revised dollar values have the potential to 
change the cost-benefit conclusions for any of the SAMDAs. The NRC 
evaluated new cost information on updates to a dollar-per-person-rem 
value of $7,300

[[Page 65096]]

consistent with guidance in NUREG-1530, Revision 1, and inflated to 
2026 dollars the previously undiscounted cost parameters used in the 
maximum benefit valuation formulas of NUREG/BR-0184. Based on the 
updated maximum benefit valuation with sensitivity analyses, the NRC 
concluded that the new cost information would not change the 
conclusions reached in the EAs issued for the original, amended AP1000 
design certification rules and the renewal AP1000.
    For any future application referencing the AP1000 design 
certification, the inflation of cost parameters (e.g., the dollar-per-
person-rem value) and revised cost-benefit guidance should be 
considered in determining the subsequent AP1000 applicability of the 
SAMDA conclusions previously described. This clarification for future 
AP1000 applications is noted in paragraph VI.7 of appendix D of 10 CFR 
part 52.

C. Summary of the Environmental Impacts of the Direct Final Rule

    On the basis of 10 CFR 51.32(b) and the evaluation in section X.B 
of this document, the NRC concludes that the direct final rule will not 
have a significant effect on the quality of the human environment. 
Accordingly, the NRC is not required to prepare an EIS for this direct 
final rule.
    Since no physical changes would occur in the human environment, the 
agency action (rulemaking) would not affect any threatened or 
endangered species or historic properties. Accordingly, the NRC finds 
that the direct final rule would have no significant environmental 
impact.

D. Finding of No Significant Impact

    The NRC has prepared this evaluation to determine the environmental 
effects of the agency action (rulemaking). This rule is primarily 
administrative or procedural in nature and therefore would not have any 
physical environmental effect. As explained in sections X.A through C 
of this document, the NRC has determined that the rulemaking would not 
change the conclusions in Appendix 1B of Revision 20 to the AP1000 DCD 
and would result in no new or different environmental effects. 
Therefore, the NRC concludes that the rule's action would not have a 
significant effect on the quality of the human environment. Based on 
this conclusion, the NRC has determined there is no need to prepare an 
environmental impact statement. Accordingly, the NRC finds the direct 
final rule would have no significant environmental impact. This 
environmental assessment and finding of no significant impact can be 
tracked with identification number NEPA ID EAXX-429-00-000-1784080234.

XIV. Compatibility of Agreement State Regulations

    Under the ``Agreement State Program Policy Statement'' approved by 
the Commission on October 2, 2017, and published in the Federal

compatibility ``NRC.'' Category NRC consists of program elements over 
which the NRC cannot discontinue its regulatory authority pursuant to 
the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (AEA), as amended, or provisions of 10 
CFR. Under the Policy Statement, a program element means any component 
or function of a radiation control regulatory program, including 
regulations and other legally binding requirements imposed on regulated 
persons, which contributes to the implementation of that program. The 
NRC maintains regulatory authority over program elements classified as 
category NRC and the Agreement States must not adopt these NRC program 
elements. However, an Agreement State may inform its licensees of these 
NRC requirements through a mechanism under the State's administrative 
procedure laws, as long as the State adopts these provisions solely for 
the purposes of notification, and does not

XV. Voluntary Consensus Standards

    The National Technology Transfer and Advancement Act of 1995, 
Public Law 104-113, requires that Federal agencies use technical 
standards that are developed or adopted by voluntary consensus 
standards bodies unless the use of such a standard is inconsistent with 
applicable law or otherwise impractical. Design certifications are not 
generic rulemakings establishing a generally applicable standard with 
which all 10 CFR parts 50 and 52 nuclear power plant licensees must 
comply. Design certifications are Commission approvals of specific 
nuclear power plant designs by rulemaking. Furthermore, design 
certifications are initiated by an applicant for rulemaking, rather 
than by the NRC. This action does not constitute the establishment of a 
standard that contains generally applicable requirements.

VII. Duration of This Appendix

    This appendix may be referenced for a period of 40 years from 
December 28, 2026, except as provided for in 10 CFR 52.55(b) and 
52.57(b). This appendix remains valid for an applicant or licensee 
who references this appendix until the application is withdrawn or 
the license expires, including any period of extended operation 
under a renewed license.

VIII. Processes for Changes and Departures

* * * * *
    B. * * *
    6. a. An applicant who references the AP1000 design 
certification rule may depart from Tier 2 * information, which is 
designated with italicized text or brackets and an asterisk in the 
AP1000 design control document, without NRC approval, except for 
departures that:
    (1) Involve a deviation from a code or standard credited in the 
plant-specific DCD for establishing the criteria for the design or 
construction of a structure, system, or component (SSC) important to 
safety;
    (2) Result in a change to a design process described in the 
plant-specific DCD that is material to implementation of an industry 
standard or endorsed regulatory guidance;
    (3) (i) Result in a change to the fuel criteria evaluation 
process, the fuel principal design requirements, or the nuclear 
design of the fuel or the reactivity control system that is material 
to a fuel or reactivity control system design function; or
    (ii) Result in any change to the maximum fuel rod average burn-
up limits or the small break LOCA analysis information in DCD 
Subsections 15.6.5.4B.2.2 or 15.6.5.4B.2.3;
    (4) Adversely affect the containment debris limits or debris 
screen design criteria;
    (5) Change the Reactor Coolant Pump (RCP) type from a canned 
motor to a different type of RCP;
    (6) Result in a change to the Passive Residual Heat Removal Heat 
Exchanger natural circulation test (first plant test), the Core 
Makeup Tank Heated Recirculation Tests (first three plants test), or 
the Automatic Depressurization System Blowdown Test (first three 
plants test) that is material to the test objectives or test 
performance criteria;
    (7) Involve structural materials or analytical or design 
methods, including design codes and analytical assumptions, that 
deviate from those credited in the plant-specific DCD for critical 
sections;
    (8) Result in a change to the design of the steel faceplates, 
internal trusses, tie bars, or headed studs of the steel-concrete 
(SC) module walls in the Nuclear Island or the Shield Building, 
including SC-to-reinforced concrete (RC) connections; or
    (9) Result in an increase in the demand to capacity (D/C) ratio 
of a critical section of the structure. The licensee must determine 
the D/C ratio under this condition for each critical section 
structural member including, but not limited to, wall segments, wall 
sections, concrete panels, slabs, or basemat sections, affected by a 
departure by:
    (i) Using the Tier 2 * information in the DCD Section 3.8 or 
Appendix 3H table that directly states the D/C ratio or states the 
area of steel provided and the area of steel required for the 
affected structural member; or
    (ii) Providing the same total area of steel across the entire 
critical section using any combination of rebar sizes and spacing 
allowed by the design basis codes used in the DCD as the total area 
of steel specified in DCD Section 3.8 and Appendix 3H tables marked 
Tier 2 *.
    b. For a departure from Tier 2 * information that does not 
require prior NRC approval, the licensee must comply with the 
requirements for Tier 2 departures in paragraph B.5 of this section. 
The departure or change to Tier 2 * information must remain Tier 2 * 
information in the plant-specific DCD.
    c. Departures from Tier 2 * information that are made under 
paragraph B.6 of this section do not require an exemption from this 
appendix.
