SUMMARY: The Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is taking interim 
final action to extend the reporting deadline under the Greenhouse Gas 
Reporting Rule for reporting year 2025 data from October 30, 2026, to 
March 10, 2027. This final action only changes the reporting deadline 
for annual greenhouse gas reports for reporting year 2025 and makes no 
other changes to the rule. The EPA is requesting comments on all 
aspects of this interim final rule.

I. General Information

A. Executive Summary

    The Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program (GHGRP, codified at 40 CFR 
part 98) applies to certain industrial facilities that emit greenhouse 
gases (GHG) (primarily facilities emitting at least 25,000 metric tons 
of carbon dioxide equivalents (MTCO<INF>2</INF>e)), upstream suppliers 
of fossil fuels and industrial GHGs (such as carbon dioxide 
(CO<INF>2</INF>) and hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)), and industries that 
capture and sequester CO<INF>2</INF> as a means of reducing 
CO<INF>2</INF> emissions. Under the GHGRP, approximately 8,200 
facilities, suppliers, and CO<INF>2</INF> injection sites submit data 
each year. Facilities calculate emissions using methodologies that are 
specified under the regulations and report data to the EPA using the 
electronic Greenhouse Gas Reporting Tool (e-GGRT). Annual reports 
covering emissions from the prior calendar year are due to the EPA by 
March 31st of each year. Once data are submitted, the EPA conducts a 
multi-step verification process to ensure reported data are accurate, 
complete, and consistent. If potential errors are identified, the EPA 
will notify the reporter, who can resolve the issue either by providing 
an acceptable response describing why the flagged issue is not an error 
or by correcting the flagged issue and resubmitting their annual GHG 
report.
    On September 16, 2025, the EPA issued a proposed rule that 
reconsidered the GHGRP (90 FR 44591, here in after referred to as the 
``GHGRP Reconsideration Proposal''). Among other things, the EPA 
proposed that no industries would need to submit annual GHG reports for 
calendar year 2025. The EPA also proposed to extend the deadline for 
submitting the calendar year 2025 report (i.e., the reporting year 2025 
reporting deadline) from March 31, 2026, to June 10, 2026. The EPA 
subsequently received over 50,000 comments on the GHGRP Reconsideration 
Proposal. On February 27, 2026, the EPA issued a final rule that 
extended the reporting deadline (hereinafter ``deadline'') for the 
GHGRP for reporting year 2025 from March 31, 2026, to October 30, 2026 
(91 FR 9712). In this action, the EPA is further extending the deadline 
for reporting year 2025 to March 10, 2027, to allow the EPA additional 
time to consider comments on the GHGRP Reconsideration Proposal and to 
take any final action before the further extended reporting year 2025 
deadline.

emitters or suppliers of GHGs or that sequester CO<INF>2</INF>

II. Background

A. What is the statutory authority for this action?

    The EPA is extending the regulatory deadline of the GHGRP for 
reporting year 2025, which was promulgated pursuant to Clean Air Act 
(CAA) section 114 and under the authority provided in CAA section 136 
for certain segments of the petroleum and natural gas source category 
(subpart W).
    The EPA's authority for the rulemaking procedures followed in this 
interim final rule is provided by Administrative Procedure Act (APA) 
section 553(b)(B), 5 U.S.C. 553(b)(B), which allows an agency to forego 
prior notice and comment ``when the agency for good cause finds (and 
incorporates the finding and a brief statement of reasons therefore in 
the rule issued) that notice and public procedure thereon are 
impracticable, unnecessary, or contrary to the public interest.'' This 
interim final rule is being issued without prior notice or opportunity 
for public comment because the EPA finds that prior notice and comment 
would be impracticable and contrary to the public interest under the 
current circumstances.
    As discussed in section II.B of this preamble, this interim final 
rule amends the GHGRP to extend the reporting year 2025 deadline from 
October 30, 2026, to March 10, 2027, to allow the EPA additional time 
to consider comments on the GHGRP Reconsideration Proposal and to take 
any final action on that proposal. The EPA finds that it is 
impracticable and contrary to the public interest for the Agency to 
provide notice and comment prior to issuing this interim final rule 
because there is not adequate time for the notice-and-comment process 
prior to the October 30, 2026 deadline that this interim final rule 
extends.
    Additionally, in a typical reporting year the Agency allows six 
weeks for reporters to work within the e-GGRT system to prepare and 
submit reports. Should the EPA later take final action on the GHGRP 
Reconsideration Proposal by retaining all or many existing reporting 
obligations, this revised deadline would allow regulated entities 
sufficient time to react to that final decision and work in the e-GGRT 
system to prepare and submit reports. Conversely, should the EPA later 
take final action on the GHGRP Reconsideration Proposal by finalizing 
all or most of the proposed rescissions and revisions, this revised 
deadline would allow regulated entities sufficient time to adjust their 
compliance efforts and avoid wasted time and effort.
    As previously stated, the EPA notes that extending the reporting 
deadline will also allow the Agency time if needed to prepare and 
release the e-GGRT system in advance of the revised

RY2025 reporting deadline. In addition, the EPA notes that the GHGRP 
requires facilities to submit reports electronically in a format 
specified by the Administrator.\1\ The EPA developed the e-GGRT for 
facilities to use to prepare and submit reports in accordance with this 
requirement. The e-GGRT system performs thousands of internal 
electronic validation and consistency checks, and appropriately 
safeguards information, including controlled unclassified information 
such as protects sensitive business information/confidential business 
information (SBI/CBI). Typically, the EPA would release e-GGRT six 
weeks in advance of the deadline so that facilities would have ample 
time to prepare and electronically submit reports prior to the 
deadline. As there is not adequate time for the EPA to have the 
electronic reporting tool prepared in time for the October 30, 2026, 
reporting year 2025 deadline that this rule extends, this also supports 
EPA's finding that it is impracticable and contrary to the public 
interest for the Agency to provide notice and comment prior to issuing 
this interim final rule.

    Furthermore, this action serves the public benefit by reducing risk 
of harm to regulated entities from inadvertently or hastily reporting 
erroneous or unnecessary information that could later be made public. 
This extension also ensures that entities subject to the GHGRP could 
benefit from any burden reduction revisions that the EPA may make for 
the 2025 reporting year in any separate final action following the 
GHGRP Reconsideration Proposal and/or have adequate time to prepare and 
submit reporting year 2025 reports.
    This final rule is effective immediately upon publication. Section 
553(d)(1) of the Administrative Procedure Act, 5 U.S.C. 553(d)(1), 
provides that final rules shall not become effective until 30 days 
after publication in the Federal Register ``except . . . a substantive 
rule which grants or recognizes an exemption or relieves a 
restriction.'' The purpose of this provision is to ``give affected 
parties a reasonable time to adjust their behavior before the final 
rule takes effect.'' Omnipoint Corp. v. Fed. Commc'n Comm'n, 78 F.3d 
620, 630 (D.C. Cir. 1996); see also United States v. Gavrilovic, 551 
F.2d 1099, 1104 (8th Cir. 1977). However, when the agency grants or 
recognizes an exemption or relieves a restriction, affected parties do 
not need a reasonable time to adjust because the effect is not adverse. 
Absent this action, regulated entities would have insufficient time to 
prepare and submit annual reports in the e-GGRT system, and by 
extending the deadline, this action relieves that restriction. 
Therefore, the normal 30-day minimum period between this action's dates 
of publication and effectiveness is not required. Accordingly, the EPA 
is making this rule effective immediately upon publication.

B. Description of Final Regulatory Amendments and Rationale

    In this final action, the EPA is amending subpart A (General 
Provisions) at 40 CFR 98.3(b)(6) to extend the reporting year 2025 
deadline to March 10, 2027. As explained in the GHGRP Reconsideration 
Proposal, the deadline is set in the regulation, and the only way that 
the EPA can alter the deadline for any year is through a regulatory 
amendment. This extension only applies to the reporting year 2025 
deadline. This action will further extend the reporting year 2025 
deadline to allow the EPA additional time to consider comments on the 
GHGRP Reconsideration Proposal and to take any final action before the 
further extended reporting year 2025 deadline of March 10, 2027. This 
extension ensures that entities subject to the GHGRP could benefit from 
any burden reduction revisions that the EPA may make for the 2025 
reporting year in any separate final actions following the GHGRP 
Reconsideration Proposal and/or have adequate time to prepare and 
submit reporting year 2025 reports. Additionally, at this point the EPA 
cannot have the electronic reporting tool prepared in time for the 
October 30, 2026, reporting year 2025 deadline, consistent with the 
GHGRP requirements that facilities submit reports electronically in a 
format specified by the Administrator.\2\

III. Request for Comment

    As explained in section II.A of this preamble, the EPA finds good 
cause to issue this interim final rule without prior notice or 
opportunity for public comment. However, the EPA is providing an 
opportunity for the public to comment on the EPA's action to extend the 
deadline under the GHGRP for reporting year 2025 data from October 30, 
2026, to March 10, 2027. The EPA intends to review comments received 
and consider whether this action should be revised, if appropriate, in 
response to comments received. If the EPA takes additional action on 
this interim final rule, as described in Section IV.L of this preamble, 
the Administrator has determined that subsequent final action will be 
subject to the requirements of CAA section 307(d), pursuant to section 
307(d)(1)(V).

B. Executive Order 14192: Unleashing Prosperity Through Deregulation

    This action is considered an Executive Order 14192 deregulatory

    This action does not impose any new information collection burden 
under the PRA. OMB has previously approved the information collection

submitting annual GHG reports for reporting year 2025 data from October 
30, 2026, to March 10, 2027.

D. Regulatory Flexibility Act (RFA)

    I certify that this final action will not have a significant 
economic impact on a substantial number of small entities under the 
RFA. In making this determination, the EPA concludes that the impact of 
concern for this rule is any significant adverse economic impact on 
small entities and that the agency is certifying that this rule will 
not have a significant economic impact on a substantial number of small 
entities because the rule relieves regulatory burden on the small 
entities subject to the rule. This action relieves regulatory burden, 
as it extends the reporting year 2025 deadline and provides entities 
more time to prepare annual GHG reports for that reporting year. We 
have therefore concluded that this action will relieve regulatory 
burden for all directly regulated small entities.

E. Unfunded Mandates Reform Act (UMRA)

    This action does not contain an unfunded mandate as described in 
UMRA, 2 U.S.C. 1531-1538 and does not significantly or uniquely affect 
small governments. This action imposes no enforceable duty on any

    This action does not have federalism implications. It will not have

30, 2026, to March 10, 2027. Thus, Executive Order 13175 does not apply 
to this action.

H. Executive Order 13045: Protection of Children From Environmental 
Health Risks and Safety Risks

    The EPA interprets Executive Order 13045 as applying only to those 
regulatory actions that concern environmental health or safety risks 
that the EPA has reason to believe may disproportionately affect 
children, per the definition of ``covered regulatory action'' in 
section 2-202 of the Executive Order. This action solely extends the 
reporting year 2025 deadline.
    Therefore, this action is not subject to Executive Order 13045, 
because it does not concern an environmental health risk or safety 
risk. Since this action does not concern human health, EPA's Policy on 
Children's Health also does not apply.

Supply, Distribution, or Use

    This action is not a ``significant energy action'' because it is 
not likely to have a significant adverse effect on the supply, 
distribution or use of energy. This action solely moves a reporting 
deadline from October 30, 2026, to March 10, 2027, and makes no changes 
that affect the supply, distribution, or use of energy.

    Under CAA section 307(b)(1), any petition for review of this final 
rule must be filed in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of 
Columbia Circuit by December 14, 2026. This rulemaking revises the

year 2025, which applies to owners and operators of facilities and 
suppliers for all source categories of 40 CFR part 98. These facilities

civil or criminal proceedings brought by the EPA to enforce the 
requirements.
