WHEREAS the War Department has  
commenced the construction of canton­
ments, air fields, fortifications, and other  
public works in the Territory of Hawaii  
which are necessary for the national  
defense; and
WHEREAS the interests of the na­
tional defense require the completion o|  
such public works at the earliest practic­
able date; and
WHEREAS by section 1  of the act of  
August 1, 1892, 27 Stat. 340, as amended  
by the act of March 3, 1913, 37 Stat. 726  
(U.S.C., title 40, section 321), the service  
of all laborers and mechanics employed  
by the Government upon any public  
work of the United States is limited to  
eight hours in any one day except in case  
of extraordinary emergency; and  
WHEREAS it appears that unless such  
limitation is suspended as to laborers  
and mechanics employed by the War  
Department in the construction of the  
above-mentioned public works in the  
Territory of Hawaii, it will be impossible,  
because of the remoteness of such places  
from sources of labor supply in the  
United States, and because of the dif­
ficulties of transporting additional labor  
from the United States, to accomplish 
the work necessary to the completion of  
such public works within the time re­
quired by the interests of the national  
defense; and
WHEREAS I find that by reason of the  
foregoing an extraordinary emergency  
exists;
NOW, THEREFORE, by virtue of the  
authority vested in me by section 1 of  
the said act of August 1, 1892, as  
amended by the said act of March 3,  
1913, and as President of the United  
States, I hereby suspend for the duration 
of the emergencies proclaimed by me on  
September 8, 1939,1  and May 27, 1941,1 
the above-mentioned provisions- of law 
prohibiting more than eight hours of  
labor in any one day of laborers and me­
chanics employed by the Government of  
the United States as to all work per­
formed by laborers and mechanics em­
ployed by the War Department in the  
construction of cantonments, air fields,
