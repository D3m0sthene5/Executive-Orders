118 TWENTY-FOURTH REPORT OF CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION. March 15, 1907. Dr. A. Brown may be appointed in the Department of Agriculture without examination on account of his special ability along the line of work in execu- tion of the pure-food and drugs law. This order was signed by the President upon the request of Secretary Wilson, of the Department of Agriculture. The reasons for Secretary Wilson's action are set forth in the following letter to the President : We are preparing to execute the pure-food and drugs law, and the Civil Serv- ice Commission, by examination, is securing for us the necessary officials. I would like to have Dr. A. Brown, of Letts, Iowa, appointed by you, on account of his special ability along the lines of work required in the execution of that law. He belongs to a large class of men of special ability who are reluctant to take examinations. I am in entire accord with the Civil Service Commission and fully approve of their supervision over entrance to the Government service. But there are exceptions to this rule, as there are to most others, and after ten years' experience in the Government service I think the President might make occasional exceptions where they will result in giving the Government the services of high-class officials who can not be obtained in any other way. I would not, however, regard it as safe to give this power to any other officer of the Government. I may add that there have been a good many men brought into the service of this Department without examination on account of their experience and pecul- iar ability in certain lines. The Department has been growing rapidly, and it was necessary quite often to do this in order that we might get men qualified to carry on our work. The tendency has been to limit the bringing in of such exceptional men, and I have been more and more turning to the Civil Service Commission to find me the officials I require. My mind is to pursue that course In the future. But there are and will be occasions where men peculiarly fitted for certain kinds of work should be put in by you. I think it would probably strengthen the service if several were put in for this new line of work, but this one is all that I shall ask. March 15, 1907. Mr. IL H.
