TWENTY-FOURTH REPORT OP CIVIL SEBVICR COMMISSION. 181 October 30, 1907. Charles H. McLellan, captain in the U. S. Reverine-Cutter Service, retired, was for a number of years during his active service detailed to the Life-Saving Service, and since his retirement has been employed for a number of temporary periods, with the consent of the Civil Service Commission, in superintending the construction of self-righting and self-bailing lifeboats and other life-saving apparatus, in all of which he is an expert, and it being necessary in the best interest of the service to continue Captain McLellan in this position, his appoint- ment is hereby authorized without compliance with the civil-service rules for such period as his services may be required, the rate of compensation to be fixed by the Secretary of the Treasury. November 9, 1907. Joseph B. Dalton may be appointed to the position of skilled laborer in the Bureau of the Census without examination under the civil-service rules. Mr. Dalton had previously served under a temporary appointment in the Bureau of the Census, and his service was most satisfactory. He is a member of the District of Columbia National Guard (Naval Militia Branch), and while on duty in that service had been instrumental in saving human life endangered by the Potomac River and had won promotion by merit and the commendation of his superiors. Because of these reasons showing Mr. Dalton's great value to the Naval Militia of the District of Columbia National Guard, and the probability of his severing his connection therewith to seek employment elsewhere, the above order was issued so as to permit his employment in the Government service. December 13, 1907. Mr. William McCambridge, of Illinois, may be transferred without examina- tion from the excepted to the competitive class in the Interstate Commerce Commission. Hon. Martin A. Knapp, chairman of the Interstate Commerce Commission, under date of December 10, 1907, wrote the President as follows : There has arisen in this office a complication in respect of the employment of certain persons which inclines me to ask your indulgence by way of an Executive order, if you are of opinion that the facts warrant such action on your part. Mr. William McCambridge, of Illinois, came into the service of the Commis- sion as the confidential clerk or private secretary of Governor Flfer, and acted in that capacity until the governor resigned from the Commission. The position of confidential clerk is exempt from the civil-service rules and regulations re- specting examinations. When Governor Fifer resigned the work of the Com- mission was becoming very heavy, and it was highly desirable to retain in the service a person of Mr. McCambridge's qualifications. Never having had a confidential clerk of my own, Mr. McCambridge was continued in the service nominally as my confidential clerk, although he never performed the duties of that position. Since the amendments to the act of June 29, 190G, the work of the Commission lias increased to such an extent and the demands upon my time have been so great that I have found it necessary to have some person to act as my confi- dential clerk, and a young man in the employ of the Commission, who was selected from the Civil Service Commission register several years ago, has been performing that service for me, but without the title or amount of compensa- tion which is customarily allowed for that class of employment. My present desire is to secure the transfer of. Mr. McCambridge from the excepted position he now occupies to the classified service without examination, and thus open the way for the appointment of an assistant whose time shall be at my disposal. If the class of work done by Mr. McCambridge could satis-' factorily be performed by some person certified by the Civil Service Commis-' slon, I would not bring the matter to your attention.
