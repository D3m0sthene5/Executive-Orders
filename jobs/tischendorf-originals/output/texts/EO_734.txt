Stickney as a clerk examiner in the Bureau of Chemistry, Department of Agriculture, which will expire by limitation on December 31, 1907, may be extended for such periods as may be necessary to make an appointment from the editorial clerk examination, which is to be held on January 8 and 9, 1908. Miss Stickney was temporarily employed as a clerk examiner at $75 per month in the Bureau of Chemistry, and the period of six months, for which temporary appointment may be authorized, would expire on December 31, 1907. There were no eligibles on the register having the qualifications desired by the Depart- ment and possessed by Miss Stickney, but an examination had been announced to be held on January 8 and 9, 1908. The Chief of the Bureau of Chemistry urged the continuation of the temporary appointment of Miss Stickney, for the reason that she had been engaged in the preparation of special reports on food preservation and their effect on digestion and health, the work being highly technical, and its completion with the greatest amount of speed compatible with accuracy was necessary in order to submit the report to Congress during the month of January. January 16, 1908. Mr. Ernest Coldwell, late collector of internal revenue for the fifth district of Tennessee, who became separated from said office and from the service by the consolidation of the two internal-revenue districts in Tennessee into one dis- trict, may, for the good of the service, be appointed a revenue agent in the Inter- nal-Kevenue Service without compliance with the civil-service rules. January 20, 1908. Mr. James S. Wickham may be employed in the Bureau of the Census upon experimental work in developing tabulating machinery, as authorized by the act of February 26, 1907, appropriating for executive, legislative, and judicial expenses, without examination under the civil-service rules. This order was issued upon the recommendation of the Commission in view of the letter from the Director of the Census, stating that the work to which Mr. Wickham was to be assigned could not be accomplished by the regular force of the office ; that he was a machinist and electrician, and had had seven years' experience in developing electrical devices; that the need for his services grew out of the fact that it has been decided to manufacture the tabulating apparatus in the Bureau's own shop rather than to have the machines made by outside parties, which necessitated the employment of a person having not only con- structive but inventive ability, both of which were possessed by Mr. Wickham.
