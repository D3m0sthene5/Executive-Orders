Mrs. Enna Wilson Noel may be reinstated to a position in the classified service in the War Department without regard to the requirements of the civil service rules. Mrs. Noelis the widow of Edward McC. Noel, whose death on April 16, 1915, termi- nated a service of nearly 33 years in the office of the Surgeon General, and leaves her dependent upon employment for support. Prior to her marriage she served as clerk in the office of the Surgeon General from December 18, 1893, to February 9, 1900, and previously in other offices since November 7, 1889. Her reinstatement is recom- mended by the Secretary of War and the Surgeon General of the Army. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation. May 11, 1915. Mrs. Laura F. Richmond may be reinstated as an operative in the Bureau of Engraving and Printing without regard to the length of time she has been separated from the service. This order is issued on the recommendation of the Acting Secretary of the Treasury in view of the efficient service rendered by the husbanaof Mrs. Richmond in the Treasury Department for 28 years, and in view of the fact that Mr. Richmond is now wholly incapacitated both mentally and phyeically for the performance of duty," and also because of the satisfactory service rendered by Mrs. Richmond while a former employee in the Bureau of Engraving and Printing where she served for a period of seven years. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation. May 13, 1915. Michael L. Eidsness, jr., of Iowa, a minor clerk of class D ($900) in the Post Office Department, may be promoted to the position of clerk without further civil service examination. This order was issued upon the recommendation of the Postmaster General, who stated that while Mr. Eidsness was not able to pass the first-grade examination he had a very exceptional record in the Post Office Department and was engaged on clerical work of high order; accomplished without apparent effort 50 per cent more work than any of the clerks with whom he was associated. Mr. Eidsness made three attempts to pass the first-grade examination, but on account of lack of early education was unable to pass. May 14, 1915. Mr. James J. Harley may be reinstated to a position in the post office at Philadel- phia, Pa., without regard to the length of time he has been separated from the service. Mr. Harley was a clerk in the Philadelphia post office from June 1, 1893, to November 15^ 1900, when he resigned. His record during that time was entirely satisfactory. His reinstatement is recommended by the postmaster at Philadelphia, who states that he is personally acquainted with Mr. Harley and feels that he is mentally and physically capable of performing with satisfaction and efficiency the duties of a postal clerk. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation. May 14, 1915. Mr. Bernard R. Jett may be transferred in the Office of the Secretary of the Interior from wireman on the per diem roll to electrician at $1,000 per annum. Mr. Jett is employed on the "Repair of Buildings" roll in a position for many years not re§:arded by the department as classified. He was .one of a small number whose retention and reemployment without a competitive status were authorized by an executive order of June 17, 1909. He has been employed intermittently since Sep- tember 9, 1902, has passed the examination for electrician, and by reason of extensive and varied electrical work has become familiar with the complicated systems of light- ing, being one of the few persons who will be able to assist materially in the prepara- tion of long deferred electrical plans for the several buildings. The action proposed will also save several hundred dollars annually by permitting; the continuous employ- ment of skilled mechanics who must otherwise be laid off when their services can not well be spared.
