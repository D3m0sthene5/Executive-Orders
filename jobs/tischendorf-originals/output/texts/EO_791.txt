138 TWENTY-FOURTH BEPORT OF CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION. April 20, 1908. Mr. Alpheus H. Mahone may be reinstated as a fish culturist in the Bureau of Fisheries without reference to the time limit of eligibility prescribed by the civil-service rules. Mr. Mahone was appointed a fish culturist in the Bureau of Fisheries on August 6, 1902, and resigned on December 26, 1904, for the purpose of going to the Argentine Republic to assist that Government in establishing fish-cul- tural stations, having been suggested for this work by the Bureau of Fisheries* He was relieved from duty with the Argentine Republic on March 11, 1906, at his own request and in accordance with his contract The Commissioner of Fisheries stated that Mr. Mahone is a first-class fish culturist and recommended his appointment on account of the fact that it is very difficult to secure suffi- cient eligibles through examination. In view of these facts the Commission recommended the reinstatement of Mr. Mahone by Executive order. April 23, 1908. Mr. John Krey may be reinstated as an assistant engineer in the Engineer Department of the United States Army in the discretion of the Secretary of War and should the needs of the service so require, without reference to the time limit of the civil-service rules. Mr. Krey was employed from October 5, 1894, to August 2, 1901, with slight interruptions, resigning when his superior officer called another assistant engi- neer in consultation with respect to some work under the charge of Mr. Krey, who considered such action a reflection upon his ability and judgment and a breach of professional etiquette. Mr. Krey was reported to have been indus- trious and energetic and a practical and competent engineer, who accomplished results as long as he was in the Government service, and his experience has been wide and apparently successful since leaving it. The Chief of Engineers reported that there was no vacancy to which Mr. Krey could be assigned, but recommended some such action as would make his services available when a vacancy occurred, inasmuch as it would be an advantage to the service to gain a man who had been tested and found satisfactory. For these reasons the Com- mission submitted the above order to the President. May 1, 1908. The appointment of Mrs. Katherine J.
