120 THIRTY-SECOND REPORT V. S. CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION. December 1, 1914. Mrs. Onie Cummings maybe appointed as a clerk in the Department of Agriculture without reference to the civil-service rules at a salary of $900 per annum. This order is issued upon the recommendation of the Secretary of Agriculture, who states that Mrs. Cummings 's training and experience are such as to enable her to render good service. She is the widow of Mr. R. F. Cummings, formerly a clerk at a salary of $1,400 per annum in the Bureau of Soils, Department of Agriculture. Mr. Cummings was in the service from October 16, 1906, until February 1, 1914, the date of his death. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation of the Secre- tary of Agriculture. December 7, 1914. Miss Louisa W. Happersett may be appointed to a position in the classified civil service without reference to the civil-service rules. This order is issued upon the recommendation of the Secretary of War, who states that the Quartermaster General, the Surgeon General, and the Judge Advocate General of the Army unite in requesting the issuance of an Executive order. Miss Happersett is the daughter of the late John C. G. Happersett, major and surgeon, Umted States Army, who rendered highly efl&cient military service during a period of 32 years, including the 4 years of the Civil War. The Surgeon General and the Judge Advocate General call special attention to the services rendered by Maj. Happersett and his family during the yellow-fever epidemic at Fort Brown, Tex., in 1882. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in recommending the order. December 11, 1914. Mrs. Sallie G. Jones, who has been employed in the Indian Bureau of the Interior Department for a period of four years, may be promoted without examination under the civil-service rules. Mrs. Jones has been employed in the Interior Department in various capacities since May, 1900. Her record for efficient and faithful service is good. She was placed in the classified service imder an Executive order issued October 9, 1908, and appointed copyist at $720 per annum. In October, 1910, she was promoted to $900, and in April, 1914, in view of the meritorious service which she was rendering, the Commissioner of Indian Affairs promoted her to a clerical position at $1,000 per annum. However, owing to a ruling of the Civil Service Commission that under the provisions of the Executive order placing her in the classified service, she was not eligible for promotion to said grade without examination, she was, on August 16, 1914, reduced to $900 per annum. Mis. Jones is the widow of Robert Tyler Jones, a g^randson of President Tyler, who was bom in the White House diuing the administration of President Tyler, and who, it is stated, was the only male child ever bom in the White House. December 19, 1914. Mrs. John L. Evans may be appointed to the position of charwoman under the superintendent of the State, War, and Navy Departments building without regard to the labor relations governing appointments of laborers in Washington. Mrs. Evans is the wiaow of John L. Evans, who was discharged a corporal from Company H, Twenty-second United States Infantry, July 12, 1902, character excel- lent. From July, 1902, until January 24, 1914, he was employed in the navy yard in this city. On said date, while working as a machinist's helper at the navy ^ard, a hammer flew off the handle and stmck him on the head, from the effects of which he died June 18, 1914, after a long illness and much suffering, leaving a wife and two small children. The acting superintendent of the State, War, and Navy Departments building recommends that an Executive order be issued authorizing the appointment of Mrs. Evans. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation. - (It will be noted that this order waives the labor regulations, and not the civil- service rules.) December 30, 1914.
