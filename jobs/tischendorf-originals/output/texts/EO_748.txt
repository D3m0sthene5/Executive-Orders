February 4, 1908. Schedule A, Subdivision II, paragraphs 6 and 7 of the civil-service rules, are hereby revoked and a new paragraph, to be numbered 6, is inserted, to read as follows, the subsequent paragraphs to be renumbered accordingly : 6. One private secretary or confidential clerk to the superintendent, and one cashier in each mint or assay office. The positions of private secretary to the superintendent, chief clerk, cashier, weigh clerk, assistant melter and refiner, and assistant coiner in the mint service were, prior to the issuance of this order, excepted from examination under Schedule A. The former Director of the Mint and the present Director of the Mint recommended to the Secretary of the Treasury, who approved their recom- mendation, that all these positions, except that of private secretary to the super- intendent, should be put in the competitive class. The Commission held, how- ever, that the position of cashier should still remain excepted, but was in favor of taking the other positions out of the excepted class. March 17, 1908. Schedule A, Subdivision III, of positions excepted from examination under the civil-service rules, is hereby amended by the addition of the following new paragraph, to be numbered " 10 :" 10. Care takers of abandoned military reservations or of abandoned or unoccupied military posts, when the positions are filled by retired noncommis- sioned officers or enlisted men. Upon the evacuation of a military post by troops large quantities of military stores and supplies are left which must be guarded and cared for until they can properly be disposed of. It was found that the work could be better performed by men who had a knowledge of such stores secured through actual military service. It was considered advisable to select for such care taking retired noncommissioned officers or enlisted men, or those who had had creditable mili- tary service and were particularly fitted for the duties. SPECIAL AGENTS— I NTEBSTATE COMMERCE COMMISSION. April 21, 1908. The civil-service rules are hereby amended by adding to Schedule A the fol- lowing section : . X. — Interstate Commerce Commission. 1. Not exceeding 20 special agents under the division of prosecutions, sub- ject to such evidence of qualification as the Civil Service Commission may pre- scribe after consultation with the Interstate Commerce Commission : Provided, That should the Civil Service Commission at any time have reason to believe that the privilege afforded by this section is abused, it may revoke it After consultation by the members of the Civil Service Commission and the members of the Interstate Commerce Commission it was decided that the inter- ests of the service required the exception of special agents under the division of prosecution of the Interstate Commerce Commission from the requirements of competitive examination. The duties of special agents are confidential, requir- ing men not only with ability as detectives, but meu of tact, address, good judg- ment, and strict integrity.
