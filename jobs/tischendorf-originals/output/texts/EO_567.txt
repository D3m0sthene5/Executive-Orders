116 TWENTY-FOURTH REPORT OF CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION. February 20, 1907. Mr. James H. Adams, who was appointed in the classified service of the Treasury Departmeut through examination and served with slight interrup- tions from December 12, 1892, to February 23, 1904, may be reinstated in the classified service without regard to the provisions of the civil-service rules. As a result of this order, Mr. Adams was appointed as a clerk in the Isthmian Canal Service for duty in the Canal Zone at a salary of $100 per month. The Commission was not informed of the reasons for the promulgation of this order. February 20, 1907. Mr. Gilman B. Howe may be appointed as clerk in the Bureau of the Census without compliance with the civil-service rules. This order was submitted to the President upon the request of the Secretary to the President, who inclosed with his communication letters from Senator Crane in regard to Mr. Howe's case. These inclosures were returned with the draft of the order, which was submitted as requested. February 21, 1907. Mr. Frank O'Neill may be reinstated to the position of stock examiner in the Department of Agriculture without regard to the one-year limitation of civil- service Rule IX. Mr. O'Neill was employed from 1895 to 1905 as a stock examiner at the National Stock Yards, Chicago, 111. In November, 1905, he resigned because of ill health. In December, 1906, he made application for reinstatement, but was barred by the one-year limitation under the rule. His failure to apply for reinstatement during his period of eligibility was due to a misunderstanding In regard to the time limit. As he had been a faithful employee during the period he served and the Department again desired to utilize his services, an order permitting his reinstatement was signed by the President. February 21, 1907. Mr. John E. Kidwell, accountant (pay clerk), at $2,200 per annum, in the Isthmian Service, may be transferred to a classified clerical position in the United States. The Commission was not advised of the reasons for the promulgation of this order. March 4, 1907. Mrs. Lillie L. Wright, formerly a clerk in the Patent Office, may be reinstated without regard to the length of time she has been separated from the service. Mrs. Wright was appointed February 3, 1891, as a copyist in the Patent Office tit $720 per annum as a result of the copyist examination. On July 1, 1S98, she was promoted to $900 per annum, and on July 3, 1903, resigned from the service. The Secretary of the Interior and the chief clerk of the Patent Office both desired her reappointment. March 5, 1907. George W. Pitts may be appointed a clerk in the Treasury Department with- out compliance with the civil-service rules. Mr. Pitts entered the Government service in 1885 as an assistant messenger, and from that date until his resignation in July, 1904, faithfully served in the Treasury Department and in the Department of Commerce and Labor. On March 25, 1905, lie was reinstated in the Department of Commerce and Labor by Executive order, apjwinted a messenger in the Post-Office Department with- out reference to civil-service Rule X, section S, requiring a service of six monthe next preceding the transfer.
