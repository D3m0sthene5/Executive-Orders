February 13, 1907. Mrs. George H. Hildreth may be appointed to a clerical position in the Bureau of Pensions without examination uuder the civil-service rules. February 16, 1907. Mrs. George H. Hildreth may be appointed to a clerical position in the classi- fied service without examination under the civil-service rules. The Executive order of February 13, permitting Mrs. Hildreth's appoint- ment in the Bureau of Pensions is revoked. It appeared that Mrs. Hildreth's husband had been employed for some time in the executive civil service and had been incapacitated by an injury received while in the service. Mrs. Hildreth had been employed for about eight months in 1888 in the Money-Order Division of the Treasury Department as a substitute clerk at a time when substitute service was permitted in the Departments at Washington. She had recently served a thirty-day emergency appointment in the office of the Auditor for the Navy Department and had rendered satisfac- tory service. She is strong and able-bodied and it appeared that she performed the duties of a clerk in a satisfactory manner. An order was first made allow- ing her appointment in the Pension Office, but as a gradual reduction of force was going on in that office the order was amended permitting her employment anywhere in the classified service. February 19, 1907. Mr. W. H. Teepe, of Maryland, who is now serving as a tabulator in connec- tion with the weighing of mails in the Post-Office Department, may be appointed to a clerical position on the permanent roll of that Department. Mr. Teepe had been temporarily employed for a number of years under the practice of the Post-Office Department to employ such assistants as were neces- sary in connection with the periodical weighing of the mails. These appoint- ments were made without certification and were not reported to the Commission until the provision of the Executive order of November 29, 1904, which forbade the employment of a person, except in accordance with the provision of the civil-service rules, unless it had been clearly determined that an examination was not necessary, was brought to the Department's attention by a letter dated January 13, 1905, in regard to the appointment of unskilled laborers. The De- partment then requested authority to appoint Mr. Teepe temporarily on several occasions, as by reason of his experience and ability he had developed unusual skill In the tabulation of mail weighing. As the Commission objected to constant temporary appointments because of the experience gained while serving as a temi>orary not authorized by the rules, a request for Mr. Teepe's appointment through Executive order was submitted to the President by the Department.
