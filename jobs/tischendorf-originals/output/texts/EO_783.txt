TWENTY-FOUBTH KEPORT OF CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION. 137 April 13, 1908. Mr. Harry A. Strohm, of Indiana, a third-class postmaster, who has had nine years' experience, may be appointed as a special agent of the Division of Classi- fication in the Office of the Third Assistant Postmaster-General without exam- ination under the civil-service rules. The Third Assistant Postmaster-General, in recommending the issuance of this order, stated that Mr. Strohm had nine years' experience in a third-class office, where the postmaster has to do all kinds of postal business, and had had prior to that time experience as a newspaper man, and was personally known from an intimate acquaintanceship of about fifteen years to be a bright, clean, capable man. In view of these facts the Commission recommended the issuance of the above order. April 15, 1908. Mr. Henry C. Towers, an expert in the Bureau of Forestry, of the Department of Agriculture, may be appointed to the competitive position of expert and chief of the section of purchases in the same service without examination under the civil-service rules. Mr. Towers had served as expert and special agent in the Department of Agri- culture since July 1, 1901, and it was desired to promote him to the position of expert and chief. The Commission was without authority to approve this pro- motion for, although the position in which Mr. Towers was serving is an ex- cepted one, the position of expert and chief is not excepted. The duties of the position to which it was desired to promote Mr. Towers consisted in assisting the fiscal agent in receiving all moneys accruing under the act of June 1, 1905, such payments being received in the form of currency, postal and express money- orders, and drafts, and amounting, it is estimated, to over $2,000,000 for the pres- ent year. The Secretary of Agriculture reported that before Mr. Towers entered the Forest Service, he was for twenty-two years connected with the private and national banks in the city of Washington and that this training and experience, coupled with his connection with the Forest Service, and his high character for integrity qualify him in an unusual degree for the performance of highly resiH)iisible duties. The Commission recognized the exceedingly high character given to Mr. Towers and, considering the confidential duties of his new position and the large amount of funds which would pass through his hands, recom- mended the above order. April 17, 1908. Mr. William S. Murphy, of Mississippi, may be appointed as a Chinese in- spector in the Immigration Service without examination under the civil-service rules on account of his extensive experience in court practice and special quali- fications, it being intended to utilize his capabilities at present in a position where there is great need of an officer who has legal training of a practical character. The Secretary of Commerce and Labor stated that Mr. Murphy's appoint- ment would be in the interests of good administration, basing this statement upon the fact that he is a member of the bar of Texas, Arkansas, Louisiana, and Mississippi, having held the position of judge of the criminal court in the western district of Arkansas, and that he had had extensive experience in court practice and had special qualifications which would make him a very efficient officer in the Chinese branch of the Immigration Service, it being intended to utilize his capabilities at El Paso, Tex., where there was great need of an officer of legal training of a practical character. In view of these reasons, the Commission concurred in the recommendation of the Secretary of Commerce and Labor for the issuance of the above order. 45417—08 10
