Her appointment as post-office clerk required an Executive order, as the position is a competitive one. June 18, 1907. Mrs. Mary A. Greenstreet, now serving under temporary appointment in the War Department by certification from the Civil Service Commission, may be selected from the clerk register of eligibles for permanent appointment. Mrs. Greenstreet was temporarily appointed to the position of clerk in the War Department January 12, 1907, through certification. Her name was reached because of the temporary nature of the position, since in certifying for brief i>eriods of employment only the names of those eligibles who are residing in the vicinity of the place of employment are considered. Her name stood third on the female clerk register for the State of Indiana, but certifications for permanent appointment in the Departments at Washington are made in accord- ance with the provisions of the civil-service law in regard to the apportionment of appointments, and Indiana was the thirty-third State in the order of certi- fication under the apportionment. The application for the Executive order was made by the Paymaster-General and approved by the Acting Secretary of War. It was stated that Mrs. Greenstreet had performed her duties with marked intelligence and expedition, and her general intelligence outside of her assigned work indicated her natural adaptability for various clerical duties. June 24, 1907. Mr. John S. Drukker, formerly a temporary employee in the Bureau of En- graving and Printing, may be reinstated as a pressman in the Bureau without reference to the civil-service rules. Mr. Drukker was temporarily appointed to the position of pressman in the Bureau of Engraving and Printing in 1898, when the war revenue act made necessary a prompt increase in the force of pressmen in that office. On May 29, 1899, an Executive order was promulgated authorizing the permanent appointment of all persons who were serving under temporary appointment on that date, in the discretion of the proper appointing officer. Mr. Drukker was in the service on tiie date mentioned, but resigned two days later, May 31, 1899. The Department did not retain him under the authority given, and the records of the Commission did not show why he was not retained. It was stated by the Director of the Bureau of Engraving and Printing that Mr. Drukker had been informed that his services would be discontinued on and after June 30, 1S99, the statement being made prior to the time that the Executive order referred to was signed, and thinking that the position could not become permanent in nature, Mr. Drukker resigned. Had he remained hi the position but a few days longer he would have been permanently appointed under the classification order. In view of his efficient services during his previous employment it was deemed advisable to reappoint him by Executive order. July 4, 1907. Mr. Horace W.
