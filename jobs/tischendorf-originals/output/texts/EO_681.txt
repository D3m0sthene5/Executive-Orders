128 TWENTY-FOURTH REPORT OF CIVIL SERVICE COMMISSION. August 12, 1907. The vacancy in the position of chemist at $4,000 per annum in the Bureau of Standards, created by the resignation of Dr. William A. Noyes, may be filled without examination and certification under the civil-service rules. A letter written by the Director of the Bureau of Standards stated that In view of the particular importance of this position in the work of the Bureau, and the highly specialized character of the work, it was essential that Doctor Noyes's successor be a representative chemist of established reputation, a trained investigator of the highest grade, and an administrative officer of experience and ability well qualified to represent the Bureau among the chem- ical interests of the country, and that to insure the appointment of the best possible chemist to this place it was desirable that the available chemists be considered and consulted more freely than is practicable with a written examination. For these reasons the Commission recommended to the President the issuance of this order. August 14, 1907. Mr. W. Dawson Johnston, of Vermont, who has been employed In the Library of Congress for several years, may be transferred to the position of collector and compiler of statistics, for duty also as librarian, in the Bureau of Educa- tion, Department of the Interior, without reference to the civil-service rules. The Commissioner of Education desired to have appointed to this position a thoroughly competent librarian, traiued and expert in modern methods of library administration, who could accomplish the task of arranging and placing on a working basis a large and valuable collection of educational and statistical literature belonging to the Bureau, and Mr. Johnston was highly recommended by the librarian under whom he had served.
