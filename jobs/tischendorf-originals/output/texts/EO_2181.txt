Fred Gilmore, of the District of Columbia, and Robert Taylor, of the District of Columbia, unclassified laborers at 1660 per annum in the Post Office Department, may be appointed classified laborers at 1660 per annum in the same department witnout reference to civil-service rules. April 27, 1915. Mr. Charles W. G. Brett may be transferred from the position of mechanician at $1,200 per annum to a clerkship at the same salary, in the Bureau of the Census, Department of Commerce, without reference to the civil-service rules. Mr. Brett was appointed as mechanician on September 16, 1908. On March 19, 1912, he met with an accident, in the line of duty, which resulted in the loss of Ids left eye. His transfer to the clerical roll is recommended by the Director of the Census, who states that Mr. Brett's experience in connection with the various machines used in the bureau, his familiarity with the matter to be tabulated, and his general educa- tion are believed to be such as to fit him for clerical duties, tind that his services have always been satisfactory. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the reconmiendation. April 27, 1915. Mrs. Lillian B. S. Grant may be reinstated to a position in the classified service in the Post Office Department without regard to the length of time she has been sep- arated from the service. Mrs. Grant, as Mrs. Lillie B. Silcott, was appointed to the position of printer's assistant in the Bureau of Printing and Engraving on August 2, 1895, and was pro- moted to operative and through the several grades of that position. On July 1, 1902, she was transferred to the Post Office Department as clerk at |900, and was promoted to $1,000 July 1, 1905. She resigned May 9, 1912. The Postmaster General advises that it is stated that Mrs. Grant's husband is a permanent invalid, and that she is therefore compelled to provide a livelihood for both her husband and herself. In view of the circumstances and the fact that the records of the department show that her services were satisfactory and her duties faithfully dischajged, the Postmaster General states that no objection would be offered by the department to the reinstatement of Mrs. Grant by Executive order. Mr. William Leroy Fairbanks may be transferred from chief clerk of the American Embassy at London to a clerkship m the Department of Commerce. He has served since 1908, at first in the State Department, then in the American Legation at Peking, and since 1910 in his present position. The Secretary of Commerce, with the concurrence of the Civil Service Commission, recommends the issuance of this order in view of the special qualifications of Mr. Fairbanks for the work of the Bureau of Foreign and Domestic Commerce.
