November  15,  193S  THE    PANAMA    CANAL    RECORD  67 New  International  Code  of  Signals The  Panama  Canal  has  received  from  the  Hydrographic  Office  of  the  United States  Navy  Department  copies  of  the  American  edition  of  the  new  international code  of  signals  to  be  effective  January  1 ,  1934.  The  code  is  published  in  two  volumes, numbered  I  and  II,  H.  O.  No.  87  for  visual  and  sound  signaling  and  H.  O.  No.  88 for  radio  signaling,  respectively.  Both  volumes  are  on  sale  at  the  branch  hydro- graphic  ofifice  at  Cristobal,  at  $1.80  each. Revision  of  the  cede  was  initiated  by  Great  Britain  at  the  International  Radio- telegraph Conference  in  Washington  in  1927  and  the  compilation  was  completed  in December  1930.  According  to  the  prefaces  of  the  two  volumes  the  code  was  to  be prepared  in  seven  editorial  editions,  in  English,  French,  German,  Italian,  Japanese, Spanish,  and  one  Scandinavian  language,  and  the  chief  changes  can  be  summarized as  the  compilation  in  two  volumes;  increase  of  the  flags  by  the  addition  of  10  numeral pennants  and  3  substitute  (repeater)  flags;  amendment  of  the  system  of  Morse  signaling by  the  introduction  of  procedure  signals  and  bringing  it  into  line  with  radio  teleg- raphy, as  far  as  possible  and  practicable;  abandonment  of  the  use  of  distant  signals and  of  fixed  semaphore;  and  arrangement  that  the  signal  letters  of  ships  should  be  the same  as  their  radio  call  signs  and  consist  of  four  letters  which  indicate  definitely the  nationality  of  the  ship  concerned.  With  respect  to  the  radio  section,  other  main features  are  the  introduction  of  words  and  phrases  applicable  to  aircraft,  and  develop- ment of  an  extensive  medical  section  and  a  code  for  accelerating  the  granting  of pratique.  It  is  considered  that  the  medical  section  will  be  of  great  value  in  securing correct  medical  advice  in  case  of  illness  or  accident.  The  Panama  Canal  has  requi- sitioned a  stock  of  the  new  pennants  and  flags  and  it  is  anticipated  that  they  will arrive  on  the  Isthmus  in  December. Transfer  of  District  Court  to  Department  o!  Justice By  section  6  of  Executive  Order  No.  6166,  dated  June  10,  1933,  issued  by  virtue  of the  authority  vested  in  the  President  by  section  16  of  the  act  of  March  3,  1933, (Public,  No.  428,  47  Stat.  1517),  the  District  Court  of  the  United  States  for  the Panama  Canal  Zone  was  transferred  to  the  Department  of  Justice.  The  effective date  of  the  transfer  was  made  November  4,  1933,  by  Executive  Order  No.  6301, dated  September  30,  1933. Under  date  of  November  3,  1933,  the  President  issued  an  Executive  Order,  "Desig- nation of  Marshal  as  officer  to  disburse  funds  for  the  maintenance  and  operation  of District  Court  of  the  United  States  for  Panama  Canal  Zone,  etc.",  ordering  as  follows: 1.  Upon  the  transfer  from  the  War  Department  to  the  Department  of  Justice of  the  function  of  supervision  over  the  District  Court  of  the  United  States  for the  Panama  Canal  Zone,  the  said  court  and  its  personnel  shall  continue  to receive  the  perquisities,  privileges,  and  allowances  to  which  they  are  now  en- titled and  receiving,  and  the  War  Department  shall  continue  to  furnish,  as  here- tofore, to  the  court  and  to  its  personnel  all  necessary  {a)  office  quarters,  equip- ment, and  facilities,  {b)  library  facilities,  and  (c)  travel  facilities,  within  the Canal  Zone;  and  the  War  Department  shall  not  require  payment  from  the Department  of  Justice  for  such  quarters,  equipment,  and  facilities. 2.  The  Marshal  for  the  Panama  Canal  Zone  shall  disburse  all  funds  under  the control  of  the  Department  of  Justice  required  for  the  maintenance  and  operation of  the  said  court  and  shall  give  bond  for  the  faithful  performance  of  his  duties in  such  amount  as  may  be  fixed  by  the  Attorney  General. 3.  This  order  is  not  intended  to  supersede,  or  in  any  wise  affect,  section  4  of Executive  Order  No.  6166,  of  June  10,  1933,  insofar  as  said  section  may  become applicable  hereto  under  the  provisions  of  Executive  Order  No.  6224,  of  Jul}'  27, 1933. 4.  Except  as  provided  in  paragraph  numbered  (3),  all  prior  Executive  Orders are  hereby  revoked  insofar,  and  to  such  extent,  as  they  may  be  in  conflict  or inconsistent  with  this  order. The  current  publications of  The  Panama  Canal  of  interest  to  shipping  are  furnished  to  the  Consular  officers  and  tiled  tor  reference. It  is  not  desired  that  inquiries  of  a  general  nature  be  addressed  to  the  Consular  officers,  or  that  they be  burdened  with  requests  which  should  be  made  direct  to  The  Panama  Canal ;  but  ships'  operators  who may  not  be  sufficiently  advised  as  to  charges,  supplies,  facilities,  etc.,  at  the  Canal  will  often  save  time by  applying  to  the  nearest  American  Consul.
