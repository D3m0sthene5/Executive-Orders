Knight, at present a bank examiner, may be transferred from his position and appointed shipping commissioner at the port of San Francisco, Cal., another position under the Government service without examination and certification, under the civil-service rules. There Is now no register of eligibles for this position. Mr. Knight had been in the public service as a national bank examiner since 1898. The Department stated that he was familiar with the shipping business and maritime affairs, and that the best interests of the service would be pro- moted by his appointment. It also stated that the position of shipping commis- sioner at the port of San Francisco requires a man of firmness, tact, and judg- ment, and that the Department was not prepared to fill the place by the pro- motion of one of the subordinates in the office* la view of these circumstances the President issued the order. October 5, 1907. Mr. William Stimpel may be appointed disciplinarian at the Carlisle, Pa., In- dian School upon noncompetitive examination showing him to be qualified by experience for the t>osition. This order was based upon a request of the Commissioner of Indian Affairs, setting forth the difficulties in securing through ordinary examination persons qualified for the position of disciplinarian, the position requiring a man of ex- ecutive ability, force of character, and knowledge of men. This has been recognized in the past by permitting the positions at other schools to be filled by the promotion or transfer of properly qualified employees, but in this case there was no one in the Department competent to be transferred or promoted to the position. Mr. Stimpel was formerly a noncommissioned officer in the Army, and has a military record which justified the belief that he could do what would be ex- pected of him if appointed to this position. October 21, 1907. John V. Davis, automatic scale mechanician, may be continued in the service for such further period as may be necessary for the completion of the work to which he is now assigned without compliance with the civil-service rules. Mr. Davis has been employed for some time in the Philadelphia mint as automatic scale mechanician, being engaged upon the work of perfecting an automatic scale invented by him to be used in the customs service, if it met with the approval of the Treasury Department. He was not on the rolls of the Philadelphia mint, but was employed there merely because of the facilities It offered in the way of tools, etc. The Department desired to retain the services of Mr. Davis until the completion of the scale, and inasmuch as the Commis- sion had no authority under the law to extend his temporary appointment, it was necessary that an Executive order be issued, which was done upon the request of the Treasury Department October 30, 1907.
