April 27, 1915. Mr. Charles W. G. Brett may be transferred from the position of mechanician at $1,200 per annum to a clerkship at the same salary, in the Bureau of the Census, Department of Commerce, without reference to the civil-service rules. Mr. Brett was appointed as mechanician on September 16, 1908. On March 19, 1912, he met with an accident, in the line of duty, which resulted in the loss of Ids left eye. His transfer to the clerical roll is recommended by the Director of the Census, who states that Mr. Brett's experience in connection with the various machines used in the bureau, his familiarity with the matter to be tabulated, and his general educa- tion are believed to be such as to fit him for clerical duties, tind that his services have always been satisfactory. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the reconmiendation. April 27, 1915. Mrs. Lillian B. S. Grant may be reinstated to a position in the classified service in the Post Office Department without regard to the length of time she has been sep- arated from the service. Mrs. Grant, as Mrs. Lillie B. Silcott, was appointed to the position of printer's assistant in the Bureau of Printing and Engraving on August 2, 1895, and was pro- moted to operative and through the several grades of that position. On July 1, 1902, she was transferred to the Post Office Department as clerk at |900, and was promoted to $1,000 July 1, 1905. She resigned May 9, 1912. The Postmaster General advises that it is stated that Mrs. Grant's husband is a permanent invalid, and that she is therefore compelled to provide a livelihood for both her husband and herself. In view of the circumstances and the fact that the records of the department show that her services were satisfactory and her duties faithfully dischajged, the Postmaster General states that no objection would be offered by the department to the reinstatement of Mrs. Grant by Executive order. Mr. William Leroy Fairbanks may be transferred from chief clerk of the American Embassy at London to a clerkship m the Department of Commerce. Mrs. Enna Wilson Noel may be reinstated to a position in the classified service in the War Department without regard to the requirements of the civil service rules. Mrs. Noelis the widow of Edward McC. Noel, whose death on April 16, 1915, termi- nated a service of nearly 33 years in the office of the Surgeon General, and leaves her dependent upon employment for support. Prior to her marriage she served as clerk in the office of the Surgeon General from December 18, 1893, to February 9, 1900, and previously in other offices since November 7, 1889. Her reinstatement is recom- mended by the Secretary of War and the Surgeon General of the Army. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation. May 11, 1915. Mrs. Laura F. Richmond may be reinstated as an operative in the Bureau of Engraving and Printing without regard to the length of time she has been separated from the service. This order is issued on the recommendation of the Acting Secretary of the Treasury in view of the efficient service rendered by the husbanaof Mrs. Richmond in the Treasury Department for 28 years, and in view of the fact that Mr. Richmond is now wholly incapacitated both mentally and phyeically for the performance of duty," and also because of the satisfactory service rendered by Mrs. Richmond while a former employee in the Bureau of Engraving and Printing where she served for a period of seven years. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation. May 13, 1915. Michael L. Eidsness, jr., of Iowa, a minor clerk of class D ($900) in the Post Office Department, may be promoted to the position of clerk without further civil service examination. This order was issued upon the recommendation of the Postmaster General, who stated that while Mr. Eidsness was not able to pass the first-grade examination he had a very exceptional record in the Post Office Department and was engaged on clerical work of high order; accomplished without apparent effort 50 per cent more work than any of the clerks with whom he was associated. Mr. Eidsness made three attempts to pass the first-grade examination, but on account of lack of early education was unable to pass. May 14, 1915. Mr. James J. Harley may be reinstated to a position in the post office at Philadel- phia, Pa., without regard to the length of time he has been separated from the service. Mr. Harley was a clerk in the Philadelphia post office from June 1, 1893, to November 15^ 1900, when he resigned. His record during that time was entirely satisfactory. His reinstatement is recommended by the postmaster at Philadelphia, who states that he is personally acquainted with Mr. Harley and feels that he is mentally and physically capable of performing with satisfaction and efficiency the duties of a postal clerk. The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation. May 14, 1915. Mr. Bernard R. Jett may be transferred in the Office of the Secretary of the Interior from wireman on the per diem roll to electrician at $1,000 per annum. Mr. Jett is employed on the "Repair of Buildings" roll in a position for many years not re§:arded by the department as classified. He was .one of a small number whose retention and reemployment without a competitive status were authorized by an executive order of June 17, 1909. He has been employed intermittently since Sep- tember 9, 1902, has passed the examination for electrician, and by reason of extensive and varied electrical work has become familiar with the complicated systems of light- ing, being one of the few persons who will be able to assist materially in the prepara- tion of long deferred electrical plans for the several buildings. The action proposed will also save several hundred dollars annually by permitting; the continuous employ- ment of skilled mechanics who must otherwise be laid off when their services can not well be spared.
