The Executive order of January 17, 1873, which is mentioned, prohibits a Federal officeholder from holding a municipal office at the same time. March 4, 1908. Mr. Charles L. Babcock may be appointed as an immigrant inspector for con- fidential duty in the Immigration Service in the Department of Commerce and Labor without examination under the civil-service rules. The Department of Commerce and Labor has found it necessary, in order to investigate satisfactorily conditions surrounding the enforcement of the law relating to immigration, to assign a competent and absolutely reliable officer to the work. Such work requires that the man performing it possess not only tact, judgment, and skill, but also an absolutely unimpeachable character and a wide knowledge of affairs and of human nature. The employee who had been assigned to this duty was transferred to a somewhat similar position under the Department of Justice, and Mr. Babcock was appointed temporarily to the position. The work could not be completed during the period for which Mr. Babcock was temporarily appointed, and upon presenting the facts to the Com- mission it recommended to the President the issuance of the above order. Mr. Babeock's duties are of a confidential nature, and the position is analogous to positions which are excepted under the civil-service rules in other Departments, such as si>ecial employees in the Treasury Department and certain pension examiners and special agents in the Interior Department.
