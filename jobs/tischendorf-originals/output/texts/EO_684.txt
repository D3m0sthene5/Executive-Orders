ORDERS AFFECTING UNCLASSIFIED LABORERS. CERTIFICATION OUTSIDE WASHINGTON, D. C. November 22, 1907. The regulations governing the employment of unskilled laborers in Federal offices outside of Washington, D. C, promulgated December 12, 1904, are hereby amended by the addition of the following clause to Regulation IV, section 1, paragraph (a) : The Commission may, however, in any civil-service district, certify from its register the three standing highest thereon shown by said register to possess the requisite qualifications for the position to be filled. As amended, paragraph (a) referred to will read: (a) The nominating or appointing officer shall request the certification of eligibles, specifying sex and principal duties of the position, and the board shall certify from the proper register the three names at the head thereof, of the sex specified, which may not have been certified three times to the office in which the vacancy exists. The Commission may, however, in any civil-service district, certify from its register the three standing highest thereon shown by said reg- ister to possess the requisite qualifications for the positiou to be filled. It was found that when an unskilled laborer was desired to do work of a particular kind, such as that to be performed on board a vessel in the Immigra- tion Service or the Quartermaster's Department, it was desirable to certify an eligible who had experience along such lines rather than one whose experience had been limited to some other lines, as work with a pick and shovel. In order that there might be no doubt of the authority to recognize legitimate demands for specific qualifications in certifying unskilled laborers, this amendment to the regulations was secured. MISCELLANEOUS ORDERS. PROMOTION CLASSIFIED LABORERS. August 28, 1907. That part of the Executive order of February 24, 100C, which provides that no laborer classified thereunder shall receive increase of pay or be promoted or transferred without examination by the Commission is hereby amended to read as follows: No laborer thus classified shall be promoted in grade or transferred without examination by the Commission; nor shall any laborer be increased in pay beyond the usual and ordinary pay of the positiou and duties such laborer cus- tomarily fills and discharges. The provision which was amended was believed to be too restrictive, as the pay of laborers ranges from $480 to $840 or more per annum. There is nothing to prevent the Department from increasing the pay of unclassified laborers in the field service according to fitness and efficiency. The classification with the restriction heretofore imposed therefore worked to the detriment of the laborer and Imposed a needless restriction in all cases where laborers have earned an increase of compensation and were to be promoted without essential change in duties.
