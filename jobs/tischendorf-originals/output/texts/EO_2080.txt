November 10, 1914. Mr. W. T. Bateman may be appointed a watchman in a navy yard without compli- ance with the civil-service rules. This order is issued on the recommendation of the Secretary of the Navy, who says the case is a deserving one and that the general character of Mr. Bateman is such that he feels justified in recommending an exemption from the civil-service rules which will permit his employment in a navy yard so that he may be able to support his family. It appears that Mr. Bateman, seaman, formerly attached to the U. S. S. Foote, is at present a patient in the Naval Hospital, Washinrton, D. C. While visiting his family a l-pounder shell which he was snowing his child exploded and blew off his left hand. The Surgeon General of the Navy concurs in the recommendation of the Sec- retary of the Navy and says Mr. Bateman is a very deserving man. Smith, who formerly held the position of clerk in the Interstate Com- merce Commission, may be reinstated to a classified position in the Post Office Depart- ment without regard to the length of his separation or the provisions of the civil-service rules requiring reinstatement in the same department. This order is issued on the recommendation of the Postmaster General because of Mr. Smith's previous efficient service in the Interstate Commerce Commission and the exceptional qualifications he has evinced in the performance of hia duties as confiden- tial clerk to the Postmaster General. November 11, 1914. Mrs. Ellen McCabe Bain may be reinstated as an operative in the Bureau of Engrav- ing and Printing without regard to the length of time she has been separated from the service. This order is issued on the recommendation of the Secretary of the Treasury, who states that Mrs. Bain served 11 years in the Bureau of Engraving and Printii^; that she rendered efficient service there; and it is believed that her reinstatement will be in the interests of the service. The Civil Service Commission states that in view of Mrs. Bain's 11 years of service, fairly recent separation, and the fact that she is not of advanced age, it will offer no objection to an Executive order being issued in her behalf.
