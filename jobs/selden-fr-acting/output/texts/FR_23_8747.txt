Pursuant to sections 1 and 2 of the Act

amended by changing the date "October 
3 1 ” wherever it appears in §§ 83.1 (i) and

made effective as soon as possible.

1 0 0 3 ) it is found upon good cause that 
notice of rule making and other public 
procedure with respect to such amend­
ment are impracticable and contrary to

days after publication in the F ederal  
Register.
The foregoing amendment shall be­
com e effective upon publication in the

DECISION TERMINATING PROCEEDING WITH  
RESPECT TO PROPOSED AMENDMENTS TO  
TENTATIVE MARKETING AGREEMENT AND  
ORDER
Thirsuant to the provisions of the Agri­
cultural Marketing Agreement Act of 
1937, as amended (7 U. S. C. 601 et seq.), 
and the applicable rules of practice and 
procedure as amended, governing the 
formulation of marketing agreements 
and marketing orders (7 CFR Part 900), 
a public hearing was held at Dallas, 
Texas, bn December 13, 1957, pursuant 
to notice thereof issued on December 4, 
1957 (22 F. R. 9839). The Acting Deputy
Administrator, Agricultural Marketing 
Service, on June 2, 1958 (23 F. R. 3930) 
filed with the Hearing Clerk, United 
States Department of Agriculture, his 
recommendéd decision containing notice 
of and opportunity to file written excep­
tions thereto.
The Department has been requested 
by producers’ cooperative associations, 
(representing the majority of producers 
in the market) including that which 
made the original request for a hearing 
on proposed amendments to the market­
ing order regulating the handling of milk 
in the North Texas marketing area, to 
terminate the proceeding on the existing 
public hearing without amendatory ac­
tion.
The principal effect of the amendment 
would be to change the seasonal pattern 
of Class I prices (without changing the 
annual level) in order to more closely 
align such prices .with Class I prices in 
other Federal order areas. This change 
would tend to reduce the disposition in 
the North Texas market during the flush 
production period of milk from other 
Federal order areas. It, therefore, would 
tend to increase the utilization in Class I 
of milk delivered directly to North Texas 
plants and, hence, it would tend to im­
prove the returns to the producers of 
such milk. The amendment would have 
had little effect on handlers’ costs of 
Class I  milk.
The amendment was designed to ad­
vantage producers in this marketing area 
but since a majority of them have re­
quested a termination of the proceeding, 
it is concluded that no action should be 
taken relative to the proposed amend­
ments to the tentative marketing agree­
ment and order on the basis of the 
existing public hearing record and the 
proceeding is hereby terminated.
This decision filed at Washington, 
D. C., this 6th day of November 1958.
