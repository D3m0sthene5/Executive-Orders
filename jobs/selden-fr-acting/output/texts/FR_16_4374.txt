PACKING OF RADIOS
In part 35 (39 CFR Part 35) make the  
following changes:
Insert new §  35.6b to read as follows:
§  35.6b Radios, packing of — (a) Elec­
tric. Radios must be packed in strong  
containers such as a flberboard box with  
at least 2 inches of firmly packed cush­
ioning material such as shredded paper  
or excelsior on all six sides completely  
surrounding the radio, unless a factory  
carton approved for shipment by parcel  
post with approved inner cushioning  
by means of spring pads, molds, etc., is  
used. Better packing would be two con­
tainers, each of double-faced corrugated  
flberboard, one within the other, the  
inside container holding the radio to be  
cushioned with a form or mold of double-  
faced corrugated flberboard to form a  
2-inch cushion of air on all six sides. In  
lieu of such forms or molds, shredded  
paper or excelsior may be used in suffi­
cient quantity to keep the inner con­
tainer 2 inches from all inner walls of the  
outside container.
(b) Dry battery.  Portable or other  
radios with plastic or lightweight case  
which contain a dry battery are accept­
able only if the battery is held in a fixed  
and immovable position therein by  
means other than the radio case and  
which cannot damage the radio regard­
less of the position thereof. If not so  
constructed, the battery must be re­
moved from the case and packed so  
that it cannot shift or damage the radio  
in transit. A  separate compartment for  
the battery may be necessary, which  
compartment must keep the weight of  
the battery off the radio regardless of  
the position of the shipping carton.
(R . S. 101, 396, sec. 24, 20 Stat. 361, secs. 304,  
309, 42 Stat. 24, 25; 5 U. S. C. 22, 369, 39  
U. S. C. 250)

BELGIUM, FINLAND, AND INDIA
1. In  §  127.216 Belgium  (39 CFR  
127.216; 16 F. R. 688) , make the follow­
ing changes:
a. Amend subdivision (iii) of para­
graph (b) (4a) to read as follows:
(iii) Parcels containing gold, or silver,  
whether manufactured or unmanufac­
tured; precious stones, jewelry or other  
precious articles, must be insured. If a  
parcel containing such articles is mailed  
uninsured, it shall be placed under in­
surance by the office which first observes  
the fact of its having been mailed un­
insured, and treated accordingly.
b. Amend paragraph (b) (6) by the  
addition of subdivision (d) to subdivi­
sion (iv) to read as follows:
(d) Instruments of payment including  
banknotes and currency may not be sent  
to Belgium by parcel post unless permis­
sion for the importation has been  
granted by the Belgo-Luxembourg Ex­
change Institute. If such permission  
has been granted and the sender elects  
to forward the articles by parcel post,  
the parcel must be insured; otherwise,  
such articles must be sent by registered  
letter mail.
2. In §  127.251 Finland  (39 CFR  
127.251) amend subdivision (v) (c) of  
paragraph (b) (4) to read as follows:
(c) Medicines and drugs, including  
any substances which may be classed as  
pharmaceuticals by the Finnish author­
ities, may not be sent by mail unless  
addressed to authorized manufacturers,  
dealers, scientists or public health of­
ficials for use or distribution in accord­
ance with Finnish regulations. Such  
substances prepared in doses for use by  
patients must be clearly and correctly  
labeled showing the nature and quan­
tity of the ingredients. If found unac­
ceptable under the Finnish regulations,  
any such articles may be prohibited or  
confiscated by the authorities.
3. In §  127.258 French settlements  
in India (.Chandernagore, Karikal,  
Mahe, Pondicherry and Yanaon ) (39  
CFR 127.258) amend paragraph (b) (4)  
to read as follows:
(4) Prohibitions  (i) Articles of gold  
or silver, diamonds and other precious  
stones.
(ii) Seditious literature.
(iii) Saccharine and similar products.
(iv) Tobacco plants.
(v) Nonexplosive components of ar­
tillery fuses.
(vi) Articles bearing false indications  
of origin.
(vii) Preserved fish, vegetables, and  
prunes not bearing an indelible indica­
tion of their origin.
(viii) Coloring materials.
(ix) Chemical and pharmaceutical  
products.
(x) Films, celluloid, etc.
(xi) In general, all articles prohibited  
Importation into France.
4. In §  127.278 India  (39 CFR 127.278) 
amend subdivision (v) of paragraph (b)  
(5) to read as follows:
(v) For other reasons, (.a)  Articles  
of gold or silver, diamonds and other  
precious stones.
(b) Any goods bearing a counterfeit  
trade-mark.
(c) Piece goods, such as are ordinarily  
sold by the yard or piece, manufactured

(d) Saccharine.
5. In §  127.334 Portuguese India  (39  
CFR 127.334) amend paragraph (b) (5)
>to read as follows:
(5) Prohibitions,  (i) Plants, seeds, all

AMENDMENT OF EXECUTIVE ORDER NO. 3149 
OF AUGUST 16, 1919, RESERVING CERTAIN 
LAND FOR THE USE OF THE NAVY DEPART­
MENT FOR NAVAL PURPOSES
By virtue of the authority vested in  
the President and pursuant to Executive  
Order No. 9337 of April 24, 1943, it is  
ordered as follows:
Executive Order No. 3149 of August 16,  
1919, which reserved and set aside a  
tract of land at Seward, Alaska, for the  
use of the Navy Department for the  
erection of wharves, coal storage yards,  
and other naval purposes, and which  
was revoked as to a portion of such  
tract by Executive Order No. 3828 of  
May 3,1923, is hereby amended by trans­
ferring the interest of the Department  
of the Navy in and to the remaining  
portion of the tract to the Department  
of the Army, for military purposes: Pro­
vided, however,  That such land, and the  
wharves, yards, and other improvements  
thereon shall also be available for use  
by the Department of the Interior, in  
such manner and at such times as shall  
not interfere with the use thereof for  
military purposes, pursuant to such  
arrangement as may be made by the  
Secretary of the Army and the Secre­
tary of the Interior.
