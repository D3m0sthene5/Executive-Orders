ADJUSTMENT IN  MAXIMUM PRICE FOR  
FLAXSEED
Pursuant to the authority vested in me 
by the Emergency Price Control Act of 
1942, as amended, and particularly by 
section 1A Ce) (2) (A) of said act as 
added by the Price Control Extension 
Act of 1946,1 hereby determine that con­
tinuation of the maximum price appli­
cable to flaxseed on June 30, 1946, would 
impede the necessary production of such 
commodity and that an adjustment in 
such price is necessary to obtain the 
necessary production. I, therefore, re­
commend to the Price Administrator that 
the maximum price for U. S. No. 1  flax­
seed be established at $4.00 per bushel, 
Minneapolis basis, with differentials for 
other locations and qualities in effect 
June 30, 1946.
There is at the present time a critical 
shortage of linseed oil. This shortage is 
expected to continue at least throflgh 
1947. Stocks of linseed oil are now at the 
lowest level of many years. Increased 
production of this commodity is essential 
to the reconversion program.

seed is necessary to encourage the move­
ment of 1946 crop to crushing mills and 
to encourage a greater production of flax-
