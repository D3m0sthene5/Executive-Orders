Mr. William Langley, a special agent in the Bureau of the Census
from October 1, 1902, to January 15, 1912, may be appointed as a clerk
in the classified service of the Census Bureau.
The Secretary of Commerce and Labor in recommending this action
alludes to the provision in an act approved March I S, 194 for the classi-
fication of thirteen special agents and their transfer to the classified service,
and to the Executive order of December 14, 1905, providing for the
classification of thirteen more special agents and their transfer to the
clerical force. lie states that the special agents thus selected for classifica-
tion were taken on account of their long training in the field and office
work and of the faithful and conscientious service they had uniformly
rendered ; that Mr. Langley has likewise the long experience and capacity,
and that none of the other special agents recently dropped from the Census
rolls have anything like the length of service and variety of experience
that Mr. Langley has.
In view of the facts, the Civil Service Commission joins with the
Secretary of Commerce and Labor in recommending this order.
WM H TAFT
THE WHITE HOUSE,
February 29, 1912.
