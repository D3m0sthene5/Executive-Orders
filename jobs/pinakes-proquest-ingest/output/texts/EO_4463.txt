/1~o ~'iI7 604. Record book at conulates.-The following record books are to be kept at
consular offices:
At inlan~rd consulates:
(1) A record of fees and declared exports, in which shall be recorded daily in
the order in which received all fees for services, including gratis services, authorized
by the Tariff of United States Consular Fees, the serial number, date, and amount
of each fee, the nature of the service, and the person for whom performed. When
fees for invoices are recorded, the kind, quantity, and value of the merchandise
covered by the invoice shall also be recorded.
(2) A fee stamp ledger, in which shall be kept a daily record of the kinds and
values of all fee stamps received and canceled, or otherwise expended.
(3) A register of correspondence received and sent, in which shall be recorded
daily every communication of whatever nature received by or sent from the office,
together with the postage expended on each.
(4) A miscellaneous record book, in which shall be kept such records as the
Department may from time to time direct, as well as records of all official matters
pertaining to the office that require to be recorded and for which no other book is
provided.
(5) A passport book, in which shall be recorded, in the prescribed manner, all
passports issued by the consulate.
(6) A register of landing or debenture certificates, in which shall be recorded
the number and date of each certificate authenticated, the port of shipment, the
name of the vessel from which the merchandise was landed, the name of the master,
the landing port, the numbers, marks and contents of packages, the date of the
bill of lading, the name of the shipper, the name of the consignee, and the date of
unlading.
At seaport consulates:
In addition to the foregoing, the following record books will be kept at seaport
consulates:
(1) A record of arrivals and departures of American vessels, in which shall be
recorded the number, date of arrival, class, name, and tonnage of all American
vessels arriving at and departing from the port, where belonging, from what port,
where bound, date of clearance, and cargo both inward and outward by kind, quan-
tity, and value.
(2) A seaman's register, in which shall be recorded a detailed list of all seamen
shipped, discharged, deserted, or deceased at the consulate, the name of the vessel
to which belonging, and the arrears of wages and extra wages collected and paid
on account of each.

(3) A record of American seamen relieved, in which shall be recorded the num-
ber and names of all seamen relieved, from what vessel discharged, date and cause
of discharge, date of leaving the consulate, andi a detailed statement of the amounts
disbursed on account of each.
(4) A ship's daily journal, in which should be recorded the name of every
American vessel arriving at the port, its registry number, tonnage, where belonging,
date of aA-rival, from what port, when and where built, owners, cargo both inward
and outward and value thereof, the names of the master and other deck officers,
any service performed for or any incident occurring to the vessel during its stay in
port, and the date of its departure.
(5) A marine note of protest book, for the entry of marine notes of protest, in
accordance with Form No. 37.
(6) A marine extended protest book, for the entry of extended notes of protest,
in accordance with Form No. 38.
605. Record books at consular agencies.--The following record books are required
to be kept at consular agencies:
At inland consular agencies:
(1) A record of fees and declared exports.
(2) A fee stamp ledger.
(3) A register of correspondence;received and sent.
(4) A miscellaneous record book.
(5) A register of landing or debenture certificates.
At seaport consular agencies:
In addition to the foregoing, the following record books will be kept at seaport
consular agencies :
(1) A record of arrivals and departures of American vessels.
(2) A seamen's register.
(3) A record of American seamen relieved.
(4) A ship's daily journal.
(5) A marine note of protest book.
(6) A marine extended protest book.
(7) A record of official services to American vessels and seamen, in which shall
be recorded an itemized statement of the official services actually and necessarily
rendered to American vessels and seamen.
606. Files.-In addition to the record books mentioned in paragraphs 604 and
605, the following files are to b9i kept at consulates or agencies as may be required:
(1) A file of correspondence received and sent, which shall contain all cor-
respondence received and copies of all correspondence sent, classified and arranged
in accordance with regulations prescribed by the Secretary of State.
(2) A file of the Department's numbered circular instructions.
(3) A file of original invoices certified at the office during the past five years.
(4) A file of powers of attorney to sign inv 'ces.
(5) A file of triplicate bills of health.
(6) A file of duplicate accounts and returns of the office.
(7) A file of approved applications of American citizens for registration, and
card file thereto.
(8) A file of alien visa applications, visa refusals and refusal notification cards.
(9) A confidential file, containing true readings of code telegrams sent and
received, and correspondence of a confidential nature.
(10) A file of the efficiency records of consular officers and employees submitted
by the officer in charge.

607. What are to be regarded as official docurent.-The originals of all
instructions, letters and telegrams addressed to a consular officer and copies of all
despatcbes, letters, and telegrams written by him in his official capacity including
all official reports, accounts and returns, also all the record books described in this
article, and all books presented to the consulate or sent to it by the Department
of State are to be considered as official documents. They must be deposited
among the consular archives, after being duly entered upon the inventory of United
States government property of the consulate (Form No. 223) and must be trans-
ferred by the officer in charge, together with all other property in his care belonging
to the United States, to his successor in office.
608. Care of archives:-All consular officers are instructed to take care that the
archives of their offices are kept in proper order; and with this in view, as well as
to facilitate reference to previous correspondence, they will keep in their offices
records (Form No. 223) of all the documents, papers, letters and books which have
been received or sent on matters connected with their official duties.
