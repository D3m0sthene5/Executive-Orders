Executive eter
Mr. George J. Bunce mayr be reinstated in a subclerical position in the
Mint and Assay Service of the United States without reference to the
period of his separation from the service.
Mr. Bunce was an employee of the United States Assay Office at
New York for nearly forty years and was for most of the time one of the
most trusted melters. Ill health required his separation from the service
on January 16, 1911. It was desired to treat his separation as a furlough,
which would have rendered him eligible for reemployment within three
years, but such action was not in accordance with the regulations. He
has now recovered his health sufficiently to be able to perform light work,
serve as doorman or upon the day watch, and is in needy circumstances.
During his employment he handled hundreds of millions of dollars worth
of precious metals and was a competent, faithful and valued employee.
This order is requested by the Director of the Mint and recommended
by the Secretary of the Treasury in recognition of Mr. Bunce's long and
faithful service to the Government in a position of trust and responsibility,
and the Civil Service Commission concurs.
WM H TAFT
THE WHITE HOUSE,
January 24, 1913.
[No. 1687.]
