Executive ~ter.
Mr. Edward E. Carter may he reinstated in the Forest Service as
forest inspector at $2800 per annum without regard to the year limit of
the reinstatement rule.
Mr. Carter entered the Forest Service through the field assistant in
forestry examination on March I1, 1905, and resigned on September 30,
1910. His salary wvas increased from $iooo to $2500 per annum by suc-
cessive promotions from forest assistant to forest inspector, chief of office
of management, and assistant forester. His duties during the last two and
one-half years of that period were identical with those which will now be
required, namely: the administration and field inspection of timber sales,
timber reconnaissance, and reforestation. His exceptional capacity had
placed him in rank next to the Assistant Forester in charge of the Branch
of Silviculture, and in the absence of that officer he handled the entire
executive work of the branch. From 1910o to the present time Mr. Carter
has been employed continuously as assistant professor in forestry at Har-
vard University, specializing on forest management, silviculture and the
administration of the forest property of the University. This experience
and training are regarded by the Department as an added qualification for
the special duties of the existing vacancy. The Department regards Mr.
Carter as one of the strongest men in technical forestry ever employed by
the Forest Service.
In view of these facts the Commission concurs with the Department
in recommending this order.
WOODROW WILSON
THE WHITE HOUSE,
26 June, 1915.
