Alaskan Timber Reserve No.1I.
Pursuant to the authority conferred upon me by the provisions of the
Act of Congress approved March 12, 1914 (38 Stat., 305), entitled "An
Act to authorize the President of the United States to locate, construct,
and operate railroads in the Territory of Alaska, and for other purposes,"
I hereby order that the timber on the following described lands be, and
the same is hereby, reserved for the use of the United States in connection
with the location, construction, and operation of government railroad or
railroads and the appurtenances thereto; Provided, that said timber reser-
vation slhall not preclude the Secretary of the Interior from authorizing
the sale or the free use, pursuant to the provisions contained in Section 11,
of the Act of Congress approved May 14~, 1898 (30 Stat., 414) of so much
of the timber upon the lands hereby reserved as shall not be necessary for
the uses for which it is reserved hereunder; and Prov~ided, that said timber
reservation shall not prevent the location, settlement, or entry under the
public land laws applicable to the Territory of Alaska, of the lands within
the reserved areas, subject to the condition that the locators, settlers, or
entrymen shall enter into an agreement whereunder they agree not to cut
or remove any of the timber on the lands located, settled upon, or entered
by them, unless authorized to do so by the Secretary of the Interior, but
no final certificates or patents shall be issued conveying the title to such
lands until it shall have been determined by the Alaskan Engineering
Commission that there is no further need of the use of such lands or the
timber thereupon by the government for effecting the purposes for which
said reservation is made; and Provided Further, that said timber reserva-
tion shall not disturb or interfere with any vested rights which shall have
accrued at or prior to the creation thereof:
Matanuska Valley
A tract of land five miles wide on each side of the proposed right
of way for the government railroad, beginning at the point of inter-
section with the Knik River, thence along the line of said railroad
right of way to the end of the branch line of railroad leading into the
Matanuska coal field.
Knik Arm
A tract of land extending five miles inland, beginning at the
mouth of Knik River, thence along the westerly and northwesterly
shore of Knik Arm, thence along the northerly shore of Cook Inlet
to the mouth of the Susitna River.
Susitna River
A tract of land five miles wide on each side of the Susitna River,
beginning at the mouth of the Susitna River and extending along said
river to the mouth of the Yentna River.
Susitna Valley
A tract of land five miles wide on each side of the proposed right
of way for the government railroad, beginning at the junction of the
main line with the Matanuska branch line, and extending along and
through the Susitna Valley to Broad Pass.
WOODROW WILSON
THE WHITE HOUSE,
22 June, 1915.
