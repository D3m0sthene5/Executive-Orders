Mrs. Elizabeth B. Pettus may be appointed to a clerical position in
the classified service without reference to the civil service rules.
Mrs. Pettus is the widowv of Major Harry L. Pettus, late of the Quar-
termaster Corps of the Army. The Quartermaster General states that
Major Pettus served continuously as assistant quartermaster, U. S. Volun-
teers, with the rank of captain, from June 14, 1899, until honorably dis-
charged from the service June 30, 1901; that he wvas commissioned quar-
termaster in the regular army with the rank of captain to take effect
February 2, 1901, and was promoted to major. on March 3, 191 1; and that
his services were of the highest order of efficiency, embracing every feature
of duty falling to the Quartermaster Department, all of which were per-
formed by him in a most satisfactory and efficient manner. In view of
the facts, the Quartermaster General recommends the issuance of an
Executive Order in this case, in which recommendation the Secretary of
War concurs.
The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation.
