James D. Calhoun may be appointed deputy collector of customs at
the Port of Tampa, in the District of Florida, in the State of Florida,
under the reorganization of the Customs Service which becomes effective
July I st, 1913, without compliance with the civil service rules.
The Civil Service Commission concurs with the Secretary of the
Treasury in recommending the issuance of this order. The Collector is
required to spend the larger part of his time at Jacksonville, the desig-
nated headquarters, and it is necessary to put deputies in charge of the
other ports in the State. The Secretary of the Treasury states that he is
not satisfied that any of the deputies in Florida are sufficiently competent
to be entrusted with the management of the important port of Tampa
where the collections amount to $1,867,763.84 per annum, which is more
than three times that of the next largest port in the State.
A number of temporary appointments have been made in the Florida
collection district without examination because of the lack of eligibles.
The number of eligibles in that district for positions of this character has
been insufficient, both in number and in the grades obtained in examina-
tion, to meet the needs of the service.
