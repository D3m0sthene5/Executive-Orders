Miss Emma B. Shreve and Miss Clara M. Tremper, wvho have been
employed temporarily in the office of the collector of customs at New York
in connection with the compilation of statistics through the installation of
accounting machines, may be permanently appointed' to clerical positions
in the Customs Service at New York without examiination under the civil
service rules.
The Acting Secretary of the Treasury states that the success of this
new system, which will result in a great saving of time and money, is
largely dependent upon the permanent at:pointment of these two women.
The accuracy of the government statistical reports depends upon the
accuracy of operations performed under the direction of Miss Shreve as
supervisor over about twelve young women operators. The collector of
customs at New York states that Miss Shreve has proven herself to be
especially capable and well fitted for this supervisory work.
The Department states that Miss Tremper is an expert operator upon
the Underwood standard electrical adding and typewriting machine. The
tabulated reports as they come from the electrical machines are turned
over to Miss Tremper wvho translates the code numbers into the appro-
priate articles, countries, etc., and transcribes this information in the form
of a typewritten report. This report is prepared upon wax sheets and any
errors therein would require it to be rewritten. Miss Tremper is one of
the speediest operators in the Unites. States, and the work now performed
by her represents the work formerly performed by the press division of
thirteen men.
This order is issued on the recommendation of the Secretary of the
Treasury with the concurrence of the Civil Service Commission.
WOODROW WILSON
THE WITE HOUSE,
29 April, 1916.
