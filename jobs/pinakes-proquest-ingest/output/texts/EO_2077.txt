Mr. W. T. Bateman may be appointed a watchmnan in a navy yard
without compliance with the Civil Service Rules.
This order is issued on the recommendation of the Secretary of the
Navy, who says the case is a deserving one and that the general character
of Mr. Bateman is such that hc feels justified in recommending an exemption
from the Civil Service Rules which wvill permit his employment in a navy
yard so that lie may be able to support his family. It appears that Mr.
Bateman, seaman, formerly attached to the U. S. S. "Foote", is at present a
patient in the Naval Hospital, Washington, D. C. While visiting his family
a one-pounder shell which he wvas showing his child exploded and blewv off
his left hand. The Surgeon General of the Navy concurs in the recomn-
mendation of the Secretary of the Navy and says Mr. Bateman is a very
deserving man. While Mr. Bateman will be discharged from the Navy for
disability he can receive no pension as his disability wvas not incurred in the
line of duty.
The Civil Service Commission does not recommend the issuance of the
order.
WOODROW WILSON
THE WHITE HousE,
