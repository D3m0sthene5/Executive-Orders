Mrs. Edith F. Spofford may be appointed from the Library of Con-
gress, the employees of which are not classified, to a clerical position in the
Bureau of Mines to take charge of the library of that Bureau.
Mrs. Spofford has had twelve years experience in the work of the
Library of Congress which is regarded as a training school for cataloguers
and is at present in charge of the catalogue and periodical division in that
library.' The relations of the Library of Congress to the Bureau of Mines
as a means of obtaining indexed and catalogued data are such as to make
it especially desirable that an expert trained and experienced in the work
of the Library of Congress shall b~e employed. Through the library of
which Mrs. Spofford will be in charge all purchases for the larger technical
library located in the Pittsburg office of the Burcau of Mines will be made
and the books, periodicals, etc., catalogued. Mrs. Spofford is said to be
familiar with German in 'addition to being an experience cataloguer and
to have a working knowledge of several other languages and is considered
by the Librarian of Congress and the heads of divisions in which she has
been employed as qualified for the position. The Civil Service Commis-
sion joined with the Secretary of the interior in recommending this Execu-
tive order in the interests of good administration in view of the facts.
