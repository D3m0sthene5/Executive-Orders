Mrs. Anna M. Curran may be reappointed as printer's assistant in the
Bureau of Engraving and Printing of the Treasury Department. She served in
this position from September 15, 1919, when she was appointed without examina-
tion under authority of an Executive order of January 1,5, 1918, until August 13,
1921, when she was furloughed because of reduction of force, her furlough being
largely due to the fact that her husband was employed in the Government ser-
vice. 'Since her furlough she has been left a widow by the assassination of her
husband. She received several bullet wounds in trying to protect him. She
has been out of the service too long to be eligible for reemployment and the
Executive order under which she received appointment has been revoked. She
could have remained in the service indefinitely if no occasion had arisen to re-
quire her separation. In view of all the circumstances the Secretary of the
Treasury recommends her reinstatement.
The Civil Service Commission concurs in the recommendation that she be
reappointed, with the same status she would have had if she had remained in
the service under her original appointment.
WARREN G HARDING
THE WHITE HOUSE,
December 30, 1922.
