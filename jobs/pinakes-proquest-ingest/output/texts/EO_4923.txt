682. Market value:
(a) Wherever employed in these regulations the wors''value'' and ''mar-
ket value" shall be understood to mean the foreign value or the export
value, whichever is higher.
(b) The foreign value of imported merchandise shall be the market value
or the price at the time of exportation of such merchandise to the
United States, at which such or similar merchandise is freely offered
for sale to all purchasers in the principal markets of the country from
which exported, in the usual wholesale quantities and in the ordinary
course of trade, including the cost of all containers and coverings of
whatever nature, and all other costs, charges and expenses incident to
placing the merchandise in condition, packed ready for shipment to
the United States. (Tariff Act of 1922, Sec. 402.)
(c) The export value of imported merchandise shall be the market value or
the price, at the time of exportation of such merchandise to the United
States, at which such or similar merchandise is freely offered for sale
to all purchasers in the principal markets of the country from which
exported, in the usual wholesale quantities and in the ordinary course
of trade, for exportation to the United States, plus, when not included in
such price, the cost of all containers and coverings of whatever nature,
and all other costs, charges, and expenses incident to placing the mer-
chandise in condition, packed ready for shipment to the United States.
If in the ordinary course of trade imported merchandise is shipped to
the United States to an agent of the seller, or to the seller's branch
house, pursuant to an order or an agreement to purchase (whether
placed or entered into in the United States or in the foreign country),
for delivery to the purchaser in the United States, and if the title to
such merchandise remains in the seller until such delivery, then such
merchandise shall not be deemed to be freely offered for sale in the
principal markets of the country from which exported for exportation
to the United States, within the meaning of this sub-division. (Tarf
Act of 1922, Sec. 402.)

719. Marking of articles and packages to indicate country of origin.-All articles
of foreign manufacture or production, which are capable of being marked, stamped,
branded, or labeled, without injury, at the time of their manufacture or production,
must be marked, stamped, branded, or labeled, so as to indicate the country of
origin in legible English words, in a conspicuous place that shall not be covered or
obscured by any subsequent attachments or arrangements. Said marking, stamp-
ing , branding, or labeling must be as nearly indelible and permanent as the nature
of the article will permit. (Tariff Act of 1922, Sec. 304.)
If any article is found upon examination not to be marked to indicate the
country of orgin it shall not be delivered until so marked at the importer's expense;
and, unless exported under customs supervision, in addition to the regular duty
imposed by law on such article, there shall be paid a duty of 10 per cent of the
appraised value thereof, which includes all dutiable charges, or if such article is
free of duty, a duty of 10 per cent of the appraised value thereof. (Tariff Act of
1922, Sec. 304.)
All packages containing imported articles must be marked to indicate the country
of origin, and also to show the quantity of their contents. If any package is not
so marked it is not delivered until properly marked under customs supervision at
the importer's expense. (Gust. Reg. 1923, Art. 471.)
720. Consular officers and customs representatves to cooperate.-Consular officers
will confer freely with customs representatives abroad regarding the market value
and invoicing of merchandise invoiced through their offices. 'These representatives
have no authority to instruct consular officers as to their official acts, but they are
expected to inform consular officers of the defects which they have observed in the
invoices of particular shippers, and consular officers should cooperate with them by
declining to certify any invoices which do not conform to the law and regulations.
Consular officers will also render to such customs representatives every assistance
in their power in the performance of their duties, giving them free access to the
records and papers of their consulates relating to trade with the United States;
communicating to them promptly any information which may be acquired showing
or indicating actual or contemplated frauds in the exportation of merchandise to
the United States, or which may be in any wise useful to such representatives in
the prosecution of their inquiries or the performance of their duties; and giving
special attention to any invoices of merchandise in which, or in relation to which,
such representatives may advise them that there is reason to apprehend that fraud
or irregularity has been or is likely to be committed.
721. Special Regulations.-The Secretary of State will issue, from time to time,
such instructions as may be necessary for the guidance of consular officers, concern-
ing special regulations affecting goods or shipments of a particular kind.
