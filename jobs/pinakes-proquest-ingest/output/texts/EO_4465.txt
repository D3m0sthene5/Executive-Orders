Paragraph 520 is amended to read as follows :
520. American vessels not required to pay.-By law no fees named in the tariff
of consular fees prescribed by the President can be collected by consular officers
from regularly documented American vessels and seamen for official services to
them.- Consular agents, however, who are compensated by fees, must furnish the
masters of every such vessel with an itemized statement of such services performed
on account of said vessel, with the fee so prescribed for each service, and also make
a detailed report of such services and fees, under such regulations as the Secretary
of State may prescribe, and they will be allowed such compensation for said ser-
vices as they would have received prior to the passage of said law. Such services
will not be compensated, unless they have been necessarily rendered.-23 Stat. L.
66; 18 Op. Att. Gen. 111, 234.
Paragraph 521 is canceled and the following substituted:
521. Other services for which no fee is to be charged.-Services required to be
performed by consular officers for which no fee may be charged are as follows:
(a) Passports for certain persons. Consular officers are not permitted to make
a charge for the issuance of passports to officers or employees of the United States
proceeding abroad in the discharge of their official duties, or to members of their
immediate families, or to seamen, or to widows, children, parents, brothers, and
sisters of American soldiers, sailors, or marines, buried abroad whose journey is
undertaken for the purpose and with the intent of visiting the graves of such sol-
diers sailors, or marines, provided the facts are made a part of the application for
the passport-41 Stat. L. 750.
(b) Visas of passports for foreign officials. Consular officers are not permitted
to make a charge for the execution of the application for a visa or for the visa of
passport for any officer of any foreign government, or members of his immediate
family, 'its armed forces, or of any state, district, or municipality thereof, traveling
to or through the United States.

collecting fees therefor.
Paragraph 522 is amended to read as follows:
522. No additional charges allowed.-When a fee is fixed in the tariff of fees for
any particular act or service, no additional fee is to be demanded for signature,
attestation or affixing the seal of office; nor may any subordinate in the consulate
charge a fee for any assistance rendered by him in connection with any service
performed by the principal officer and for which the latter has charged a fee.
In regard to the verification of invoices, a penalty is provided for charging for
blank forms, advice, or for clerical services in the preparation of the declaration
or certificate, or for receiving any fee greater than that authorized by law; and if
a consular officer collects or knowingly allows to be collected any other or greater
fees than are allowed by law for any service, he is liable in treble the amount of

3
the unlawful charge (plus the costs of recovery) besides the liability to refund it.
In such case the Secretary of the Treasury is authorized to retain the amount of
the overcharge out of the compensation of the consular officer.--R. S. Sees. 1716,
1723. Provision has been made by law for the refundment at the Treasury of
fees and charges erroneously collected by consular officers, from the regularly docu-
mented vessels of the United States when the claim shall have been made within
one year from the date of collection.--33 Stat. L. 59, Sec. 26.
Paragraph 523 is amended to read as follows:
523. Consul liable for fees not collected .- If a consular officer omits to collect
any fees which he is required to collect for a service, he is liable to the United
States therefor as if he had collected them, unless they shall be remitted, on good
cause shown, by the Secretary of the Treasury. Receipts must be furnished by all
consular officers to owners, agents, consignees, masters, or commanders of all ves-
sels from whom fees or charges or extra wages or arrears of wages of seamen shall
be collected.-R?. S. Secs. 1724, 4213.
Paragraph 524 is amended to read as follow:
524. Tonnage and other fees charged against the Treasury.-The tonnage fees
provided for in fees 16 and 17 of the Tariff of the United States Consular Fees are
not charged by consular officers against regularly documented American vessels
and are not charged by consular agents against the Treasury for such vessels run-
ning regularly, making weekly or monthly trips, or otherwise, to or between foreign
ports, for more than four trips in a year. These fees are chargeable, however, by
consular agents against the Treasury for each vessel on the first four trips in each
calendar year. They are chargeable at the foreign port where the principal offices
of the steamship company or owners are located, but are not to be charged at
other consular offices on the route, whether within or without the same allegiance.
Consular fees other than tonnage fees are charged by consular agents against
the Treasury for all services performed for regularly documented American vessels;
and when a vessel makes a special trip to a foreign port other than that to which
it runs on its regular trips, at consular agent at the port thus visited is entitled to
receive tonnage fees for the special trip.-R?. S. Sec. 1720; 1 Comp. Dec. 374.
