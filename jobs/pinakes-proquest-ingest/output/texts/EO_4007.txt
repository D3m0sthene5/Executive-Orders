Executive 0rbet
AUTHORIZING THE ALIEN PROPERTY CUSTODIAN TO SELL
CERTAIN PROPERTY UPON THE NEW YORK STOCK EXCHANGE
By virtue of the authority vested in me by an Act of Congress entitled"An act to define, regulate, and punish trading with the enemy and for otherpurposes", approved October 6, 1917, known as the " Trading with the Enemy
Act", and the amendments to such Act embodied in "An act making appropria-
tions to supply urgent deficiencies in the appropriations for the fiscal yearending June 30, 1918, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses and forother purposes", approved March 28, 1918, and the amendment to such actembodied in "An act making appropriations to supply deficiencies in appro-
priations for the fiscal year ending June 30, 1919 and prior fiscal years on account
of war expenses, and for other purposes", approved November 4' 1918, and all
other amendments thereto, to make certain orders such as that herein contained,
I hereby, in the public interest, make the following determination, order, rule
and regulation :
TheAle Property Custodian is hereby authorized to sell upon the New
York t chne at such time as to him or to his authorized agent may
seem advisable, without public or other advertisement, ninety-seven thousand,
two hundred ninety-five shares of the capital stock of the Standard Oil Companyof New Jersey, a corporation organized under the laws of the S tate of New
Jersey, of the par value of twenty-five dollars ($25.00) per share which said
shares were heretofore required to be conveyed, transferred, assigned and
delivered to the Alien Property Custodian as the property of Carl Schutte,
Louise Braesecke, Gustav Schutte, Wilhelm A. Riedemann, Erwin C. Riedemann
and Meta Schutte under the provisions of the said " Trading with the Enemy
Act, provided that no part of the said stock hereby authorized to be sold, shall
be sold at a price less than twenty-five dollars ($25.00) per share.
My reasons for the foregoing determination and order are :
First : That the dividends paid upon this stock are so small as to be dis-proportionate to the investment as based upon the current market quotations,
and it does not appear that the earnings of the corporation are such as to justify
an increase in the dividend rate, and that an investment of the proceeds of
the sale in Liberty Bonds or Certificates of Indebtedness of the United States
Government would produce a yield greatly in excess of that derived from the
dividends upon the said stock.
Second : That a public or other advertisement of the sale of the said stock
would have a tendency to defeat the purpose of the sale by causing a depression
in the market price.
Third : That an attempted sale at auction would be a useless formality,
would involve unnecessary expense and inconvenience and would cause such
delay as to seriously imperil the realization of an adequate price.
CALVIN COOLIDGE
THE WHITE HousE,
