Execcutive Order
Edward A. Gisburne may be appointed to a classified position without
reference to his physical condition after recommendation by some appoint-
ing officer and such noncompetitive examination as the Civil Service
Commission may require.
Mr. Gisburne was a messenger boy in the classified service of the
Navy Department from September 14, 1908, to February 7, 1910, when
he resigned to enlist in the Navy. He wvas honorably discharged from the
Navy on August 29, 1914, by reason of c'isability resulting from wounds
incurred in the line of duty, and was awarded a medal of honor for
attempting to save a comradewhile wounded himself. Under the law he
is entitled to preference in original appointment if found, upon examina-
tion, to possess *ba required business capacity.
This action is taken upon the request of the postmaster at Boston,
Massachusetts, and the Executive order is recommended by the Civil
Service Commission.
WOODROW WILSON
THE WHITE HOUSE,
29 October, 1914.
