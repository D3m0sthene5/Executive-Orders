)Executive Orber
By virtue of the authority contained in Sec. 8, of the Panama Canal Act, as
amended by the Act of Congress approved September 21, 1922, it is'ordered;
1. That the following rules of practice and procedure for the United States
District Court of the Canal Zone shall be in force on and after the date hereof.
__2. That all rules of practice and procedure contained in the Codes of Criminal
and Civil Procedure of the Canal Zone, in conflict herewith, and the rules of practice
and procedure, numbered 1 to 23, inclusive, contained in the Executive Order of
December 30, 1921\are hereby repealed.
- RULES OF COURT
1
CHANGE OF VENUE
The district judge may order a change of venue in any case, civil or criminal,
from one division of said court to the other, whenever in his opinion, in the interest
of justice, such action becomes necessary. Such change of venue may be ordered
upon the motion of the judge, on the application of either party or by consent of
parties.
Whenever a change of venue has been ordered by the court, the clerk shall
immediately make out a true transcript of all the orders made in said cause, and
certify thereto under his official see [, and transmit the same with the original papers
in the case to the other division of the district, and the case shall be tried therein
as if it had been instituted there originally.
(Old Rule 2. L. C. Z. 19-197. Ses. 11 & 12, K. . 163)
2
COURT Doc~rs
The clerk, under the advice of the judge and the district attorney, may cause
to be prepared, and shall keep, all such dockets as may be necessary for the pur-
poses of said court.
(OldL Rule3)
3
MoTION DAYS AND DOCKETS
Saturday of each week shall be motion day in the Balboa division of said
court, and the first and third Mondays of each month shall be motion days in the
Cristobal division thereof.
Unless the court is engaged in the trial of a case, such motion days shall be
devoted to hearing motions, demurrers, entering decrees, assigning causes for trial,
confirming reports, entering default judgments, and any other matter ii the con-
duct and preparation of causes preliminary to or after trial.
All motions or other hearings not consented to must be made- in writing, filed
with the clerk, and by him noted upon the book known as the "Motion Docket".
Motions filed in the Balboa division, on or before Thursday of each week shall be
heard on the following Saturday, and motions filed in the Cristobal division on
or before Friday preceding the first and third Mondays of each month shall be
heard on the next following motion day, unless in any case the court orders such

2
hearing deferred; provided that any motion may be heard by consent of the parties
at the conclusion of the regular motion docket, or any motion may be heard at any
time upon the order of the court and upon such notice to the parties as the court
may direct.
(Old Rule 4)
4
PLACING MOTION ON DOCKET is NOTICE.
Any motion or demurrer filed with the clerk and by him placed upon the docket,
shall be notice to opposing counsel, and no further notice need be given except in
those instances where the law requires notice to be otherwise served.
(Old Rule 5)
5
How PLEADINGS AND MOTIONS MAY BE FILED
An answer may be filed in the clerk's office if presented within the time pre-
scribed by the rules or the Code of Civil Procedure. If the time shall have expired
within which an answer or any other pleading must be filed, it may be filed there-
after only by consent of the court. Any pleading which the court directs to be
filed within a given time may be filed in the clerk's office, if presented for filing
within the time prescribed.
Any motion or demurrer may be filed in the clerk's office unless there be some
order of the court or rule of procedure which prohibits the filing thereof, and all
pleadings or motions filed with the clerk must be noted of record by him.
(Old Rule 6)
6
PLEADINGS IN CIVIL ACTIONS
All pleadings and motions in civil cases shall be signed by the attorney or party
to the suit or his duly authorized agent or representative, and shall be endorsed on
the back thereof with the docket number, the name or names of the parties plaintiff
and defendant, the nature of the action, and the name of the attorney of record.
There shall be filed with the original of all motions and all pleadings other than
the complaint, a copy thereof for the adverse party, which shall be endorsed on the
back in like manner as the original; and it shall be the duty of the clerk to show the
date of the filing thereon and promptly deliver or mail such copy to the adverse
party or his attorney of record.
(Old Rule 8)
7
JuR Y
In the trial of any cause, criminal or civil, where a jury trial may be demanded,
if either party shall desire a jury, request theref or must be made at the time when
such cause is assigned for trial. At the trial each side shall be entitled to six per-
emptory challenges. A waiver of a challenge by either party shall preclude such
party, except by consent of court, from thereafter challenging peremptorily any
juror then in the jury box, and the remaining challenges of such party shall be
limited to jurors thereafter called.
(Old Rule 7)
8
EXCEPTIONS
a. An exception is an objection upon a matter of law to a decision made by a
court, tribunal, judge, or other judicial officer, in an action or proceeding. Except
as provided in subsection b. hereof, the exception must be taken at the. time the
decision is made. Rulings of the court upon minor discretionary matters, such as
adjournments, postponements of trials, and the like, shall not be subject to exception.
b. WHAT DEEMED EXCEPTED To: The verdict of the jury, the final decision
it an action or proceeding, an interlocutory order or decision finally determining

8
the rights of the parties or some of them, an order or decision from which an appeal
may be taken, an order sustaining or overruling a demurrer, granting or refusing a
motion to set aside an information, a motion in arrest of judgment, a motion for a
new trial, making or refusing to make an order after. judgment affecting any sub-
stantial rights of the parties, refusing to grant a change of the place of trial, allowing
or refusing to allow an amendment to a pleading, striking out a pleading or a portion
thereof, an order made upon ex parte application, an order or decision made in the
absence of a party, and instructions given or refused, are deemed to have been
excepted to.
(Old Rule 16; Code of Civ. Proc., Sec. 134. Cal. C. C. P., Sec. 646-647. Code of Crim,
Pro., L. C. Z., 206)
9
TIME FOR FILING BILL OF EXCEPTIONS
A bill of exceptions must be prepared, settled, and filed within ten days after
judgment is entered; provided, however, that the court or judge, for good cause
shown, upon application made within such time, may extend the time for preparing,
settling and filing such bill of exceptions, for a period not to exceed sixty days from
the time of making such order; provided, further, that an additional extension of
time beyond that granted by the court may be allowed on stipulation of parties.
(Old Rule 9. Code of Crim. Proc., L. C. Z., 206. Code Civ. Pro., Sec. 136)
10
MOTION FOR NEW TRIAL
A motion for a new trial shall be made only after verdict of the jury or decision
by the court, and before judgment. Such motion shall be filed within five days
after verdict of the jury or decision by the court, unless, for good cause shown,
the court or judge, within such five-day period, extends such time. Such motion
shall be in writing and must set out specifically the grounds upon which the same
is made. When a ground of a motion is the insufficiency of the evidence to justify
the verdict or decision, the motion must specify the particulars in which the evi-
dence is alleged to be insufficient. If a ground of the motion be error in law
occurring at the trial and excepted to by the moving party, the motion must
specify the particular errors upon which the party will rely, and in the case of a
question as to the admissibility of evidence the question, objection or motion,
ruling, and exception thereto must be fully set out. Such motion shall be heard
and determined as speedily as possible after the same is filed.
(Old Rule 10. Cal. C. C. P., Sec. 659)
11
MOTIONS AND ACTIONS TO VACATE OR SET ASIDE JUDGMENTS
A motion to vacate or set aside a judgment or order of the district court must
be in writing, and must be filed within ten days after such judgment or order was
entered except in the case of a default judgment. In the latter case th~e same
must be filed within the time and upon the terms prescribed in Section 106 of the
Code of Civil Procedure, which is hereby re-enacted and adopted by reference as
a part of these rules. Such motion must be accompanied by a showing that a
meritorious defense exists to the cause of action upon which the judgment is based.
Such a motion shall not operate in any case as a stay of execution unless the court
shall so order, or the party applying therefor shall execute a supersedeas bond to
be approved by the court.
An action to reverse, vacate and set aside the judgment of subordinate tribu-
nals, obtained by fraud, accident, mistake or excusable negligence, must be brought
within six months from the rendition of such judgment; otherwise, the procedure
therein as prescribed in Sections 141 to 145, inclusive, of the Code of Civil Proce-
dure, shall be followed, and such sections are hereby re-enacted and adopted by
reference as a part of these rules.
(Old Rule 15. Code Civ. Proc., Sees. 106, 141 to 145)

ARGUMENTS ON MOTIONS .
No argument of any motion shall be extended beyond thirty minutes to the
side without permission of the court.
(Old Rule 20)
13
TIME FOR MAKING DEFENSE
In all civil actions commenced in the district court, except divorce and
admiralty actions, where personal service has been had, the person served shall
make defense in court within ten days from the service of summons, and where
service by publication is had, within forty days from the last publication of
summons.
(Old Rule 18)
14
TIME WITHIN WicH COURT ORDERS MUST BE PREPARED
When a written order is taken in court, and the attorney for either side is
directed to prepare the order so taken, it shall be prepared and presented to the
court or judge for approval and delivered to the clerk not later than five days
thereafter.-
(Old Rule 19)
15
PREPARING COURT ORDERS
All orders, minutes of proceedings, or actions taken in court, and required to
be made matters of record, must be prepared and recorded not later than one week
from the date when made.
(Old Rule 23)
16
INFORMATIONS
Every offense of which the district court has jurisdiction must be prosecuted
by information signed and verified by the district attorney, or, in the case of his
absence or inability to act, by a~n assistant district attorney appointed by the
Governor of The Panama Canal. The verification must state that the information
is based upon due investigation of the facts relating to the crime charged therein.
All informations so prepared must be filed with the clerk. A criminal cause so
prosecuted shall stand for trial during the term in which the information is filed or
at the next succeeding term, after the apprehension of the defendant, unless for
good cause shown, a continuance thereof to a later date is ordered. Any such
information may be amended in matters of form; bu~t such amended information
must not charge a crime different from the one charged or sought to be charged in
the original information.
(C01i Rule 17. E. 0., p. 97, Code Crim. Pro., Secs., 3, 65, 94, 100. L. C. Z., pp. 173, 183,
187, 188)
17
BONDS TO BE RECORDED
All fiduciary bonds, attachment bonds, bonds required to be executed in all
matters of receivership, injunction proceedings, and any and all other bonds re-
quired by the Code of Civil Procedure, except bonds for cost, bonds for arrest,
or appeal from inferior courts, shall be copied in full and properly indexed by the
clerk in a book kept for that purpose, and such copy, duly authenticated by him,
shall have the force and effect of the original, and the cost of recording shall be
taxed as part of the cost in the court proceeding or action wherein filed, or in the
settlement thereof.
(Old Rule 11)

BONDS AND COSTS
The Court may at any time before trial require a nonresident plaintiff to file
an undertaking for costs. The defendant may at any time move for security for
costs, and when the motion is made the nonresidence must be shown by affidavit,
unless it is admitted or it appears upon the papers or from the record.
(Old Rule 13)
20
BONDS
In all proceedings in which a bond is required to be given the clerk may accept
a cash deposit in the sum of the bond, a corporate surety, or a personal bond with
approved sureties. No corporate surety may be accepted which is not qualified to
do business in -the Canal Zone. At least one of the sureties on a personal bond
shall be a reieto h.Canal Zone. Where a cash bond is given, money deposited
with the clerk may be withdrawn only upon order of the court.
(Old Rule 14)
21
STENOGRAPHIC NOTES
Whenever, in the discretion of the court, the importance of the case demands,
the judge may cause the testimony in the case, or such part of the testimony as
he may deem proper, to be taken down in shorthand by the official court reporter
connected with the court and qualified to do such work, and the testimony so taken
shall not be transcribed unless so directed by the judge. Provided: Upon approval
of the court, any lawyer practicing before the court may have his own stenographer
in attendance for the purpose of reporting a case for his own personal use.
The original stenographic notes, taken by the court reporter, shall be a part of
the proceedings in the cause; and, unless such notes are filed pursuant to an order
of the judge, directing the stenographer to file the same with the clerk forthwith,
or within a specified time, the same must be carefully preserved by the court reporter
for at least twelve months after the final judgment of the court is rendered. th
Whenever the official court reporter takes down the testimony in a case,th
party making application for such work, except the Government, shall reimburse
to the Government of the Canal Zone the actual time of the court reporter while
thus engaged, no fractional part of a day less than a half day to be calculated;
unless for good and sufficient cause shown the court shall waive this charge.
A party to either a civil or criminal cause, who desires a transcript of the
stenographic notes of the testimony and proceedings therein, or a part thereof,
shall request the court reporter to furnish the same. The court reporter will
furnish to such party an estimate of the cost thereof, and such party, unless it be
the Government, shall deposit with the court reporter the estimated cost of such
transcript, and thereupon the court reporter must furnish such transcript with all
reasonable diligence, to the party requesting the same. The court reporter shall
charge the feesaloe for such service, and shall return to the party making such
deposit any unused portion thereof. The party requesting such transcript shall
be liable for any excess of such fees over such estimate, to be paid before delivery
of such transcript.
(Old Rule 21)

PROCEDURE ON APPEAL FROM MAGISTRATE'S COURTS
a. In a civil action where an appeal has been perfected from a magistrate's
court to the district court, the party appealing shall pay to the clerk a docket fee
of Five Dollars ($5.00) on or before the first day of the next term of the district
court, or, if the record on appeal is not lodged ten days before the first day of the
next term, then such docket fee shall be paid within ten days after the record on
appeal is lodged with the clerk.. If such docket fee be not paid within the time
fixed, then the appellee may pa$'such fee, and on motion may have the same
judgment entered as that entered in the magistrate's court, plus the appellee's
costs in this court, or a trial of the case in the district court.
b. When such case is docketed the clerk shall notify the parties by mail of
such fact. If not disposed of as provided above by the rendition of judgment,
the plaintiff shall file a written complaint within five days after such notice is mailed,
together with a copy thereof for each defendant. The clerk must then forthwith
mail to each defendant, or his attorney of record, a copy thereof, and the defendant
shall have five days after such mailing to plead thereto. Where the defendant
files a cross complaint the same procedure shall be followed as provided above

7
upon the filing of the plaintiff's complaint. Such time for filing pleadings may be
extended by the court or judge. In framing the issues and in the trial and deter-
mination of such action on the merits, except as provided herein, the same procedure
shall be followed as in a case originally commenced in the district court.
c. Such action shall be tried and determined in the district court on the same
issues presented in the magistrate's court; provided, however, that where an appeal
is taken from a default judgment, the party defaulted may, upon timely application
to the district court and upon a clear showing of mistake, surprise, inadvertence,
or excusable neglect, be admitted to plead upon such terms as may be imposed.
No pleading or amendment thereto shall be allowed which will increase the recovery
beyond the jurisdiction of the magistrate's court. The issues in the magistrate's
court shall be determined from the record on appeal transmitted by the magistrate
to the clerk of this court.
(New and Code Civ. Pro., Sec. 68-10.5)
d. In criminal actions appealed from a magistrate's court, the affidavit may
be amended in this court as to matters of form or substance where the rights of
the defendant are not substantially prejudiced thereby; but the amended affidavit
may not charge a crime different from that charged, or sought to be charged, in
the original affidavit.
e. If it shall appear from the record on appeal or otherwise be made clearly
to appear to the satisfaction of the district court, that the transcript of a magis-
trate's court does not show all of the proceedings in said court required by law to
be shown therein, unless such defects be waived, the following proceedings may
be had : The district court shall forthwith notify the magistrate in writing of. the
precise matter to be considered and allow him to amend his return according to
the facts. If he neglects or refuses so to do, the district court may issue a rule
requiring such magistrate to appear and show cause why his return should not be
amended; but he shall, in no case, be required to amend his return unless it shall
clearly appear that the return does not show the proceedings according to the
facts.
(Code Civ. Pro., Sec. 71)

LIBERAL CONSTRUCTION-AUTHORITY OF JUDGE
The foregoing rules shall be construed to promote justice. Where the Codes
of Criminal or Civil Procedure or these rules fail to specify a procedure in any given
case or matter, the court or the judge may adopt and follow such procedure as will
insure the due administration of justice between the parties.
