RULES GOVERNING THE GRANTING AND ISSUING OF PASPORTS IN THE
UNITED STATES
Section 4075 of the Revised Statutes of the United States, as amended by the
act of Congress approved June 14, 1902 (32 Stat. 386), provides that "the Secretary
of State may grant and issue passports, and cause passports to be granted, issued,
and verified in foreign countries by such diplomatic or consular officers of the
United States, and by such chief or other executive officer of the insular possessions
of the United States, and under such rules as the President shall designate and
prescribe for and on behalf of the United States." The following rules are
accordingly prescribed for the granting and issuing of passports in the United
States.
Section I. AUTHORITY TO ISSUE PASSPORTS IN THE UNITED STATES AND ABROAD
1. Sections 4075 and 4078 of the Revised Statutes as amended by the Act of
June 14, 1902 (32 Stat. 386) provide that no one but the Secretary of State may
grant and issue passports in the United States, and he is empowered to refuse them
in his discretion.
2. Passports are not issued by American diplomatic and consular officers
..broad, except in cases of emergency. A citizen who is abroad and desires to pro-
cure a passport should apply therefor to the Secretary of State through the nearest
American diplomatic or consular officer.
3. Passports are issued in the Virgin Islands, Hawaii, the Philippines and
Porto Rico by the chief executives of those Islands. Applications for passports
by persons residing therein should be made to such chief executives.
Section II. To WHOM PASSPORTS ARE ISSUED
4. Section 4076 of the Revised Stat ates of the United States as amended by
the Act of June 14, 1902 (32 Stat. 386), provides that "no passport shall be granted
or issued to, or verified for, any other persons than those owing allegiance, whether
citizens or not, to the United States."
5. A passport will not be issued to a minor contrary to the request of the
parental or other guardian.
Section III. PERSONS WHO MAY BE INCLUDED IN ONE PASSPORT
6. Only persons who are citizens or who owe allegiance to the United States
may be included in a passport of the United States.
7. A passport issued to a husband or father may include his wife and minor
children. A woman's passport may include her minor children.
8. A minor brother or sister may be included in the passport of an older brother
or sister.
9 A minor grandchild, niece or nephew of tender years may be included in the
passport of the relative when the application therefor is accompanied by a request
from the parental or other guardian.
10. Members of the immediate family who are 21 years of age, maidservants
and manservants must bear separate passports.

Section IV. FEE FOR THE EXECUTION OF AN APPLICATION AND FOR A PASSPORT;
PASSPORTS ISSUED WITHOUT FEES
11. Section 1 of the Act of June 4, 1920 (41 Stat. 750) provides in part as
follows :
"From and after the 1st of July, 1920, there shall be collected and
paid into the Treasury of the United States quarterly a fee of $1 for
executing each application for a passport and $9 for each passport issued
to a citizen or person owing allegiance to or entitled to the protection of
the United States: --- - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - - -
"* * * that no fee shall be collected for passports issued to
officers or employees of the United States proceeding abroad in the dis-
charge of their official duties, or to members of their immediate families,
or to seamen, or to widows, children, parents, brothers, and sisters of
American soldiers, sailors, or marines buried abroad whose journey is
undertaken for the purpose and with the intent of visiting the graves of
such soldiers, sailors, or marines, which fact shall be made a part of the
application for the passport."
12. Under the provisions of the foregoing act a fee of $1.00 must be collected
for the execution of each application for a passport. There is no exception to
this rule.
13. A fee of $9.00 must be collected for each passport issued except as here-
inafter provided in rule 14.
14. In accordance with the provisions of the Act of June 4, 1920 (41 Stat. 750)
no fee should be collected for passports issued to officers or employees of the United
States proceeding abroad in the discharge of their official duties, or to members
of their immediate families, or to seamen, or to widows, children, parents, brothers
and sisters of American soldiers, sailors, or marines buried abroad whose journey
is undertaken for the purpose and with the intent of visiting the graves of such
soldiers, sailors, or marines.
15. The fee of $9.00, payable except as provided in Rule 14, in currency, postal
or express money order must accompany each application for a passport. Postal
or express money orders should be made payable to the Disbursing Clerk, Depart-
ment of State. Drafts or checks will not be accepted in the payment of passport
fees.
Section V. APPLICATIONS FOR PASSPORTS
16. The Act of June 15, 1917 (Title IX, Section 1, 40 Stat. 227) requires every
applicant for a passport issued under the authority of the United States to submit
a written application, duly verified by his oath and containing a true recital of each
and every matter of fact which may be required by law or by rules authorized by
law as a prerequisite to the issuance of a passport.
17. The application should be made in person and signed by the person to
whom the passport is to be issued.
18. The application must be made before a clerk of a Federal court or a State
court authorized by the Act of Congress of June 29, 1906 (34 Stat. 596), to naturalize
aliens, or before an agent of the Department of State.
19. The seal of the court, when the application is executed before a clerk of'
court, or the seal of the agency of the Department, when the application is executed
before such agent must be affixed to the application.
20. Where the application is not made at or near the place where the applicant
resides, the applicant should give the name and address of a reputable person,
residing at or near the place of the applicant's residence, to whom the clerk of
court, the Department's agent, or the Department itself may address such inquiry
as may be necessary concerning the applicant. Where it is necessary to make
inquiries by telegraph, the applicant should bear the expense thereof.

Section VI. CONTENTS of APPLICATION FOR A PASSPORT
a. General. Each application for a passport must contain the following:
21. The applicant's name.
22. The names, places and dates of birth of other persons to be included in
the passport (wife and minor children).
23. If the wife is to be included in the husband's passport the place and date
of the marriage.
24. The place and date of the applicant's birth.
25. The name of the applicant's father, the country of his birth and his pres-
ent place of residence. If the applicant is a married woman the same information
must be given concerning her husband.
26. When and for what, period of time the applicant has resided outside of the
United States.
27. The applicant's place of permanent residence, if any, in the United States
and the occupation followed by him at that place.
28. The number, date, place of issue, and the disposition made of any previous
passport issued to the applicant.
29. The period within which the applicant intends to return to the United
States for permanent residence.
30. The applicant's intended port and date of departure, and the name of the
vessel, if passage has been arranged for in advance.
31. The applicant's oath of allegiance to the United States.
32. The applicant's signature in full; thus, John Henry Smith, not J. H.
Snith.-A married woman should sign her own Christian name or names with the
family name of her husband; thus, Mary Elizabeth Doe; not Mrs. John Doe. If the
applicant is a married woman who desires the use of her maiden name on her pass-
port, she may, in applying for a passport, use her maiden name and sign her applica-
tion with that name followed by the name of her husband, thus: "Mary Doe
(wife of John Jones) ", provided she submits satisfactory proof that she customarily
uses her maiden name and is regularly known thereunder in the community where
she lives. If the applicant signs by mark, two attesting witnesses to the signature
are required.
33. Description of the applicant.
b. Object of the applicant's journey abroad.
34. The applicant for a passport must state in his application the names of
the countries he expects to visit and the object of the visit to each.
35. The Secretary of State may within his discretion require an applicant
for a passport, or for the amendment or extension of a passport to submit satis-
factory evidence of the object of his journey abroad.
c. Photographs of the applicant and persons accompanying the applicant.
36. The application must contain a photograph of the applicant and the per-
sons, if any, by whom the applicant is accompanied. A group photograph of all the
persons to be included in the passport is preferable.
37. Photographs should have a light background and must be on thin paper not
more than 3 by 3 inches and not less than 21/% by 2 % inches in size.
38. The photograph of the person or persons included in the application must
be attached to the back of the application under the seal of the officer before whom
the application is executed.
39. A duplicate copy of the photograph attached to the application must accom-
pany the application for use in the passport for which application is made.
40. Pas'ort photographs must be signed by the applicant, the signature to
correspond th the signature in the application.

41. Photographs in uniform will not be accepted except from applicants who
are mn the active service of the military forces of the United States and who intend
to proceed abroad on official business.
d. Affidavit of a supporting witness.
42. The applicant must be accompanied by at least one credible witness, a
citizen, who has known the applicant for a period of two or more years.
43. The application should contain the supporting affidavit of such a witness
who must state the period of time during which he has known the applicant; that
the applicant is the person whom he represents himself to be, and that his state-
ments are true to the best of his knowledge and belief.
44. The applicant or the witness should reside within the jurisdiction of the
officer before whom the application is executed.
45. No lawyer or other person who expects to receive a fee ini connection with
the application or passport will be accepted as a witness.
46. A passport issued by the Secretary of State on or after January 3, 1918, to
which is affixed the photograph and signature of the person to whom the passport
was originally issued will be accepted in lieu of an identifying witness.
e. A woman's application.
47. A woman who has never been married must state in her application that
she has net been married.
48. A married woman must state in her application the date of her marriage.
49. A widow or divorcee must state in her applihcationl the date of her marriage
and the date of the termination of the marital relation.
f. Applications of naturalized citizens.
50. An applicant who is a citizen through his own naturalization, must state in
his application the date of his immigration, the period of his continuous residence in
the United States, the name of the court and place in which he was naturalized and
the date of his naturalization as shown in his certificate or record of naturalization.
He must also state the precise periods and places, of his foreign residence, if any,
subsequent to naturalization. If the signature in the application does not conform
to the applicant's name as written on his certificate of naturalization, an explanation
of the difference should be submitted.
51. If the applicant claims citizenship through the naturalization of husband
or father, the applicant must state that he or she is the wife or child, as the case may
be, of the person described in the certificate of naturalization, and with reference to
the husband or father, the date of his immigration, period of his continuous residence
in the United States, the name of the court and place in which he was naturalized,
and the date of his naturalization as shown by the certificate or record of naturaliza-
tion. The applicant must also state the period of his or her own continuous resi-
dence in the United States and the precise periods and places, if any, of his or her
foreign residence subsequent to the naturalization of the husband or father.
g. Application of a resident of an insular possession of the United States who
owes allegiance to the United States.
52. A resident of an insular possession of the United States who owes allegiance
to the United States must state in his application that he owes allegiance to the
United States and that he does not acknowledge allegiance to any other government.
Section VII. APPLICATIONS OF OFFICERS OR EMPLOYEES OF THE UNITED STATES,
ANL OF THEIR IMMEDIATE FAMILIES
53. Officers or employees of the United States proceeding abroad under orders
must submit with their applications a written request, from the head of the Depart-
ment or office in which the applicant is employed, for the isF -'e of a passport to the
applicant. The request should set forth the nature of the employment of the
applicant and the official nature of his journey abroad.

54. Applicants for passports who are the members of the immediate family
of an officer or employee of the United States who is about to proceed abroad or
who is abroad in the discharge of official duties are not required to submit docu-
mentary evidence of their status if they refer to a passport issued to such an officer
or employee of the United States.
Section VIII. APPLICATIONS OF AMERICAN SEAMEN
55. As a rule American seamen tray-'ling as such should use in lieu of passports
the Seaman's Certificate of Citizenship issued by Customs officials. Nevertheless
passports are issued to seamen who are citizens or who owe allegiance to the United
States whenever passports are required upon the travel in which the seaman is
engaged.
56. A seaman must transmit with his application, for cancellation, the Sea-
man's Certificate of American Citizenship or other official document. of nationality
or identity of which he is the bearer.
Section IX. APPLICATIONS OF PERSONS VISITING THE GRAVES OF AMERICAN
SOLDIERS, SAILORS OR MARINES BURIED ABROAD
57. An application for a passport executed by a widow, child, parent, brother,
or sister of an American soldier, sailor, or marine buried abroad must be accom-
panied by a statement from the War Department, Washington, setting forth the
name of the deceased American soldier, sailor, or marine to wvhonm the applicant
claims relationship and that the body is buried in a foreign country. Passports
issued upon such applications will be valid only for the country to be 'visited, and
countries en route, and for the purpose of visiting the grave.
Section X. EVIDENCE OF CITIZENSHIP TO ACCOMPANY APPLICATIONS FOR
PASSPORTS
a. Native citizen.
58. A person born in the United States in a place where official records of
birth were kept at the time of his birth must submit with the application a birth
certificate under th" seal of the official custodian of birth records. A certificate to
be acceptable must show the place and date of birth and that the record thereof
was made at the time of birth or shortly thereafter. If a birth certificate is not
obtainable that fact should be shown and the application should be supported by
a baptismal certificate or a certified copy of the record of baptism under the seal
of the church in which the applicant was baptized, giving the pla5ce and date of
birth, the date of baptism and the date on which the record of baptism was made.
A baptismal certificate to be acceptable must show that the baptism occurred
within a short time after the birth of the applicant. If birth and baptismal cer-
tificates are not obtainable an affidavit of the parent or of the physician, nurse, or
midwife, who attended the birth, or the affidavit of a reputable person having
sufficient knowledge to be able to testify as to the place and date of the applicant's
birth may be accepted. A person who did not attend the birth but who testifies
concerning the place and date of the applicant's birth should state briefly how, and
through what source the knowledge was acquired.
59. A person born abroad whose father was a native citizen of the United
States must submit with the application the evidence of the father's birth in this
country as required in the preceding section.
60. If the applicant comes within the provisions of Rules 58 or 59 reference to
a previous application with which the required evidence of birth was submitted
will be sufficient, provided the application upon which a previous passport was
issued is found to contain the necessary record of the evidence submitted.

77. A resident of an insular possession of the United States who owes allegiance

knowledge of the date and place of the applicant's birth was acquired.
Section XIII. THE USE OF TITLES AND BUSINESS, OR PROFESSIONAL NAMES IN
PASSPORTS
78. Professional and other titles will not be inserted in passports, but an
applicant's name in religion, an author's nzoin tie plume, at stage or business name,
etc., may be included in parentheses after the bearer's name.
Section XIX. ADDITIONAL REGULATIONS
79. The Secretary of State is authorized to make regulations on the subject
of granting and issuing of passports additional to these rules and not inconsistent
with them.
