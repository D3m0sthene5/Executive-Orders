Mrs. Florence G. Miller, widow of the late Lieutenant Edward V.
Miller, United States Army, may be appointed a clerk in the classified
service of the United States, subject to such tests of fitness as the Civil
Service Commission may require.
Notable in achievement, the exceptionally arduous and efficient service
of Lieutenant Miller is entitled to special consideration. He was detailed
in 1902 to civil duty in the isolated province of Palawan, Philippine Islands,
a province inhabited principally by uncivilized peoples, and during this
entire service of eight years his devoted wife shared his lot. He was
drowned in the performance of his official duty.
This service wvas characterized by the Chief of Staff before the mili-
tary committee of the House of Representatives as more than equal
through its personal influence to a batallion of troops, and was recently
made the subject of a formal resolution of the Philippine civil commission
recognizing the splendid work which he did and the vicissitudes which he
and his wife endured in their exile for an unbroken period of eight years
as meriting the substantial recognition of Congress.
Mrs. Miller is left without sufficient means for the support of herself
and child. While this case is so extraordinary and exceptional as to place
it in a class by itself and amply to justify special recognition, the appoint-
ment of Mrs. Miller who is known to be a woman of high character and
attainments, subject to demonstration of her qualifications by the usual
civil-service examination, appears to be meritorious and in the interests of
the public service. The Civil Service Commission joins the Secretary
of War in recommending the issuance of this order.
WM H TAFT
[HE WHITE HOUSE,
