Mr. Jesse L. Suter may be appointed to a clerical position in the classi-
fied service without reference to the civil' service rules.
Mr. Suter was appointed confidential clerk att $ 1800 per annum in the
office of the First Assistant Postmaster General on August c~, 1909, the
position being excepted from examination under Schedule A of the Civil
Service Rules. The Postmaster General states that Mr. Suter since his
appointment has qualified himself in an exceptional manner for usefulness
in the Department ; that he is a man of college education and trained in
journalism, for which reason he has been assigned to editorial work, and
that in the course of his duties he has familiarized himself with the opera-
tions of all branches of the postal service. As the staff of men in the
Department competent to do editorial work with expert knowledge of the
subject matter is limited, the Postmaster General is of the opinion that it
will be of decided advantage to the Government to have Mr. Suter con-
tinue in the service.
The Civil Service Commission does not ',oncur in recommending this
order.
WM H TAFT
THE WHITE HOUSE,.
