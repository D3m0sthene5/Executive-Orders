Mr. Thomas H. Vanderford may be reinstated to an appropriate
position in the Internal Revenue Service without reference to the period
of his separation from the service.
Mr. Vanderford wvas a deputy collector of internal revenue from
October 11, 1893, to July 10, 1898, when hie was removed for political
reasons. A former Collector of Internal Revenue, under whom he served
and by whom he wvas separated from the service, makes the following
statement regarding him in a letter dated May 9, 1913:
Mr. Vanderford was an officer in the service when I was appointed
Collector in 1898 and had been for four years prior to my appoint-
ment. He served under me for practically a year. I regarded him
as one of the best officers in the service and after ten years' experience
as Collector I think I can truthfully say he w~as as good an officer as I
ever knew. I separated him from the service in order to appoint a man
of my own party.
The Commissioner of Internal Revenue in a letter dated July 7, 1913,,
states that he has personally known Mr. Vanderford for many years, that
he is a man of sterling integrity, honest and fearless, and in his opinion
possesses just such traits of character as would make a competent and
efficient revenue officer. The records of the Internal Revenue Bureau
show that he rendered satisfactory service and was an efficient employe.
In view of the above facts the Commission concurs with the recom-
mendation of the Secretary of the Treasury and the Commissioner of
Internal Revenue for the issuance of this order.
WOODROW WILSON
THE WHITE HOUSE,
