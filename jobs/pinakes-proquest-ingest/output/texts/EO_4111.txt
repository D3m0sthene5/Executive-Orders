284. Change the note at the top of page 227 to rend as follows:
Note.-Where, to a charge of desertion, accused files a plea denying
desertion but admitting absence without leave, and pleading, by exception
and substitution, not guilty of violation of the fifty-eighth article of war, but
guilty of violation of the sixty-first article of war, such plea of guilty alone is
not in itself sufficient basis for a conviction of desertion, no matter how long
the absence without leave thereby admitted. But this rule does not apply
where (a) there is proof, independent of such plea, of the prolonged unau-
thorized absence of an accused, as, for instance, a morning report or an extract
copy thereof; or (b), where, although there is no proof, other than such plea,
of unauthorized absence for the period thereby admitted there is other proof
from which reasonably it may be inferred that the accused intended to desert
the service. In case of such a plea the court should receive evidence of the
facts and circumstances and determine from the evidence before it whether
or not the accused is in fact guilty of desertion or only of some lesser included
offense.
