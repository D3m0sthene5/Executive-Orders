Executive Order~
AUTHORIZING THE ALIEN PROPERTY CUSTODIAN
TO SELL CERTAIN PROPERTY AT PRIVATE SALE
By virtue of the authority vested in me by an Act of Congress entitled "An act
to define, regulate, and punish trading with the enemy and for other purposes",
approved October 6, 1917, known as the "Trading with the Enemy Act", and the
amendment to such act embodied in "An act making appropriations to supply
urgent deficiencies in the appropriations for the fiscal year ending June 30, 1918,
and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses and for other purposes", approved
March 28, 1918, and the amendment to such act embodied in "An act making
appropriations to supply deficiencies in appropriations for the fiscal year ending
June 30, 1919, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses, and for other
purposes", approved November 4, 1918, to make certain orders such as that herein
contained, I hereby, in the public interest, make the following determination, order,
rule and regulation:
The Alien Property Custodian is hereby authorized to sell at private sale, at
such time a nd place as to him or to his authorized agent may seem advisable,
without public or other advertisement, for and in consideration of the sum of
SEVENTY-SEVEN HUNDRED FIFrY ($7,750) DOLLARS, certain real estate now being
held and administered by him as such Mlien Property Custodian, and heretofore,
upon requirement, conveyed, transferred, assigned and delivered to him as the
property of MARY M. BERGENER, residing in Germany, whom, after investigation,
he did determine to be an enemy within the purview of said Act as amended, which
said real estate is situate in the City, County and State of New York and described
as follows:
ALL that certain lot, piece or parcel of land, with the buildings and improve-
ments thereon erected, situate, lying and being on the southerly side of Houston
Street, between Goerck and Mangin Streets, mn the Eleventh Ward of the City
of New York, bounded and containing as follows: to-wit: BEGINNING at a
point on the southerly side of Houston Street aforesaid distant sixty feet
westerly from the southwesterly corner of Houston and Mangin Streets; thence
running wsterly, in front on Houston Street, twenty feet; thence southerly,
and parallel with Mangin Street, seventy-five feet; thence easterly and parallel
with Houston Street, twenty feet; and thence northerly, and parallel with
Mangin Street, seventy-five feet to Houston Street aforesaid, at the point
aforesaid, the place of beginning. Containing in width in front and rear
twenty feet and in depth on each side seventy-five feet, be the said several dis-
tances more or less.
My reasons for the foregoing determination, order, rule and regulation are:
(a) An advantageous offer for the sale of saidl real estate has been received by
the Alien Property Custodian, as shown by the appraisals of the property sold;
(b) An attempted sale at public auction would be a useless formality and would
involve unnecessary expenses, delay and inconvenience, as the sum received by
this sale in excess of all appraisals is in excess of the value as indicated by appraisals;
(c) It appears that it is to the interest of the trust held, in that its value will
be conserved by this sale by the substitution of the proceeds in lieu of the property
sold, because of the difficulty of the administration of such real estate and its
consequent deterioration;
(d) The character of this property is such that there are greater possibilities
of a decrease in its value than an increase, and that such sale made at this time
would, therefore, be to the best advantage of the trust.
