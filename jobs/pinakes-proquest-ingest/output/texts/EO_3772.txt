Mrs. Frank L. Scott may be a~inted to a clerical position in the casi-
fled service of the War Department without regard to the civil service rules.
This order is recommended by the Chief of Finance, under whom Mrs.
Scott served under temporary appointment. She has performed her duties in
a satisfactory manner and by her general aptitude has become a valuable asset
to the service. Her husband is confined in St. Elizabeths Hospital suffering
from paresis and his recovery is hopeless, this condition doubtless having resulted
largely from his service as a soldier in the Tropics and to overwork during the
late war. While in the Tropics he contracted a fever, which caused a generally
weakened condition of his system. During the war he organized an important
unit of the War Department in Washington and gave attention to his work
without regard to hours. Mrs. Scott has been left solely upon her own resources,
as her husband receives no pension or other remuneration.
The Secretary o:\War, while reluctant to recommend an Executive order,
states that favorable action will be agreeable to him.
The Civil Service Commission does not concur in recommending this order.
