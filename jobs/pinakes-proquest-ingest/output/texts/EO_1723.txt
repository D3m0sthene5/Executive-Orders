Executive crber.
Mr. Frank P. Caviezel may be reinstated as special employee in the
Internal Revenue Service without reference to the period of his separation
from the service.
Mr. Caviezel was a deputy collector of internal revenue from Septem-
ber 1, 1899, to June 25, 1908, when he was appointed internal revenue
agent, and continued in that capacity until July 13, 1911~, when his services
were discontinued because an indictment had been returned against him
alleging acceptance of bribes and other violations of law. The case came
up for trial in December, 1912, and resulted in acquittal. Before recoin-
mending his reinstatement, the Commissioner of Internal Revenue deemed
it advisable- to have the matter fully investigated by a revenue agent. The
revenue agent states that evidence on which the indictment was based wvas
furnished by convicted crooks, and is of the opinion, as was evidently the
trial jury, that the evidence upon which the indictment was based should
be entirely disregarded and that the jury was right in finding him innocent
of the charge. He therefore recommended that Mr. Caviezel be reinstated
at the earliest date practicable. The members of the trial jury state in a
petition that the charges in the indictment were based entirely upon perjury
and conspiracy by a class of men unworthy of belief. His superior officers
testified on the witness stand that his record is unblemished except for the
charges made in the indictment. The jury therefore urgently recom-
mended his reinstatement which they believed to be his ju~st due.
The Secretary of the Treasury requests this order upon the recom-
mendation of the Commissioner of Internal Revenue, and the Civil Service
Commission concurs.
WM H TAFT
THE WHITE HOUSE,
February 27, 1913.
