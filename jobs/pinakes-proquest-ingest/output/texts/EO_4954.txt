William A. Marshall may be reinstated to an appropriate position in the
General Accounting Office without regard to the time limit on reinstatement under
the civil service rules.
Mr. Marshall was employed in the Treasury Department and in the General
Accounting Office for approximately three years as a messenger, having been dis-
continued December 31, 1922, on account of failing health. He is now physically
fit to resume work and has a wife and six small children to support.
This order is recommended by the Comptroller General of the United States,
who states that Mr. Marshall was faithful, efficient, and deserving and his conduct
exempIlary at all times. The Civil Service Commission concurs.
