Executive Other.
Pursuant to the authority conferred upon me by the Act of Congress
approved April 20, 1922 (42 Stat. 496), entitled "An Act to authorize an appro-
priation to enable the Director of the United States Veterans' Bureau to provide
for the construction of additional hospital facilities and to provide medical,
surgical, and hospital services and supplies for persons who served in the World
War, the Spanish-American War, the Philippine Insurrection, and the Boxer
Rebellion, and are patients of the United States Veterans' Bureau", that por-
tion of CapCuse ihgn containing 675 acres, more or less, hereinafter
described, is hereby transferred to the United States Veterans Bureau as con-
templated by the authority above cited. The portion of Camp Custer hereby
transferred to the United States Veterans' Bureau is described as follows:
"Beginning at the point of intersection of the east line of Section 30
T 1 S R 8 W with the southerly boundary of the right-of-way of the Michigan
Central Railroad, the said point being 97 feet more or less north of the
southeast corner of said section; running thence south on the east line of
said section and the east line of Section 31 T i S R 8 W to a point of 300
feet north of the east and west quarter line of said Section 31; thence
westerly to the north and south quarter line of said section 31; thence
south on the north and south quarter line to the central quarter post of
said Section 31; thence west on the east and west quarter line of said
Section 31 to the county line between Calhoun and Kalamazoo Counties;
thence north on said county line to the east quarter post of Section 36 T i
S R 9 W, 38 feet more or less; thence west on the east and west quarter
line of Section 36 to the central quarter post of said section 36; thence
north on the north and south quarter lines of Sections 36 and 25 T i S R
9 W to the southerly boundary of the Michigan Central Railroad; thence
easterly along said boundary line to the point of beginning, containing 675
acres more or less".
WARREN G HARD ING
THE WHITE HOUS~,
May 10, 1923.
