AUTHORIZING THE ALIEN PROPERTY CUSTODIAN TO SELL,
CERTAIN PROPERTY UPON THE NEW YORK STOCK EXCHANGE

By virtue of the authority vested in me by an Act of
Congress entitled "An act to define, regulate, and punish trading with
the enemy and for other purposes", approved October 6th, 1917, known
as the "Trading with the Enemy Act", and the Amendments to such Act
embodied in, "An Act making appropriations to supply urgent deficiencies
in the appropriations for the fiscal year ending June 30th, 1918, and
prior fiscal years, on account of war expenses and for other purposes,"
approved March 28th, 1918, and the Amendment to such act embodied in,
"An act making appropriations to supply deficiencies in appropriations
for the fiscal year ending June 30th, 1919, and prior fiscal years,
on account of war expenses and for other purposes," approved November
4th, 1918, and all other amendments thereto, to make certain orders
such as that herein contained, I hereby, in the public interest, make
the following determination, order, rule and regulations:

Shallian Property Custodian is hereby authorized to
sell upon the New York Stock Exchange, at such time as to him or to
his authorized agent may seem advisable, without public or other adver-
tisement, Five Hundred (500) shares of the common stock of the Corn
Products Refining Company, a Corporation organized under the laws of
the State of New Jersey, of the par value of Twenty Five Dollars
($25.00) per share, which said shares were heretofore required to be
conveyed, transferred, assigned and delivered to the Alien Property
Custodian as the property of Vlamer Levy, under the provisions of the
"Trading with the Enemy Act".

By reasons for the foregoing determination and order

are:

First: That the dividend record of the corporation over a
period of several years is not such as to justify its classification
as an investment stock and that the income return upon the investment,
based on the current market quotations, is too small to warrant the
retention of the principal in a security, the market value of which
fluctuates so widely.

Second: That the sale of the said stock and the investment of
the proceeds in Liberty Bonds or Certificates of Indebtedness of the
United States Government would insure a fair income upon the invest-
ment and conserve the principal, preventing possible depreciation by
reason of a decline in the market price.

Third: That the sale of the said stock will result in the
realization in tangible form of a great increase in principal by
virtue of the appreciation of the market value of the stock since
the time of its issue and since its seizure by the Alien Property
Custodian.

Fourth: That a public or other advertisement of the sale of
the said stock or attempted sale at auction, would be a useless
formality involving unnecessary expenses and inconvenience, and would
cause such a delay as to impair the realization of a price equal to
that now prevailing on the open market.

THE WHITE HOUSE,

March 20, 1925.
