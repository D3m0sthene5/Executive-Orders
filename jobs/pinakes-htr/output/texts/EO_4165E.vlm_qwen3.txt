EXECUTIVE ORDER

AUTHORIZING THE ALIEN PROPERTY CUSTODIAN TO SELL
CERTAIN PROPERTY UPON THE NEW YORK STOCK EXCHANGE

DEPT. OF STATE
MAR 10 1925
DIVISION OF PUBLICITY

By virtue of the authority vested in me by an
act of Congress entitled "An act to define, regulate, and punish
trading with the enemy and for other purposes", approved October 6,
1917, known as the "Trading with the Enemy Act", and the amendments
to such act embodied in "An act making appropriations to supply
urgent deficiencies in the appropriations for the fiscal year ending
June 30, 1918, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses
and for other purposes", approved March 28, 1918, and the amendment
to such act embodied in "An act making appropriations to supply
deficiencies in appropriations for the fiscal year ending June 30,
1918, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses, and for
other purposes", approved November 4, 1918, and all other amendments
thereof, to make certain orders such as that herein contained, I
hereby, in the public interest, make the following determination,
order, rule and regulations:

The Alien Property Custodian is hereby
authorized to sell upon the New York Stock Exchange, at such time as
to him or to his authorized agent may seem advisable, without public
or other advertisement, Three thousand (3000) shares common stock of
the American Locomotive Company, incorporated under the laws of the
State of New York, of no par value, which said shares were heretofore
required to be conveyed, transferred, assigned and delivered to the
Alien Property Custodian as the property of "Jisner Bankverein, Vienna,
Bernhard Zilk, Julius Zilk, Behrens & Herman, Knauth Knahod & Zahn, and Ludwig Rosauer.

By reasons for the foregoing determination

and order are:

Firsts: That there has been such a variation in the
earnings applicable to the common stock of the corporation over a
period of several years as to preclude its classification as a
proper investment for trust funds.

Seconds: That the sale of the said stock and the investment
of the proceeds in Liberty Bonds or Certificates of Indebtedness
of the United States Government would insure a fair income upon the
investment and conserve the principal, preventing possible depreciation
by reason of a decline in the market price.

Thirds: That the sale of the said stock will result in
the realization in tangible form of a great increase in principal by
virtue of the appreciation of the market value of the stock since its
original purchase, and since its seizure by the Allen Property
Custodian.

Fourths: That a public or other advertisement of the
sale of the said stock or attempted sale at auction would be a useless
formality involving unnecessary expense and inconvenience and
would cause such a delay as to imperil the realization of a price
equal to that now prevailing on the open market.

THE WHITE HOUSE,
March 9, 1925.
