AUTHORIZE THE ALIEN PROPERTY CUSTODIAN
TO SELL AT PRIVATE SALE CERTAIN SHARES
OF STOCK OF THE SCHUYLER & MERRIMAN COMPANY.
A circular ink stamp from the Department of State. The text "DEPT. OF STATE" is at the top, "DIVISION OF PUBLICATION" is at the bottom, and the date "OCT 5 1927" is in the center.
By virtue of the authority vested in me by an Act of Congress entitled "An act to define, regulate, and punish trading with the enemy and for other purposes", approved October 6, 1917, known as the "Trading with the Enemy Act", and the amendments to such Act embodied in "An act making appropriations to supply urgent deficiencies in the appropriations for the fiscal year ending June 30th, 1918, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses and for other purposes", approved March 25th, 1918, and the amendment to such act embodied in "An act making appropriations to supply deficiencies in appropriations for the fiscal year ending June 30th, 1919, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses, and for other purposes", approved November 4th, 1918, and all other amendments thereto, to make certain orders such as that herein contained, I hereby, in the public interest, make the following determination, order, rule and regulations:
The Alien Property Custodian is hereby authorized to sell at private sale without public or other advertisement three thousand (3000) shares of the preferred common stock and accumulated dividends of the Schuyler & Merriman Company, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, of a par value of $100.00 per share, heretofore required to be converted, transferred, assigned and delivered to the Alien Property Custodian as the property of United States, owned, for Three Hundred Twenty-five Thousand ($250,000.00) dollars upon

the following terms, viz., One Hundred Fifty Thousand ($150,000.00) Dollars in cash and the remainder, One Hundred Seventy-five Thousand ($175,000.00) Dollars, represented by a note payable July 1, 1928, with interest at five (5%) per cent; the said stock to be held as security by the Alien Property Custodian until the said note is paid.
My reasons for the foregoing determination and order are:
First: That the Schulte & Koerting Company is a comparatively small concern. It has outstanding $100,000.00 of common stock divided into one thousand (1000) shares of $100.00 par value each, and $500,000.00 of preferred stock divided into three thousand (3000) shares of a like par value. The Alien Property Custodian holds the entire issue of the preferred stock, but no common. Voting privileges are in the common stock exclusively.
Second: That no dividends have ever been paid to the Alien Property Custodian on the preferred stock so that it is placed in the position of holding the entire issue of preferred stock of the company without receiving any return thereon, and without the possibility of a voice in the management of the concern.
Third: That the stock is held for account of the estate and the heirs of the estate have manifested their willingness to accept this price and have requested the Alien Property Custodian to negotiate the sale on the terms as hereinbefore stated.
Fourth: That considering the current position and indicated earning ability of the Company, it would appear the offer of Three Hundred Twenty-five Thousand ($725,000.00) dollars for the three thousand (3000) shares of preferred stock and accumulated dividends represents a fair market value. Also, that in view of the fact that the common and preferred issues are closely held, there is little public interest in the affairs of the company and the shares have no established market. It is therefore believed that advertisement and public sale of the stock would involve unnecessary expense and would not result advantageously to the trust.
THE WHITE HOUSE
