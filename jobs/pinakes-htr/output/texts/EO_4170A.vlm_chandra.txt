EXECUTIVE ORDER
AUTHORIZING THE ALIEN PROPERTY CUSTODIAN TO SELL
CERTAIN PROPERTY FROM THE NEW YORK STOCK EXCHANGE.
Mar. 14 1921
DIVISION OF FOREIGN AFFAIRS
By virtue of the authority vested in me by an Act of Congress entitled "An act to define, regulate, and punish trading with the enemy and for other purposes", approved October 6, 1917, known as the "Trading with the Enemy Act", and the amendments to such Act embodied in "An act making appropriations to supply urgent deficiencies in the appropriations for the Fiscal year ending June 30th, 1918, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses and for other purposes", approved March 28th, 1918, and the amendment to such act embodied in "An act making appropriations to supply deficiencies in appropriations for the fiscal year ending June 30th, 1919, and prior fiscal years on account of war expenses, and for other purposes", approved November 4th, 1918, and all other amendments thereto, to make certain orders such as that herein contained, I hereby, in the public interest, make the following determination, order, rule and regulations:
The Alien Property Custodian is hereby authorized to sell upon the New York Stock Exchange, at such time as to him or to his authorized agent may seem advisable, without public or other advertisement, Eight thousand Two hundred Fifty (8250) shares Common stock of the Southern Railway Company, incorporated under the laws of the State of Virginia, of One Hundred Dollars ($100.00) par value, heretofore required to be conveyed, transferred, assigned and delivered to the Alien Property Custodian as the property of Deutsche Bank of Berlin, Hugo Stirmes, etul., Hardy & Company, Dresdner Bank of Berlin, and Paul Lindenburg and Oscar Jelsmann as Agents for the Dresdner Bank.

My reasons for the foregoing determination
and order are:
First: That dividends have been paid on the Common Stock of the Corporation in only two years since its organization, and that there has been such a variation in the earnings applicable to such Common Stock over a period of several years as to preclude its classification as a proper investment for trust funds.
Second: That the sale of the said stock and the investment of the proceeds in Liberty Bonds or Certificates of Indebtedness of the United States Government would insure a fair income upon the investment and would prevent a possible loss of income through a decline of earnings of the corporation and consequent reduction of the dividend, and would conserve the principal, preventing a possible depreciation thereof by reason of a decline in the market price.
Third: That the sale of the said stock will result in the realization in tangible form of a great increase in principal by virtue of the appreciation of the market value of the stock since its original purchase, and since its seizure by the Alien Property Custodian.
Fourth: That a public or other advertisement of the sale of the said stock or attempted sale in connection would be a useless formality involving unnecessarily expense and inconvenience and would cause such a delay as to imperil the realization of a price equal to that now prevailing on the open market.
