December 11, 1920.
Mrs. Natalie Summers, of the District of Columbia, may be appointed to a
clerical position in the classified service of the Government without reference
to the requirement of examination under civil-service rules.
Mrs. Summers is the widow of Mr. Maddin Summers, who entered the con-
sular service on July 27, 1899, and served the United States efficiently in Spain,
Bolivia, Serbia, Brazil, and Russia, as consular assistant, consul, and consul
general, and also in connection with various international commissions and
conferences of a diplomatic or commercial nature. Mr. Summers assumed
charge of the consulate general at Moscow, Russia, on November 1, 1916, while
it was in charge of the interests of several governments taking part in the war
and administered that difficult office with great credit to himself and advan-
tage to his Government during the first year of American participation in the
war, until he died suddenly of overstrain on May 4, 1918. Mr. Summers left
a widow, Mrs. Natalie Goraynoff Summers, and a minor son, whose property
interests were rendered valueless by the revolution in Russia. The Acting
Secretary of State recommends that an Executive order be issued authorizing
the appointment of Mrs. Summers in the classified service of the Government.
The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation.
