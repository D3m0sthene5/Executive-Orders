April 23, 1926.
The civil-service rule prescribing citizenship as a prerequisite for appoint-
ment may be waived in the case of Mr. Joseph Roith, in order that he may be
permitted to continue in employment as ordnance man in the Naval Gun Factory,
navy yard, Washington, D. C.
This order is issued upon the recommendation of the Secretary of the Navy,
who states that Mr. Roith was born in Bavaria, Germany, on July 12, 1886,
and, when 5 years old, came to this country with his father and mother
who settled in Bloomer, Wis., and still live there; that Mr. Roith enlisted in
the United States Marine Corps on October 18, 1905, and served four years,
being honorably discharged on October 18, 1909; that he reenlisted on January
15, 1910, for four years and was appointed corporal on March 3, 1910, and
honorably discharged on January 14, 1914. He has been continuously em-
ployed at the Naval Gun Factory, navy yard, Washington, D. C., since July
9, 1914, and is now engaged in handling and loading certain pyrotechnic
materials, work for which no other employee of the Naval Establishment is
qualified, by reason of his knowledge and training. On August 22, 1924, while
Mr. Roith was engaged in handling certain pyrotechnic material, he was
severely burned and injured in a minor explosion. Mr. Roith remained outof the military service in the late war because of the fact that his services
at the Naval Gun Factory could not be replaced without substantial material
loss to the adequate and effective operation thereof.
The Civil Service Commission does not concur in this recommendation.
