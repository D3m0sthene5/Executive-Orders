December 19, 1914.
Mrs. John L. Evans may be appointed to the position of charwoman under the
superintendent of the State, War, and Navy Departments building without regard
“to the labor regulations governing appointments of laborers in Washington.
Mrs. Evans is the widow of John L. Evans, who was discharged a corporal from
Company H, Twenty-second United States Infantry, July 12, 1902, character excel-
lent. From July, 1902, until January 24, 1914, he was employed in the navy yard in
this city. On said date, while working as a machinist’s helper at the navy yard, a
hammer flew off the handle and struck him on the head, from the effects of which he
died June 18, 1914, after a long illness and much suffering, leaving a wife and two
small children.
The acting superintendent of the State, War, and Navy Departments building
Iocoinmgnds that an Executive order be issued authorizing the appointment of Mrs.
vans.
The Civil Service Commission does not concur in the recommendation.
(It will be noted that this order waives the labor regulations, and not the civil-
service rules.)
