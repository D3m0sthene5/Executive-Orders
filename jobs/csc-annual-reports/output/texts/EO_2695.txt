September 6, 1917.
Miss Eulalie A. Chase may be appointed a clerk in the competitive class in the
postal service without regard to the civil-service rules.
The Postmaster General states that Miss Chase has been in charge of a contract
postal stat.on in San Diego, Cal., for about five years and that it has been decided
to change this station from a contract to a classified branch of that office, thus necessi-
tating the employment of clerks who are under the civil service.
With respect to the qualifications of Miss Chase, the Postmaster of San Diego says
that she is one of the very best postal clerks in the city, efficient and exceptionally
bright in handling mail and the public; that the tremendous volume of business at
Station 1 was built up mainly by the amazing popularity and capability of Miss Chase;
that it would be an actual calamity to lose her services; and that it would be almost
impossible to put a new clerk in her place because she knows how to take care of every
branch of the work.
