March 21, 1917.
“In reinstatements and transfers under the civil-service rules the Civil Service
Commission may, when not inconsistent with existing laws, disregard the period
during which the employee was in the military service under the President’s call of
June 18, 1916.”’
As illustrating the class of cases to which this order applies, it may be stated that
the commission had under consideration a request for approval of the reinstatement
of a former employee who had been out of the civil service eight months at the time
he was mustered into the military service. He had been out of the civil service much
more than a year, and his reinstatement was not permissible under existing rules.
The man was represented as having been the most efficient employee in the work on
which he was engaged that the bureau ever had, and his reinstatement was recom-
mended as being very desirable and for the good of the service. In another case the
commission authorized a transfer, but before it became effective the person was
mustered into the military service. The position was filled but became vacant, and
the department desired to renew the request for approval of the transfer. It was not
permissible under existing rules, for the reason that the person had not actually served
in a competitive position for the six months preceding the date of transfer. The
order was therefore recommended by the commission.
