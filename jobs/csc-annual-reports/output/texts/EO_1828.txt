September 18, 1913.
Mr. David A. Robbins, formerly a letter carrier at Boston, Mass., may be reinstated
as a letter carrier in the Postal Service without regard to the length of his separation
from the service.
Mr. Robbins was appointed a substitute letter carrier February 15, 1900; appointed
a carrier December 30, 1902, at $600; promoted to $800 December 30, 1903; resigned
April 28, 1904; reinstated as senior substitute carrier November 15, 1904; promoted
to carrier at $800 November 22, 1904; promoted to $1,000 November 22, 1905; pro-
moted to $1,100 July 1, 1907; promoted to $1,200 July 1, 1908; reduced to $1,100 August
1, 1910; resigned October 31, 1910, for personal reasons. He made application for
reinstatement in a letter dated May 17, 1911, and in reply was informed of the con-
ditions of the reinstatement rule and that his application must be renewed through
the postmaster in order to receive consideration. On October 15, 1912, he made a
second request for reinstatement.
The Postmaster General states that from the facts presented, it would seem that an
Executive order reinstating Mr. Robbins in the service would be warranted. The
Civil Service Commission does not recommend this order.
