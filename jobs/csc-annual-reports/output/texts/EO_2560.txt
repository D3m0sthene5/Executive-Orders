March 26, 1917.
“When the Civil Service Commission decides that, because of a public sign yl:
there is need of the immediate filling of a position for which there is no suitable
eligible, the commission may authorize the filling of such position by the appointment
of a person shown to be qualified by such noncompetitive tests of fitness as the com-
mission may prescribe.”’
An order of this character was necessary in the present exigency to enable the
departments to obtain employees with unusual qualifications where there were no
eligibles immediately available without the delays ordinarily incident to the holding
of competitive examinations to obtain eligibles. It became necessary to allow a con-
siderable number of temporary appointments without requiring tests of fitness pending
the holding of competitive examinations, subject to the condition that the persons
thus temporarily appointed should later enter the examination. This requirement
proved embarrassing to the departments in this season of stress, due to the fact that
many qualified persons will not accept temporary appointment subject to entering
competition with other applicants. It seemed wiser in such cases where urgency
required to allow permanent appointment outright upon noncompetitive tests in
order to offer the largest inducements possible to qualified persons to accept the
appointments tendered. The order enables the commission to make such necessary
exceptions to competition as it would recommend to the President at the request of
the departments, and its operation is confined to such cases. :
