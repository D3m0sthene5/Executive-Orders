June 19, 1916.
Civil Service Rule VII is hereby amended by adding at the end of section 2
the following :
“And operatives, office of the Auditor for the Post Office Department, for
the audit of accounts and vouchers of the Postal Service by means of labor-
saving devices; and operatives so appointed shall not be eligible for transfer
or promotion to apportioned positions or for assignment to other work.”
The effect of this amendment will be to except from the requirement of
apportionment the operatives described and to permit the holding of a local
examination. The work of operatives is mechanical rather than clerical, and
to a great extent has been done by women employees in the nonapportioned
service transferred from the Bureau of Engraving and Printing under sec-
tion 7 of Rule X: The Secretary of the Treasury states that many persons
appointed from the clerical registers of distant States, in accordance with the
apportionment, have not been adapted to the work, thus occasioning em-
barrassment to the department. Operatives should be young women with
nimble fingers who understand the Kind of work they will be called upon to
perform and will not become dissatisfied with the work and seek transfer or
promotion. The entrance pay is low, the work is fluctuating, and it is there-
fore desirable to appoint women from a local register of eligibles that they
may be available as the work may require, and whose services can be ter-
minated without embarrassment.
This order is issued upon the recommendation of the Secretary of the Treas-
ury, with the approval of the Civil Service Commission.
