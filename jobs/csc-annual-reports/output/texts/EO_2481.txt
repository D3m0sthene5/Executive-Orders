October 31, 1916.
Civil Service Rule VI, section 3, is hereby amended by adding the following sen-
tence:
“This term may be suspended under such conditions as the commission may pre-
scribe in the case of an eligible who as a member of the National Guard or Reserves was
mustered into the military or naval service of the United States under the call by
the President of June 18, 1916, and at the expiration of such service his eligibility
may be restored for a period equivalent to that during which it was suspended.’ =
As amended the section will read:
“3. The term of eligibility shall be one year, beginning with the date on which
the name of the eligible is entered on the register. This term may be extended, in
the discretion of the commission, for all those on any register, when the conditions
of good administration render it inexpedient to hold a new examination. This
"term may be suspended under such conditions as the commission may prescribe in
the case of an eligible who as a member of the National Guard or Reserves was mus-
tered into the military or naval service of the United States under the call by the
President of June 18, 1916, and at the expiration of such service his eligibility may
be restored for a period equivalent to that during which it was suspended. ”’
This order was recommended by the commission and is similar to the Executive
order of May 13, 1898, which provided that in case a person whose name was upon
any register should be mustered into the military or naval service of the United
States at a time when the United States might be engaged in hostilities with an
enemy, the period of eligibility of such person should, under such conditions as the
Civil Service Commission may prescribe, be considered as suspended during the
time such eligible might be serving in the Army or Navy of the United States. This
provision was omitted from the civil-service rules when they were revised in 1903.
