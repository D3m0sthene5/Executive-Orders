August 6, 1913.
Mr. Thomas H. Vanderford may be reinstated to an appropriate position in the
Internal Revenue Service without reference to the period of his separation from the
service.
Mr. Vanderford was a deputy collector of internal revenue from October 11, 1893,
to July 10, 1898, when he was removed for political reasons. A former collector of
internal revenue, under whom he served and by whom he was separated from the
service, makes the following statement regarding him in a letter dated May 9, 1913:
“Mr. Vanderford was an officer in the service when I was appointed collector in
1898 and had been for four years prior to my appointment. He served under me for
practically a year. I regarded him as one of the best officers in the service and after
10 years’ experience as collector I think I can truthfully say he was as good an officer
as I ever knew. I separated him from the service in order to appoint a man of my
own party.”
The Commissioner of Internal Revenue in a letter dated July 7, 1913, states that he
has personally “known Mr. Vanderford for many years, that he is a man of sterling
integrity, honest and fearless, and in his opinion possesses just such traits of character
as would make a competent and efficient revenue officer. The records of the Internal
Bevoune Bureau show that he rendered satisfactory service and was an efficient
employee.
In view of the above facts the commission concurs with the recommendation of the
Sag of the Treasury and the Commissioner of Internal Revenue for the issuance
of this order.
