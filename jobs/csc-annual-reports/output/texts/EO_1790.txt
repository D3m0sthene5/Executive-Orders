June 27, 1913.
Section 6 of civil-service Rule XI, prohibiting the transfer or promotion of messen-
ger boys to positions in the apportioned service, is hereby waived so as to permit of
the promotion of Thomas R. Stewart to the apportioned position of assistant messenger
in the Office of the Secretary of Commerce.
This order is issued upon recommendation of the Secretary of Commerce and is
based upon Mr. Stewart’s very high record for efficiency during his service as messen-
ger boy in the Department of Commerce since his appointment on January 24, 1910.

James D. Calhoun may be appointed deputy collector of customs at the port of
Tampa, in the district of Florida, in the State of Florida, under the reorganization
of the Customs Service which becomes effective July 1, 1913, without compliance
with the civil-service rules.
The Civil Service Commission concurs with the Secretary of the Treasury in recom-
mending the issuance of this order. The collector is required to spend the larger part
of his time at Jacksonville, the designated headquarters, and it is necessary to put
deputies in charge of the other ports in the State. The Secretary of the Treasury
states that he is not satisfied that any of the deputies in Florida are sufficiently com-
petent to be entrusted with the management of the important port of Tampa where
the collections amount to $1,867,763.84 per annum, which is more than three times
that of the next largest port in the State.
A number of temporary appointments have been made in the Florida collection
district without examination because of the lack of eligibles. The number of eligi-
bles in that district for positions of this character has been insufficient, both in number
and in the grades obtained in examination, to meet the needs of the service.
