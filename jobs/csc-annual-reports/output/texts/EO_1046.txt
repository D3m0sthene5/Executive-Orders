March 2, 1909.
Paragraph 4, Section IV,b Schedule A, of the civil-service rules relating to the
Department of Justice, is hereby amended to read as follows:
4. Any person employed as field deputy in the office of a United States marshal or
whose chief duties are to serve process.
Field deputies are paid by fees; office deputies by salary. In some regions where
the field work entails much travel the fees are not sufficient to secure the services of
suitable men. This is also true of bailiffs in large cities, who act as criers and devote
their time outside of court to serving process. These employees are therefore desig-
nated and salaried as office deputies; but they are left by the amendment in the ex-
cepted class with field deputies, where they properly belong by reason of their duties.
The paragraph formerly excepted ‘‘any person employed as office or field deputy in
the office of a United States marshal.”” The amendment therefore makes office depu-
ties competitive, where both the designation and duties of the position are of that
character.
