May 28, 1923.
Mr. George L. Williams may be appointed to a position of laborer in the Post Office
Service without examination under the civil service rules.
This order is issued on the recommendation of the Postmaster General, who states
that while Mr. Williams was temporarily employed as mail messenger at Jefferson City,
Mo., he was held up by bandits on March 1, 1921, who carried him into the country and
bound him to a tree and escaped with the registered pouches. He helped to identify
the thieves when they were arrested and was many times threatened by their friends.
He has been employed as a temporary laborer since June 1, 1921.
Examinations have been held by the Civil Service Commission, but no eligibles have
been obtained. Mr. Williams is 58 years of age and did not take the examination, as
persons more than 45 years of age are not appointed in the Postal Service.
The Civil Service Commission concurred in the recommendation.
