December 30, 1922.
# Mrs. Anna M. Curran may be reappointed as printer’s assistant in the Bureau of
Engraving and Printing of the Treasury Department. She served in this position
from September 15, 1919, when she was appointed without examination under author-
ity of an Executive order of January 15, 1918, until August 13, 1921, when she was
furloughed because of reduction of force, her furlough being largely due to the fact
that her husband was employed in the Government service. Since her furlough
she has been left a widow by the assassination of her husband. She received several
bullet wounds in trying to protect him. She has been out of the service too long to
be eligible for reemployment, and the Executive order under which she received
Shpoigimeit has been revoked. She could have remained in the service indefinitely
if no occasion had arisen to require her separation. In view of all the circumstances
the Secretary of the Treasury recommends her reinstatement.
The Civil Service Commission concurs in the recommendation that she be reap-
pointed with the same status she would have had if she had remained in the service
under her original appointment.
