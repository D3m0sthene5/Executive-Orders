September 14, 1915.
James O. Brockenshire, chief musician, United States Army, retired, may be
appointed as inspector of musical instruments at $780 per annum, Quartermaster
Corps of the Army, without examination under the civil-service rules.
The Acting Secretary of War states that the waiving of the civil-service rules to
authorize the appointment of Mr. Brockenshire is requested by the Acting Quarter-
master General of the Army, in view of his long service in the Army as chief musician
and his varied experience with Army bands, which especially qualify him for the
position. The Acting Quartermaster General reports that he is a thorough musician,
able to play almost any instrument in Army bands, and is just the type of inspector
needed by the depot quartermaster at Philadelphia, under whom he has been em-
ployed at different times during the past two years, rendering entirely satisfactory
service.
.Mr. Brockenshire was in the military service as musician for nearly thirty years,
voluntarily retiring on June 1, 1914, with a “most excellent character.” During his
service, with the exception of about one year, he was with the Seventh Cavalry.
The acting adjutant of the Seventh Cavalry states that he had been held in the highest
esteem by every officer and enlisted man with whom he had come in contact officially
and personally and was universally admired and respected both as a soldier and as a
man because of his constant devotion to duty and courteous treatment of his associates.
Maj. Gen. J. F. Bell, who knew him about 30 years, states that he is one of the best
men he had ever known, being entirely sober, reliable, upright, and honorable,
always courteous and most obliging, because he is a gentleman at heart; and, further,
that he is a disciplinarian with a special faculty for getting along with and managing
bands. Almost if not equally strong testimonials were given by ins. Gen. Garling-
ton, Brig. Gen. Charles Morton, Brig. Gen. T. N. Baldwin, retired, and numerous
officers of the Seventh Cavalry.
Because of Mr. Brockenshire’s unusual service, splendid character, and musical
ability, so strongly stated by Army officers who have known him intimately, and his
demonstrated qualifications for the position of inspector of musical instruments, the
Civil Service Commission recommends this order.
