February 14, 1925.
Mrs. May B. Bowman and Mrs. Elizabeth H. Sullivan may be permanently
reinstated as operatives in the Bureau of Engraving and Printing, without regard
to the length of time they have been separated from the service.
Mrs. Bowman has had 11 years' service as an employee of the bureau and
is now serving under temporary appointment, her status for permanent reinstate-
ment having lapsed. She is the wife of David J. Bowman, a former plate printer,
who had approximately 10 years' service in the bureau and was compelled to
resign on account of an injury received as a result of an accident which has made
him almost totally incapacitated for work. Mr. and Mrs. Bowman have three
small children dependent upon them for support.
Mrs. Sullivan served in the bureau from June 9, 1908, until July 21, 1917,
when she resigned to be married. She is the widow of Charles A. Sullivan, a
former plate printer in the bureau, who died October 1, 1924, as a result of ink
poisoning incurred in the performance of his work. Mrs. Sullivan has two small
children and a widowed mother dependent upon her for support.
This order is issued on the recommendation of the Secretary of the Treasury.
The Civil Service Commission submits these cases without prejudice, stating
that it is without jurisdiction.
Done for charity.
