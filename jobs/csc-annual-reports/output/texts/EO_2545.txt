March 17, 1917.
Twenty-eight deputy collectors of internal revenue named in letter of the Secretary
of the Treasury dated March 8, 1917, appointed without reference to the ¢ivil-service
rules under authority of the act of October 22, 1913, may be transferred to competi-
tive classified positions of field deputy collector, income-tax roll, to serve until not
later than August 31, 1917. Owing to the demand for war munitions, there has becn
a heavy increase in the production of tax-free alcohol. This has made necessary the

employment of an unusual number of storekeepers and gaugers, with the result that
the appropriation from which storekeepers-gaugers and agents are paid has been de-
pleted to a point where a deficiency of considerable magnitude is threatened, not-
withstanding the utmost reduction of the force of the storekeeper-gaugers. This
deficiency can be averted only by the transfer of the force known as ‘‘special gaugers”
{Sionekaeper gauge assigned to special duty under division revenue agents) to the
eputy collector roll. By transferring them to positions as general deputy collectors
on the regular roll, the storekeeper-gaugers-agents appropriation can be relieved.
It is desired to transfer deputy collectors Appointed without examination but assigned
to income tax work to the income tax-roll. :
This will enable the Treasury Department to utilize the several separate appropria-
tions from which the field force of the Internal Revenue Service is paid, and to thus
continue the service of competitive classified special gaugers whose services would
otherwise have to be dispensed with, though needed.
Employees so transferred do not thus acquire any rights of competitive employees
under the civil service rules, but will be retransferred to excepted positions unless
separated from the service.
he Civil Service Commission concurs in this order.
