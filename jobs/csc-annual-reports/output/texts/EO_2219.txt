June 26, 1915.
Mr. Edward E. Carter may be reinstated in the Forest Service as forest inspector at
$2,800 per annum without regard to the year limit of the reinstatement rule.
Mr. Carter entered the Forest Service through the field assistant in forestry exami-
nation on March 11,1905, and resigned on September 30,1910. Hissalary wasincreased
from $1,000 to $2,500 per annum by successive promotions from forest assistant to
forest inspector, chief of office of management, and assistant forester. His duties
during the last two and one-half years of that period were identical with those which
will now be required, namely, the administration and field inspection of timber sales,
timber reconnaissance, and reforestation. His exceptional capacity had placed himg
in rank next to the assistant forester in charge of the branch of silviculture, and in the
absence of that officer he handled the entire executive work of the branch. From 1910
to the present time Mr. Carter has been employed continuously as assistant professor
in forestry at Harvard University, specializing on forest management, silviculture
and the administration of the forest property of the university. This experience and
training are regarded by the department as an added qualification for the special
duties of the existing vacancy. The department regards Mr. Carter as one of the
strongest men in technical forestry ever employed by the Forest Service.
In view of these facts the commission concurs with the department in recommend-
ing this order.
