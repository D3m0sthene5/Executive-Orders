April 29, 1916. :
Miss Emma B. Shreve and Miss Clara M. Tremper, who have been employed
temporarily in the office of the collector of customs at New York in connection
with the compilation of statistics through the installation of accounting ma-
chines, may be permanently appointed to clerical positions in the customs serv-
ice at New York without examination under the civil-service rules. The Acting
Secretary of the Treasury states that the success of this new system, which
will result in a great saving of time and money, is largely dependent upon the
permanent appointment of these two women. The accuracy of the Government
statistical reports depends upon the accuracy of operations performed under the
direction of Miss Shreve as supervisor over about 12 young women opera-
tors. The collector of customs at New Yorw states that Miss Shreve has
proven herself to be especially capable and well fitted for this supervisory work.
The department states that Miss Tremper is an expert operator upon the
Underwood standard electrical adding and typewriting machine. The tabulated
reports as they come from the electrical machines are turned over to Miss
Tremper, who translates the code numbers into the appropriate articles, coun-
tries, etc., and transcribes this information in the form of a typewritten report.
This report is prepared upon wax sheets, and any errors therein would require
it to be rewritten. Miss Tremper is one of the speediest operators in the United
States, and the work now performed by her represents the work formerly per-
formed by the press division of 13 men.
This order is issued on the recommendation of the Secretary of the Treasury,
with the concurrence of the Civil Service Commission
