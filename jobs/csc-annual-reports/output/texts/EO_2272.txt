November 16, 1915.
Mr. Charles Hardin may be reinstated as a clerk at not to exceed $1,200 per
annum in the Post Office Department without regard to the length of his sepa-
ration from the service. Mr. Hardin was appointed August 15, 1887, through
appropriate examination as a clerk at $900 in the Post Office Department at
Washington, and was removed from the position of clerk, class 8 ($1,600), on
January 26, 1901, for irregularities in connection with the dead-letter sale.
The records of the department show that Mr. Hardin's removal was based on
the report and recommendation of a post-office inspector who investigated the
charges. The Postmaster General reports that this report, which it is under-
stood was favorable to Mr. Hardin, was apparently extracted from the files by
the previous administration. He further states that as it is believed that the
removal of Mr. Hardin, and the manner by which he was removed, was unjust
to him, the department feels that it would be in the interest of justice to rein-
state him. The Civil Service Commission does not recommend the issuance of
an order in this case.
