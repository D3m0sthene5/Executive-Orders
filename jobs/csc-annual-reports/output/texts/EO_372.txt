November 18, 1905.
The President issued the following order:
Schedule A of the civil service rules is hereby amended by adding to section 2,
Treasury Department, a new clause to be numbered 12, to read as follows:
12. One examiner of tobacco, and one examiner of tea in the Customs Service at
the port of Chicago.
The Treasury Department in April, 1904, appointed without examination an exam¬
iner of tea at the port of Chicago, and May 17, 1904, an examination along lines
of former examinations for this class of positions was held and a register of eligi-
bles secured. The temporary appointee failed, and the Department having been
informed that a rerating would not affect the position of any competitor objected to
each and all of those certified “because of mental unfitness for the position for which
they apply.” The Department insisted that it was only anxious to secure the services
of a competent tea examiner. The first examination was accordingly canceled, and
another examination was held May 10, 1905, along lines agreed upon with the Depart¬
ment in cases where practical tests are included. The temporary appointee passed
this second examination, and was certified along with two others who had also passed
the first examination. The Department, however, selected the temporary appointee,
who was then 62 years old, and who, in the examinations, failed to show exceptional
qualifications.
The President, in view of all the facts in the case, at the instance of the Commis¬
sion, placed the position of examiner of tea at Chicago in the excepted class, and the
appointee, therefore, does not now have a competitive status.
At the request of the Treasury Department an examination prepared along the
lines of previous examinations which had been satisfactory was held September 2,
1903, for examiner of tobacco at the port of Chicago. The temporary appointee
failed, and the Department requested his reexamination on the ground that special
assignments away from Chicago had prevented him from making preparation for the
examination. Objection was also made to the relative weights and order of the sub¬
jects. The first examination was therefore canceled and a second examination held
February 17, 1904, in which the practical portion of the examination was increased
from 50 to 70 per cent. The temporary appointee again failed, but on rerating
became eligible, too low, however, to be reached for certification. After several
months’ delay, the Department finally declined to make selection, and asked for
another examination, stating that the temporary appointee had large practical expe¬
rience and that the Department was anxious to secure the services of one thoroughly
qualified for the examination of tobacco. A third examination was accordingly
held, after consultation with the Department, May 10, 1905, practical questions,
tests, and experience constituting 95 per cent. The temporary appointee was third
on the resulting register, and was selected, over two years after his original tempo¬
rary appointment, at the age of 63. In the opinion of the Commission, the exami¬
nation failed to show that he possessed unusual or peculiar qualifications, and in
selecting him the Department passed over men acknowledged to be well qualified.
In view of all the circumstances in the case, the President, at the instance of the
Commission, placed the position of examiner of tobacco at Chicago in the excepted
class, and the temporary appointee, therefore, does not now possess a competitive
status.
