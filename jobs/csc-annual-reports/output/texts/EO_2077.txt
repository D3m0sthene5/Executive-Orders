November 10, 1914.
Mr. W. T. Bateman may be appointed a watchman in a navy yard without compli-
ance with the civil-service rules.
This order is issued on the recommendation of the Secretary of the Navy, who says
the case is a deserving one and that the general character of Mr. Bateman 1s such that
he feels justified in recommending an exemption from the civil-service rules which
will permit his employment in a navy yard so that he may be able to support his family.
It appears that Mr. Bateman, seaman, formerly attached to the U. S. S. Foote, is at
present a patient in the Naval Hospital, Washington, D. C. While visiting his family
a l-pounder shell which he was showing his child exploded and blew off his left
hand. The Surgeon General of the Navy concurs in the recommendation of the Sec-
retary of the Navy and says Mr. Bateman is a very deserving man. While Mr. Bate-
man will be discharged from the Navy for disability he can receive no pension, as his
disability was not incurred in the line of duty.
The Civil Service Commission does not recommend the issuance of the order.
